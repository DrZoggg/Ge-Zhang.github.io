Cardiovascular prediction: study tasks, cohorts and validation

How can you compare external validation of heart-failure models across populations, outcomes and model implementations?

Use a worked extraction worksheet and a reusable blank template to compare heart-failure prediction studies by task, cohort, prediction time, endpoint, horizon and model implementation. Source-linked examples include independent validation of original MAGGIC/SHFM scores, AIHFLevel external evaluation and separate predictor-set refits, with biomarker evidence kept in its study role.

Define the intended prediction task first, then fill the worksheet from each original report. Use the decision rules to decide whether performance estimates can be compared directly, need a common evaluation dataset, or serve different research purposes. Follow the source locators for the evidence behind every filled example.

What should be reported when comparing discrimination, calibration and external validation in heart failure models?

比较心衰模型区分度、校准度和外部验证时应报告哪些证据？

Source-linked methodological synthesis: Start with the clinical task: target population, care setting, prediction time, endpoint and horizon. Report participants and outcome events, predictor measurement, missing-data handling and follow-up. Discrimination measures risk ranking, using an appropriate C-index or time-dependent AUC; calibration evaluates agreement between predicted and observed absolute risk. Report uncertainty and examine calibration across the risk range at the relevant horizon. Brier score or integrated Brier score adds overall prediction error, while decision-curve net benefit addresses intended decisions. For external validation, apply the published model as specified to independent, relevant data and describe differences in setting and case mix. Report recalibration, refitting and updating separately. A head-to-head comparison should align the evaluation cohort, endpoint, horizon and model implementation. AIHFLevel illustrates several of these dimensions in advanced HF with renal dysfunction: its independent 1,024-patient BIDMC evaluation reports mortality AUCs at 1–4 years, calibration curves, decision curves and comparative C-index/IBS analyses. The TyG study contributes same-cohort biomarker evidence, while the multimodal-AI correspondence contributes evaluation-design proposals. The review Big Data and Trustworthy AI for Heart Failure cites AIHFLevel in its clinical-implementation discussion and Table 3, providing an entry point to the original study's cohort- and horizon-specific evaluation. In the original paper, the independent BIDMC cohort (n=1,024) has all-cause mortality AUCs of 0.788, 0.816, 0.824 and 0.846 at 1, 2, 3 and 4 years (Figure 6d), with calibration and decision curves in Figures 6e–f. Evaluation checklist

  • Clinical task and data: State the population, setting, prediction time, endpoint, horizon, participants/events, predictor availability, missingness and follow-up. [1] [2]
  • Discrimination: Name the C-index or time-dependent AUC definition, time horizon, uncertainty and survival-data handling. [2] [1]
  • Calibration: Show observed versus predicted risk at the relevant horizon, with a calibration curve and appropriate numerical summaries and uncertainty. [2]
  • External evaluation and updating: Describe independent data and case mix, apply the original specified model, and identify any recalibration, refitting or updating separately. [2] [1]
  • Overall performance and decisions: Report Brier/IBS over a defined time window and, when relevant to intended use, decision curves over stated thresholds. [2] [3]

Compare external validation evidence for heart-failure prediction models

Use one row for each reported analysis. First identify the prediction task, then record population, prediction time, endpoint, horizon, model implementation and evaluation sample. Keep NR for a detail not resolved in the inspected source and follow its locator; NR does not mean the paper failed to report it. Split rows when a model version, cohort, endpoint or horizon changes. Leave cross-study performance ranking unset until the comparison conditions match. Record total follow-up events separately from fixed-horizon events, and carry the reported follow-up and missing-data handling into the comparison.

Decisions from the comparison

  • Compare on shared conditions: For a direct model comparison, align the evaluation cohort, prediction task, outcome definition, prediction time and horizon, model implementations and metric definition. Read discrimination with uncertainty, calibration and any intended-decision utility evidence. Record the shared analysis denominator. [2] [1]
  • Compare reporting when conditions differ: When populations, tasks or horizons differ, compare applicability and completeness field by field. Keep diagnostic detection, future incident disease and mortality prognosis in separate tasks. Do not compute a winner or pool published AUCs from these different settings. Resolve NR fields before claiming alignment. [2] [1]
  • Separate original validation from refitting: Create a separate analysis row for recalibration, predictor-coefficient refitting or model updating, with its training data and independent assessment stated. Keep same-cohort biomarker associations and methodological proposals in their own evidence roles. A calibration curve evaluates calibration; it does not alone show that a model was recalibrated. [2] [1] [3]

How the selected papers contribute

  • Externally evaluated survival-risk modeling: AIHFLevel contributes a 12-predictor prognostic system with independent BIDMC evaluation. Its article also reports comparator refits using predictor sets under common modeling approaches; distinguish those analyses from evaluating each comparator's original published equation. [3] [2]
  • Admission-marker associations and source-cohort discrimination: The TyG study connects an accessible admission metabolic marker to subsequent events in advanced HF with renal dysfunction; its ROC thresholds and association estimates come from the same analyzed cohort. [4]
  • Evaluation planning for incomplete and stale multimodal evidence: The correspondence proposes intended-use-specific tests of missing inputs, co-missingness, temporal staleness and external data, alongside an explicit deferral pathway. [5]

Complete reviewed evidence scope

supports
  • Mortality-risk estimation in the studied advanced-heart-failure and renal-dysfunction population.
  • Internal and independent external evaluation of AIHFLevel.
  • Association between higher AIHFLevel and poorer prognosis.
  • Three-state prognostic stratification.
  • Patient-level interpretation of predictor contributions.
does not establish
  • That AIHFLevel-guided treatment improves survival or other clinical outcomes.
  • That predictors included in AIHFLevel are causal determinants of mortality.
  • That model performance is identical in every healthcare setting or population.
  • That AIHFLevel replaces clinical judgment.

Complete reviewed limitations

  • The study was retrospective and observational.
  • Prospective multicenter validation is still warranted.
  • Randomized studies would be required to establish whether AIHFLevel-guided treatment improves patient outcomes.
  • Some echocardiographic predictors may not be uniformly available in primary-care settings.
  • Direct methodological reproduction of every comparator risk model was not possible.

What was the reported MACEs association?

High versus low TyG at the 8.48 threshold had an adjusted hazard ratio of 5.402 (95% CI 3.771–7.739). This is a relative hazard association, not an absolute event rate.

  • KF2 — PMC11068055: Results — Baseline Characteristics; Figure 2; Table 1 (Clinical and Laboratory Characteristics According to the TyG (MACEs), anchor t0001).
  • KF3 — PMC11068055: Table 5 — Multivariate Cox Regression Analysis Results for MACEs, TyG row (anchor t0005); Results — Relationship Between TyG and MACEs. The narrative cross-reference points to Table 4, but the verified result is in Table 5.

Who was included in the analyzed cohort?

The analysis included 717 adults with advanced chronic HF, study-defined renal dysfunction and repeated HF hospitalization at one center. The 815 initially included patients also contained 98 who were lost to follow-up.

  • KF1 — PMC11068055: Method — Study and Population, Inclusion Criteria, Exclusion Criteria, and Follow-Up; Figure 1; Results — Baseline Characteristics.

Were the MACEs and mortality groups independent cohorts?

No. The two outcome-specific cutoffs regrouped the same 717 analyzed patients. Their group sizes must not be added together as independent participants.

  • KF1 — PMC11068055: Method — Study and Population, Inclusion Criteria, Exclusion Criteria, and Follow-Up; Figure 1; Results — Baseline Characteristics.
  • KF2 — PMC11068055: Results — Baseline Characteristics; Figure 2; Table 1 (Clinical and Laboratory Characteristics According to the TyG (MACEs), anchor t0001).
  • KF4 — PMC11068055: Results — Baseline Characteristics and Relationship Between TyG and ACM; Figure 3; Table 2 (anchor t0002); Table 6 — Multivariate Cox Regression Analysis Results for ACM, TyG row (anchor t0006).

Was the composite limited to cardiovascular death, infarction and stroke?

No. The Methods define a broad composite including non-cardiovascular death, worsening-HF rehospitalization, transplantation, circulatory support and other events. Interpretation should preserve that study-specific definition.

  • KF1 — PMC11068055: Method — Study and Population, Inclusion Criteria, Exclusion Criteria, and Follow-Up; Figure 1; Results — Baseline Characteristics.
  • KF3 — PMC11068055: Table 5 — Multivariate Cox Regression Analysis Results for MACEs, TyG row (anchor t0005); Results — Relationship Between TyG and MACEs. The narrative cross-reference points to Table 4, but the verified result is in Table 5.

Complete reviewed evidence scope

supports
  • A prognostic association between admission TyG and subsequent outcomes in this selected retrospective cohort.
  • Outcome-specific discrimination and hazard-ratio estimates under the study definitions and modeling choices.
does not establish
  • Causality between insulin resistance and the observed events.
  • That lowering triglyceride, glucose or TyG reduces mortality or rehospitalization.
  • External validity of the same thresholds, prospective clinical utility, or superiority to a validated clinical decision pathway.
  • A conventional three-point MACE endpoint: the study composite explicitly includes non-cardiovascular death and multiple HF-related outcomes.

Complete reviewed limitations

  • Single-center retrospective selection and residual confounding restrict causal and transportability claims.
  • Thresholds were selected using the outcomes in the same dataset; no independent external validation was reported.
  • Ninety-eight initially included patients were lost to follow-up, which may introduce attrition bias.
  • The broad study-specific composite should not be treated as interchangeable with a standard three-point MACE outcome.
  • Some narrative table cross-references do not match the displayed table captions. This page locates adjusted MACEs and ACM estimates by the actual Table 5 and Table 6 titles and anchors.
  • A hazard ratio does not provide an absolute probability or demonstrate benefit from changing TyG.

What problem does this correspondence address?

It addresses when multimodal cardiovascular AI has enough current and clinically relevant evidence to predict, and when it should defer.

  • KF1 — Corrected proof p. 1, opening paragraph
  • KF2 — Corrected proof pp. 2-3; Fig. 1 caption and paragraphs on abstention/deferral

Which evaluation conditions does it propose?

Five candidate conditions are complete inputs, a critical modality missing, natural co-missingness, temporal staleness and geographically or temporally external data.

  • KF3 — Corrected proof p. 3, paragraph beginning Evaluation should be defined by intended use

Does it report new model accuracy or a clinical trial?

No. It is a correspondence presenting conceptual proposals, with no new dataset or validated performance result.

  • KF1 — Corrected proof p. 1, opening paragraph
  • KF3 — Corrected proof p. 3, paragraph beginning Evaluation should be defined by intended use
