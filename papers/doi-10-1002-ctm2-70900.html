Author evidence note

What can one patient teach the next?

The letter develops a practical framework for learning from individualised treatment outcomes: use earlier cases to adapt safety monitoring, test mechanistic hypotheses and assess treatment benefit as three distinct research questions. Timely outcome reporting makes these comparisons possible, even before a complete mechanistic explanation is available.

Source: Publisher PDF, p. 1, opening paragraph and Figure 1; p. 2, final main-text paragraph.

Compare cases for the question being asked

Figure 1 proposes AI-assisted comparison of clinical trajectories, treatment exposure, biological context and prior evidence. For a safety signal, drug, dose, route and event timing can make an earlier case informative even across different diagnoses. For apparent benefit, the relevant comparison starts with the pretreatment disease course and reliable outcomes meaningful to the patient.

Source: Publisher PDF, Figure 1; pp. 1-2, paragraph beginning That distinction matters; p. 2, paragraph beginning AI could help.

Turn biological differences into testable hypotheses

The letter distinguishes a drug failing to affect its intended target from reaching the target without restoring function. These competing explanations motivate different measurements and experiments. Molecular subtypes and computational models can help frame such tests; prognostic risk prediction and estimation of treatment effects require different evidence.

Source: Publisher PDF, p. 2, paragraphs on benefit, biological differences and AI-assisted comparison.

Use in research design and scientific writing

The framework can support an Introduction or Discussion on learning across individualised therapies, a protocol rationale for safety measurements, or a case report explaining which observations should change monitoring or prompt further investigation. It links the choice of comparison to the intended action, while keeping assessment of treatment benefit tied to appropriate comparative evidence.

Source: Publisher PDF, Figure 1 and p. 2, AI-assisted comparison and case-sharing paragraphs.

Evidence scope and limitations

Source-located summary of an Invited Letter proposing how individualised treatment outcomes can inform learning across patients.

  • This methodological Invited Letter proposes a framework and discusses published examples; it reports no new patient cohort or validated AI system. Detailed patient-level sharing remains subject to consent and privacy safeguards.
