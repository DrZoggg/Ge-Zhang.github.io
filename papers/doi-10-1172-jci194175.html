Evidence Scale

Vascular smooth muscle cell-derived KIF13B inhibits proinflammatory responses to protect against atherosclerosis

A mechanistic translational preclinical study combining associative human carotid-plaque evidence with mouse atherosclerosis genetics, pooled single-cell analysis, primary human aortic smooth-muscle-cell perturbation, biochemical rescue and preclinical pharmacology to investigate how KIF13B regulates VSMC state through KCTD10-dependent KLF4 turnover.

Human participants, paired plaque regions, public dataset samples, mice, pooled single-cell libraries and independent cell-culture replicates are different denominators and must not be summed. External human datasets provide cross-source corroboration, not prospective clinical validation. Five mice pooled into one library per genotype are not five independent single-cell libraries. Research Question

Does VSMC-derived KIF13B regulate vascular smooth-muscle-cell phenotypic state and experimental plaque stability through KCTD10-dependent KLF4 turnover, and can reducing KLF4 activity rescue the consequences of KIF13B loss in experimental systems? Human plaque findings are associative; causal and intervention evidence is preclinical.

Author Evidence Summary

This study integrates paired human carotid endarterectomy plaque regions, independent public human transcriptomic, proteomic, methylation and spatial-transcriptomic evidence, VSMC-specific and global mouse Kif13b perturbation, aortic single-cell RNA sequencing, primary human aortic smooth-muscle-cell gain- and loss-of-function experiments, biochemical rescue and preclinical pharmacology. Human observations associate lower KIF13B, particularly in VSMCs, with advanced or unstable plaque features and lower collagen, but do not establish human causality. In male AAV8-PCSK9 mice, VSMC-specific Kif13b deletion increased lesion burden and adverse plaque-composition features without detected differences in plasma total cholesterol or triglycerides in the tested model. Pooled mouse single-cell analysis and primary HASMC perturbation linked KIF13B loss to reduced matrix/collagen programs and increased inflammatory VSMC programs. KLF4 knockdown rescued KIF13B-loss phenotypes in HASMCs. CHX/MG132, ubiquitination, co-immunoprecipitation and KCTD10-overexpression experiments support a model in which KIF13B maintains KCTD10 abundance and KCTD10 promotes KLF4 ubiquitination and proteasomal turnover; how KIF13B controls KCTD10 remains unresolved. Kenpaullone reduced KLF4 promoter activity/expression and rescued cellular and mouse phenotypes in this study, but this is preclinical proof-of-concept. Kenpaullone is not established as a KLF4-selective drug, and no human treatment efficacy was tested.

These human tissue and public-dataset observations are associative and do not establish that changing KIF13B alters plaque outcomes in patients.

Study Design & Analytical Framework

Mechanistic translational preclinical study integrating associative human plaque observations and public multi-omic datasets with causal mouse genetics, pooled single-cell RNA sequencing, primary HASMC perturbation, biochemical rescue and preclinical pharmacologic intervention.

Paired carotid endarterectomy plaque regions from 6 patients; independent public human plaque datasets; male mouse atherosclerosis models; primary human aortic smooth muscle cells. No treated human atherosclerosis cohort.

What This Study Adds

  • Defines a VSMC-specific KIF13B role in atherosclerosis that is biologically distinct from the previously reported macrophage KIF13B-MERTK efferocytosis pathway.
  • Connects associative human plaque evidence with cell-specific mouse genetic causality without treating the human observations as causal.
  • Links KIF13B to VSMC matrix-versus-inflammatory phenotypic programs through functional KLF4 rescue.
  • Provides biochemical and rescue evidence for a KCTD10-KLF4 ubiquitination/proteasomal-turnover mechanism while leaving the upstream KIF13B-to-KCTD10 regulatory step unresolved.
  • Provides preclinical pharmacologic proof-of-concept with Kenpaullone while separating experimental rescue from drug selectivity, human efficacy and clinical safety.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Association between lower KIF13B and more advanced or unstable human atherosclerotic plaque features across local tissue and independent public datasets.
  • A causal contribution of VSMC Kif13b loss to measured atherosclerotic plaque phenotypes in the tested male mouse models.
  • Cell-autonomous effects of KIF13B gain and loss on matrix, inflammatory and contractile programs in primary HASMCs.
  • Functional placement of KLF4 downstream of KIF13B in HASMC perturbation and rescue experiments.
  • Evidence consistent with KCTD10-mediated KLF4 ubiquitination and proteasomal turnover downstream of KIF13B.
  • Preclinical cellular and mouse rescue after Kenpaullone exposure under the tested conditions.

Does Not Establish

  • That low KIF13B expression or KIF13B methylation causes plaque instability in humans.
  • That KIF13B, KCTD10, KLF4 or Kenpaullone has proven therapeutic efficacy or safety in patients with atherosclerosis.
  • That Kenpaullone is selective for KLF4 or that all Kenpaullone effects in this study are mediated specifically through KLF4.
  • A universal lipid-independent KIF13B mechanism in humans; the reported absence of TC/TG differences applies to the tested mouse comparison.
  • Independent replication of the study-generated single-cell comparison, because five mice were pooled into one library per genotype.
  • One definitive single-cell diet duration or one unambiguous cluster assignment for every Figure 5-7 subpanel where the article text and captions disagree.
  • The molecular mechanism by which KIF13B regulates KCTD10 abundance.
  • Generalization of the causal or pharmacologic mouse findings to females, because only male mice were used.

Limitations

  • The local human cohort contained six carotid endarterectomy patients with paired within-plaque regions; human observations are associative and small-sample.
  • The local advanced-versus-adjacent classification was based on anatomical location and macroscopic surgical appearance rather than molecular or histopathological criteria.
  • Stable/unstable public datasets and local advanced/adjacent paired regions use different plaque definitions and should not be treated as interchangeable cohorts.
  • Human methylation observations are associative and do not establish that KIF13B hypermethylation causes KIF13B downregulation or plaque instability.
  • The PXD062283 repository contains 87 plaques overall, whereas the JCI stable/unstable comparison uses a subset of 19 and 38; repository size and analytical subset must not be conflated.
  • Five mice were pooled into one single-cell library per genotype, limiting independent biological replication at the single-cell library level.
  • Figure 5/Results report a 20-week Western-diet single-cell experiment, whereas downstream Figure 6/7 captions reference 12 weeks; this discrepancy is unresolved.
  • Results describe inflammatory genes in VSMC-Fib4 while the Figure 5K caption labels the panel VSMC-Fib1; the evidence page therefore avoids treating that subpanel label as unambiguous.
  • Only male mice were used, and sex was not analyzed as a biological variable.
  • Tagln-Cre targets smooth-muscle compartments beyond plaque VSMCs; reported intestine/bladder measurements do not establish general systemic safety of pathway targeting.
  • The study establishes KCTD10-dependent KLF4 ubiquitination/rescue evidence but does not identify the mechanism by which KIF13B regulates KCTD10 abundance.
  • Kenpaullone is pharmacologically nonselective; its rescue does not prove KLF4-selective target engagement or exclude contributions from other kinase targets.
  • Kenpaullone was evaluated only in cells and mice. No human atherosclerosis efficacy, dosing, pharmacokinetic or safety trial was performed.

Q&A

What is the human evidence linking KIF13B to atherosclerotic plaque severity?

Paired carotid endarterectomy tissue and independent human transcriptomic and proteomic datasets showed lower KIF13B in more advanced or unstable plaque states, with prominent VSMC differences and lower collagen in advanced local plaque regions. These are associative human observations and do not establish that manipulating KIF13B changes plaque outcomes in patients.

Evidence: KF1

Did VSMC-specific Kif13b deletion cause worse atherosclerosis in mice?

In the tested male AAV8-PCSK9 model, VSMC-specific Kif13b deletion increased lesion burden, necrotic core, macrophage accumulation and apoptotic-cell signal and reduced collagen/plaque-stability features. No genotype difference in measured plasma total cholesterol or triglycerides was detected in the primary comparison.

Evidence: KF2

What did the single-cell experiment show, and were there five independent replicates per genotype?

The pooled aortic single-cell comparison associated KIF13B loss with reduced matrix/collagen programs and increased inflammatory VSMC-derived programs. Five mice were pooled into one single-cell library per genotype, so the design does not provide five independent scRNA-seq libraries per group.

Evidence: KF3

Why does this page not assign one definitive diet duration to all single-cell analyses?

The Results and Figure 5 describe the generated aortic single-cell experiment after 20 weeks of Western diet, whereas Figure 6 SCENIC and Figure 7 integrative captions reference 12 weeks. The evidence page preserves this source discrepancy instead of silently harmonizing it.

Evidence: KF3

How does KIF13B affect primary human vascular smooth muscle cells?

Under oxLDL, KIF13B knockdown reduced contractile/matrix features, collagen secretion and collagen-gel contraction and increased inflammatory features, while KIF13B overexpression produced reciprocal effects. These are in vitro primary-HASMC perturbation data, not human treatment outcomes.

Evidence: KF4

Is KLF4 functionally downstream of KIF13B?

In primary HASMC experiments, KIF13B loss increased KLF4, and concomitant KLF4 knockdown rescued the collagen-suppressive and inflammatory phenotype caused by KIF13B knockdown. This supports a downstream functional relationship in the tested cell system.

Evidence: KF5

What evidence supports the KIF13B-KCTD10-KLF4 mechanism?

KIF13B knockdown stabilized KLF4 in CHX/proteasome experiments and reduced KLF4 ubiquitination. Integrated analyses nominated KCTD10, co-IP supported KCTD10-KLF4 association, KCTD10 increased KLF4 ubiquitination, and KCTD10 overexpression rescued KIF13B-loss phenotypes. The study does not resolve how KIF13B itself controls KCTD10 abundance.

Evidence: KF6

Does Kenpaullone prove that KLF4 is a clinically validated treatment target for atherosclerosis?

No. Kenpaullone reduced KLF4 promoter activity/expression and rescued cellular and mouse phenotypes in this study, but the experiments were preclinical. Kenpaullone is also a pharmacologically nonselective kinase inhibitor rather than an established KLF4-selective clinical drug, and no human atherosclerosis treatment efficacy or safety was tested.

Evidence: KF7

How does this JCI study differ from the European Heart Journal KIF13B study?

The JCI study focuses on vascular smooth muscle cells and a KIF13B-KCTD10-KLF4 mechanism controlling matrix/inflammatory VSMC states. The European Heart Journal study focuses on macrophage KIF13B, MERTK-dependent efferocytosis and an ITCH/CBL regulatory network. Together they describe distinct cell-type-specific KIF13B mechanisms in experimental atherosclerosis.

Evidence: KF1, KF6

Which public datasets and repositories support this evidence page?

Key resources include study-generated mouse single-cell data GSE314838, human stable/unstable carotid plaque RNA-seq GSE120521, human plaque proteomics PXD062283, public spatial-transcriptomic evidence associated with GSE274572, the article's public methylation datasets, the JCI Supplemental Data, and the Supporting Data Values file.
