## Official Abstract

### Purpose

Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a prevalent form of heart failure with limited therapeutic options. This study explores a novel treatment strategy involving the delivery of exosome-derived miRNA-185-5p inhibitors encapsulated in liposomes, aiming to target cardiac tissue and alleviate myocardial apoptosis and cuproptosis in DCM.

### Methods

The miRNA-185-5p inhibitor, identified in our previous study and extracted from exosomes, was encapsulated in liposomes functionalized with a cardiac-targeting peptide. This system was used in both in vitro and in vivo models of DCM induced by doxorubicin (DOX). We evaluated the effects of this treatment on cardiac function, apoptosis, cuproptosis, oxidative stress, and fibrosis using echocardiography, histological analysis, Western blotting, and biochemical assays.

### Results

In vitro experiments demonstrated that the Lipo@miR-185-5p inhibitor markedly attenuated apoptosis and cuproptosis in H9C2 cells and iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, with a 42.6% reduction in apoptotic cell rates and over 50% downregulation of cuproptosis-related markers (both P < 0.01). In vivo, the targeted liposomal formulation significantly improved cardiac function in DOX-induced DCM mice, as evidenced by a 27.3% increase in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and a 36.5% reduction in myocardial fibrosis area (P < 0.01), along with enhanced survival. These findings underscore the therapeutic potential of this targeted delivery strategy for the treatment of dilated cardiomyopathy.

### Conclusion

Lipo@miR-185-5p inhibitor, utilizing exosome-derived miRNA and targeted liposomal delivery, effectively alleviates DCM-induced myocardial dysfunction. This approach represents a promising therapeutic strategy for cardiovascular diseases by targeting specific molecular mechanisms involved in heart failure.

## Research Question

In the tested doxorubicin-associated cardiac injury models, does delivery of a miR-185-5p inhibitor in peptide-functionalized liposomes alter cardiac function, injury and cell-death-associated readouts?

## Author Evidence Summary

This preclinical investigation assessed miR-185-5p-inhibitor-loaded liposomes in doxorubicin (DOX)-injured H9C2 cells, human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes in vitro and male C57BL/6 mouse models. The delivery vehicle is a peptide-functionalized liposome, not a clinical infusion of patient-derived exosomes. Loaded particles had a reported DLS diameter of 172.35 ± 0.8 nm and encapsulation efficiency of 98.0 ± 1.2%; TEM and DLS size measurements represent different measurement contexts. Mouse experiments reported greater cardiac fluorescence with the targeting peptide and improvements in echocardiographic and histological readouts. Cell experiments assessed apoptosis- and cuproptosis-associated proteins and other injury signals. These findings support the tested preclinical delivery strategy; they do not establish human efficacy, human safety, a general DCM therapy or complete pathway-specific causality. Abstract-only survival/percentage claims and unresolved protocol-reporting details are not promoted into independently verified quantitative key findings.

## How the Study Was Done

### Study profile

- Study Design: Preclinical formulation and intervention study using DOX-associated cellular and mouse cardiac injury models
- Evidence Type: Preclinical delivery, cardiac-phenotyping and cell-injury-associated evidence
- Population: H9C2 cells, human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes in vitro and male C57BL/6 mice under the reported DOX-associated injury conditions
- Primary Endpoint: Experimental cardiac function and myocardial injury readouts after the liposomal intervention
- Secondary Endpoint: Formulation characteristics, biodistribution, fibrosis, cell-death-associated markers and limited tolerability readouts
- External dataset evaluation: No
- Data modalities: liposome formulation and particle characterization, fluorescence biodistribution imaging, mouse echocardiography, histology, Western blot and cell-injury assays, short-term organ histology and biochemical testing

## What This Study Adds

- A peptide-functionalized liposomal delivery approach for a miR-185-5p inhibitory payload.
- Preclinical biodistribution and cardiac-function/histology evaluation in DOX-associated injury models.
- Cell-injury-associated molecular readouts and limited experimental tolerability assessment, with explicit separation from human treatment evidence.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Formulation characterization and labeled-preparation biodistribution in the tested experiments.
- Improvement in reported cardiac function and histology in the DOX-associated mouse model.
- Changes in cell-death-associated readouts in H9C2 and iPSC-derived cardiomyocyte experiments.
- Limited tolerability observations within the tested settings.

### Does Not Establish

- Human treatment efficacy, clinical safety or approval of the liposomal miR-185-5p intervention.
- A universally effective therapy for all etiologies of dilated cardiomyopathy.
- Exosome infusion or clinical transplantation of patient-derived vesicles.
- Exclusive cardiac targeting or an intact-drug tissue dose inferred solely from fluorescence.
- Complete miRNA-target/pathway-specific causality from marker changes alone.
- An independently verified quantitative survival advantage or harmonized percentage-effect estimate from the abstract alone.

### Limitations

- The experimental disease model is DOX-associated injury; generalization to other causes of human DCM is unestablished.
- The main text and abstract differ in quantitative presentation; abstract-only survival language and percentage effects are not elevated to independently verified key findings.
- The printed animal-model dosing description requires source/author clarification before experimental reproduction; no corrected dose or protocol recommendation is supplied.
- Human iPSC-derived cells, cultured-cell replicates and assay-specific mouse n values are not interchangeable biological units.
- Fluorescence targeting, marker changes and limited organ testing do not establish clinical biodistribution, complete causal mechanism or human safety.

## Q&A

### Is this a human DCM treatment trial?

No. It uses DOX-injured cell systems and mouse models. Human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes remain an in-vitro system, not treated patients.

Evidence: KF3, KF5

### What is being delivered: exosomes or liposomes?

The experimental carrier is a peptide-functionalized liposome carrying a miR-185-5p inhibitory payload. The exosome-related title and earlier expression work should not be read as a clinical exosome infusion.

Evidence: KF1

### How were the particles characterized?

The loaded formulation had a DLS diameter of 172.35 ± 0.8 nm, encapsulation efficiency of 98.0 ± 1.2% and approximately 80% release at 48 hours. TEM describes approximately 100-nm morphology; that is not the same measurement as hydrodynamic size.

Evidence: KF1

### What evidence supports cardiac targeting?

The targeting-peptide preparation showed greater cardiac fluorescence than the non-targeted preparation in the reported mouse comparison. Fluorescence does not by itself quantify an intact active-drug dose or establish exclusive heart delivery.

Evidence: KF2

### What cardiac and cellular outcomes were reported?

Mouse echocardiography and histology showed favorable changes under the tested conditions, and cell experiments showed changes in apoptosis- and cuproptosis-associated proteins. These are preclinical readouts, not human outcomes.

Evidence: KF3, KF4, KF5

### Does the article establish safety or a survival benefit in people?

No. Its tolerability testing is limited and experimental. Abstract-only survival and percentage claims are not treated here as independently verified key outcomes, and no human treatment cohort is present.

Evidence: KF3, KF6
