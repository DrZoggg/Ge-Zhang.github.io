## Evidence Scale

**Engineered cardiac-cell EVs protect endothelium and functionalize NiTi interfaces**

EVs from ZFP36-overexpressing AC16 cells improved viability and reduced lipid oxidation in RSL3-stressed endothelial cells. In a separate mouse infarction experiment, direct EV injection improved ventricular function and reduced fibrosis at day 21. A PDA/HA-modified NiTi surface retained and released EV-associated particles. The engineered coating supported endothelial cells in direct contact and improved vascular tissue markers at day 14 in rats.

## Research Question

Can EVs generated by ZFP36-engineered cardiac cells protect stressed endothelium and contribute biological activity when incorporated into a NiTi vascular interface?

## Author Evidence Summary

The study compares EVs from ZFP36-overexpressing AC16 cells with EVs from empty-vector donor cells. Under RSL3 exposure, engineered EVs improve endothelial viability and mitochondrial membrane-potential readouts and reduce reactive oxygen species and lipid oxidation. A separate mouse MI injection experiment reports higher left ventricular ejection fraction and fractional shortening at day 21, alongside a smaller fibrotic area than with control EVs. A polydopamine/hyaluronic acid coating on NiTi retains 72.80% ± 0.83% of input particles by nanoparticle tracking analysis and releases particles over 14 days in PBS. The direct-contact cell assays compare engineered and control EV coatings. In a three-arm rat arterial-filament experiment, the engineered coating has higher CD31 and α-SMA staining and lower TNF-α, OPN and 4-HNE staining at day 14.

## How the Study Was Done

### Study profile

- Study Design: Donor-cell engineering and EV characterization; RSL3-stressed endothelial intervention; intramyocardial EV delivery in mouse MI; PDA/HA NiTi functionalization, particle loading/release and direct-contact assays; rat femoral-artery injury/filament implantation.
- Evidence Type: Preclinical cellular intervention, particle/material characterization, animal cardiac function/histology and arterial tissue-marker measurements.
- Population: AC16 EV-producing cells and HUVEC recipients; male C57BL/6J mice for MI and male Sprague-Dawley rats for arterial injury/implantation.
- Primary Endpoint: Endothelial protective activity of donor-cell-engineered EVs and the resulting NiTi interface under the tested conditions.
- Secondary Endpoint: EV properties, ROS/mitochondrial/lipid oxidation readouts, mouse day-21 cardiac function/fibrosis, particle loading/release, and rat day-14 vascular morphology/markers.
- External dataset evaluation: No
- Data modalities: qPCR and immunoblot, NTA and TEM EV characterization, Endothelial viability/fluorescence assays, Mouse echocardiography and histology, AFM/SEM and contact angle, Particle loading and release, Rat arterial histology/immunostaining

## What This Study Adds

- Tests donor-cell-engineered EVs across endothelial stress, direct myocardial delivery and a functionalized implant interface.
- Provides a concrete PDA/HA-NiTi immobilization sequence with particle-based loading/release and directly contacting endothelial assays.
- Pairs short-term arterial histology with endothelial, inflammation, smooth-muscle-associated and lipid-oxidation tissue markers, with the actual comparator arms explicit.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- The observed preclinical EV-preparation and endothelial-intervention comparisons.
- Day-21 mouse function/fibrosis after myocardial injection and day-14 rat arterial tissue-marker responses to the coated filament.
- Particle/material characterization and direct-contact coating comparisons under the reported laboratory conditions.

### Does Not Establish

- An isolated ZFP36-cargo causal mechanism or long-term in-vivo EV retention/release and implant durability.
- An in-vivo engineered-versus-control-EV coating comparison: that arm appears in the cell contact experiment, not the rat implant experiment.
- Clinical coronary stent effectiveness, human treatment benefit or comprehensive chronic biosafety.

### Limitations

- Engineering AC16 cells alters a complete EV preparation; the responsible cargo and downstream molecular pathway remain unresolved.
- The rat implant study has no control-EV coating arm. Mouse myocardial injection and rat vascular implantation support different delivery-specific endpoints.
- The release study measures particles in PBS over 14 days. Longer-term coating stability, intact-cargo delivery, reproduction using primary-cell EVs and chronic biosafety remain open.
- The main article describes supplementary screens and assays. Exact discovery cohort identities, preparation yields and protein-to-particle ratios, and detailed values from Figures S3–S5 remain unresolved.

## Q&A

### What was engineered, and what does ZFP36OE-EV mean?

AC16 donor cells were engineered to overexpress ZFP36. Their released EV preparation is called ZFP36OE-EV. Empty-vector-transduced AC16 cells supplied control EVs. Figure 2 shows donor-cell expression and EV particle size, morphology and marker characterization.

Evidence: KF2

### What endothelial injury conditions and EV dose were tested?

The HUVEC experiment used 0.5 µM RSL3 for 12 hours and 3,000 NTA-counted EV particles per seeded cell. Engineered EVs improved viability and mitochondrial membrane-potential readouts and reduced reactive oxygen species and lipid oxidation relative to control EVs. Ferrostatin-1 served as a control specifically in the C11-BODIPY lipid-oxidation assay.

Evidence: KF3

### What mouse cardiac result followed direct EV injection?

During MI surgery, each EV-treated mouse received a total of 20 µg EV protein in 20 µL across five infarct-border-zone sites. Compared with control EVs, engineered EVs gave higher left ventricular ejection fraction and fractional shortening at day 21 (n = 6) and lower fibrosis area (n = 5).

Evidence: KF4

### How were EVs incorporated into NiTi and what release was measured?

Polydopamine followed by hyaluronic acid produced the NPH surface, which was incubated with engineered EVs and washed. Particle loading was 72.80% ± 0.83% (mean ± SD, n = 3). Nanoparticle tracking analysis measured EV-associated particles released over 14 days into PBS at 37 °C.

Evidence: KF5

### Did the cell-material experiment include a control-EV coating?

Yes. The stressed HUVECs contacted bare NiTi, NPH, NPH with control EVs or NPH with engineered EVs. Bare NiTi without RSL3 served as the baseline. The engineered coating gave a higher live-cell percentage and lower C11-BODIPY oxidation than the control-EV coating (n = 3 for the quantified assays).

Evidence: KF6

### What did the rat arterial implant experiment show?

At day 14, arteries with engineered-EV-coated filaments showed better-preserved architecture, higher CD31 and α-SMA positive areas, and lower TNF-α, OPN and 4-HNE positive areas than bare NiTi and PDA/HA-modified NPH (n = 5 for the marker panels). The rat experiment used those three material groups. Control-EV coating was tested in the separate cell-contact experiment.

Evidence: KF7

### What does the main paper report about the supplementary analyses?

The main article reports transcriptomic screening and Figure 1 shows day-3 mouse expression results. Main Results describes the mismatch in GABARAPL1 between the human screen and mouse comparison. It also reports EV preparation variability, HUVEC-associated fluorescent label, coating-extract effects and serum cytokines. The cited plots in Figures S1–S5 and the preparation yields and protein-to-particle ratios remain unresolved.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF6, KF7

### Which comparisons are most useful for designing follow-up studies?

The study separates particle-dose treatment of stressed cells, protein-dose myocardial injection and material-interface delivery. It uses control EVs in the free-EV, myocardial-injection and direct-contact experiments. The rat filament experiment uses bare NiTi and PDA/HA-modified NPH controls. These designs support comparisons of delivery routes, material controls and assay units.

Evidence: KF3, KF4, KF5, KF6, KF7
