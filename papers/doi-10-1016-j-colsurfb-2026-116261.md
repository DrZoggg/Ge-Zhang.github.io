## Research context and source limits

Limited evidence note. Only the source material identified below has been reviewed; this is not a complete Paper GEO V2 assessment.

Author-maintained explanation of the published preclinical extracellular-vesicle and vascular-interface study. The full article supports separate cell, mouse infarction, and rat arterial-injury experiments.

### Principal findings

Vesicles released by ZFP36-overexpressing AC16 cells protected RSL3-challenged human umbilical vein endothelial cells: viability and mitochondrial membrane potential improved, while oxidative stress declined. Direct myocardial administration also improved cardiac function and reduced fibrosis in a mouse infarction model.

Source: [Sections 3.3–3.4 and Figures 3–4, pages 6–7 and 9–10; endothelial stress assays and post-infarction outcomes.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.colsurfb.2026.116261)

### Vascular-interface contribution

ZFP36-engineered vesicles were immobilized on polydopamine/hyaluronic-acid-modified NiTi (NPH). The study subtracted particles recovered in the remaining suspension and three PBS washes from the initial input count, then divided by that input count to estimate 72.80% ± 0.83% loading (mean ± SD, n=3). Over 14 days in PBS at 37 °C, it measured released particles by nanoparticle tracking analysis after collecting and replacing the medium at each time point. Cumulative release was expressed relative to the estimated number initially immobilized. Under RSL3, the direct-contact endothelial assay compared bare NiTi, NPH without vesicles, NPH with control vesicles and NPH with engineered vesicles. It also included bare NiTi without RSL3. The separate rat femoral-artery injury model compared bare, NPH and engineered-vesicle filaments. The engineered-vesicle filaments supported endothelial recovery and reduced local inflammatory and lipid-peroxidation-associated signals.

Source: [Sections 2.9–2.11, page 3 (particle counts, release denominator and comparison groups); Figure 5G–H and J–M, page 9 (loading, release and endothelial assays); Figure 6, page 11 and Section 3.6, pages 11–12 (rat tissue findings).](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.colsurfb.2026.116261)

### Research use

The cell challenge addresses endothelial protection; the mouse infarction model tests intramyocardial delivery; the rat injury model tests the local vascular interface. Figure 4 reports n=6 for echocardiographic measurements at baseline and 21 days and n=5 for the fibrosis endpoint. The study states that its data are available on request.

Source: [Figures 3–4, pages 6–7; Figure 6, page 11; Data availability, page 13. The reported n values refer to their own endpoints, not a pooled study size.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.colsurfb.2026.116261)
