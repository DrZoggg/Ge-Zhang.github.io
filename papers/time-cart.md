## Evidence Base

**Deciphering the tumor immune microenvironment from a multidimensional omics perspective: insight into next-generation CAR-T cell immunotherapy and beyond**

This narrative review synthesizes how tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) composition, cellular spatial organization, genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, microbiome and machine-learning methods could inform CAR-T cell design and solid-tumor immunotherapy research. It presents no original prospective CAR-T trial, systematic pooled estimate, or clinically validated patient-selection tool.

## Research Question

Which tumor immune microenvironment features and omics-informed engineering strategies may help explain obstacles to CAR-T therapy in solid tumors, and which clinical claims remain unsupported by a narrative review?

## Author Evidence Summary

The authors discuss the spatial distribution of tumor and immune cells, host and tumor molecular context, antigen choice, metabolic persistence, immunosuppression, infiltration and exhaustion, supported by examples from the published literature. Claims about candidate interventions are attributed to cited prior studies and are not new experimental results of this review.

## Review Design & Evidence Synthesis

### Review profile

- Study Design: Narrative review of tumor immune microenvironment heterogeneity, multi-omics characterization, machine learning and candidate CAR-T design strategies.
- Evidence Type: Qualitative synthesis of prior mechanistic, computational, preclinical and clinical publications without reproducible meta-analytic pooling.
- Diseases Covered: Solid tumors with immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments, Tumor contexts used in cited immunotherapy studies
- Translation Scope: CAR-T and immunotherapy research strategies, especially solid tumors; the article does not test a new CAR-T product or treatment protocol.
- Evidence domains: Spatial immune contexture and immune-infiltration phenotypes, Genetic, transcriptomic, proteomic and microbiome dimensions of TIME, CAR-T antigen targeting, trafficking, metabolic fitness and exhaustion, Machine-learning analysis of spatial and molecular immune evidence

## What This Review Adds

- Provides a coherent account of the spatial, genetic, molecular and immune-cell dimensions relevant to CAR-T research.
- Connects candidate CAR-T engineering strategies with heterogeneity and immunosuppression barriers, while remaining a narrative literature synthesis.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- The published literature discusses spatially distinct immune phenotypes and diverse omics representations of TIME.
- Candidate CAR-T design and analytic strategies can be organized by antigen selection, cell fitness, infiltration and exhaustion.
- The review offers a historical research framework for questions about TIME and CAR-T engineering.

### Does Not Establish

- A new prospective study, newly recruited CAR-T treatment cohort or randomized comparison.
- A systematic review protocol, exhaustive meta-analysis or pooled clinical effect estimate.
- That any cited experimental design is universally safe, approved or superior in clinical solid tumors.
- A validated individual-patient CAR-T response model or definite clinical care recommendation.

### Limitations

- Narrative inclusion of research examples does not satisfy systematic search or risk-of-bias assessment criteria.
- Statements about specific clinical trials refer to original cited publications and may have changed since the 2024 review.
- Cited cell, mouse and clinical evidence differ in population, design, endpoints and translational readiness.
- No new external clinical dataset, treated cohort, novel model performance estimate or clinical benefit is generated by this review.

## Q&A

### Did this paper enroll CAR-T-treated patients?

No. This is a narrative review synthesizing existing studies and does not report a newly recruited clinical cohort or original therapeutic efficacy estimate.

Evidence: KF5

### Why do spatial immune-cell patterns matter?

Cells that remain in stromal or peripheral compartments may have different relationships with tumor cells than cells infiltrating malignant nests. The review summarizes these patterns from prior studies.

Evidence: KF1

### Which omics layers are discussed in TIME?

Host genetics, tumor genomes and epigenomes, transcriptomics, proteomics and microbiome data, alongside spatial and cellular measurements.

Evidence: KF2

### Which CAR-T engineering challenges does the review organize?

Antigen targeting, metabolic fitness, resistance to suppressive signals, tissue infiltration and exhaustion are discussed as areas for research, not proven universal solutions.

Evidence: KF3, KF5

### Does the review validate a machine-learning predictor for CAR-T response?

No. It discusses candidate machine-learning applications to pathology, antigen discovery and treatment response; no original prospective performance validation is established.

Evidence: KF4

### Can the clinical-trial counts in Table 1 be summed as this review’s patients?

No. The table describes cited external studies; its reported enrollment values are not new participants in the review or a pooled meta-analytic sample.

Evidence: KF5
