## Official Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) has advanced predictive modeling in medicine, yet many models remain disease-specific and difficult to generalize across clinical settings. Key challenges include the trade-off between interpretability and accuracy, reliance on single algorithms, limited external validation, and biased feature importance estimation.

In this Perspective, we discuss how methodological advances in computational sciences, including automated machine learning (AutoML) and neural architecture search (NAS), reveal a gap between automated hybrid systems and current clinical modeling practices.

To address these challenges, we outline a principled artificial intelligence hybrid ensemble framework based on three design principles: integration of diverse learners, consensus-driven validation across independent cohorts, and transparent feature attribution using Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP). This framework emphasizes methodological robustness, interpretability, and cross-disease applicability to support the translation of artificial intelligence models into clinical practice.

## Research Question

Which methodological principles should guide the design, evaluation and interpretation of clinical AI ensembles?

## Author Evidence Summary

The Perspective connects clinical AI design to the practical demands of evaluating and interpreting ensemble models. It advocates combining complementary learners according to the clinical setting and selecting models through aggregate evaluation across independent cohorts. Its discussion of SHAP emphasizes that feature attributions depend on data distributions and should be considered alongside sensitivity analysis and calibration. Distribution shift, computational resources, standardization and clinical workflow integration frame the translation agenda. On this evidence page, these arguments support methodological choices and limitations discussions; performance estimates and empirical validation claims belong to the cited primary studies, not to this Perspective.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Cite for a context-sensitive ensemble design rationale, not as an original comparison proving that a particular ensemble outperforms alternatives.
- Cite when planning independent-cohort evaluation and model selection; obtain actual validation results from the relevant empirical studies.
- Cite for methodological caution about attribution robustness; SHAP values are not evidence that a feature causally changes an outcome.
- Cite for resource, evaluation and workflow considerations in a translation plan, not as proof of improved patient outcomes.

### Does Not Establish

- A newly developed model with completed clinical validation or demonstrated cross-disease generalization.
- Original AUC, C-index, ablation or treatment-effect estimates generated in this Perspective.
- Stable causal mechanisms inferred from SHAP feature attributions.
- Proven patient-outcome improvement, clinical guideline status or regulatory approval.

### Limitations

- This is a methodological Perspective, not a systematic review or meta-analysis with a reported systematic search protocol.
- Claims about samples and model performance in cited applications require the corresponding primary studies; those studies were not all independently re-audited for this page.
- Proposed independent-cohort evaluation, attribution robustness checks and deployment principles still require empirical implementation and assessment.
