Official Abstract

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive and rapidly fatal disease with an intricate etiology. Identifying biomarkers for early PAH lesions based on the exploration of subtle biological processes is significant for timely diagnosis and treatment. In the present study, nine distinct cell populations identified based on gene expression profiles revealed high heterogeneity in cell composition ratio, biological function, distribution preference, and communication patterns in PAH. Notably, compared to other cells, endothelial cells (ECs) showed prominent variation in multiple perspectives. Further analysis demonstrated the endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) in ECs and identified a subgroup exhibiting a contrasting phenotype. Based on these findings, a machine-learning integrated program consisting of nine learners was developed to create a PAH Endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition Signature (PETS). This study identified cell populations underlying EndMT and furnished a potential tool that might be valuable for PAH diagnosis and new precise therapies.

Research Question

Which endothelial transcriptional states are associated with pulmonary arterial hypertension, and can genes selected from an EndMT-related state support a candidate molecular classifier of PAH versus control tissue?

Author Evidence Summary

This research combined public single-cell and bulk lung transcriptome analyses with targeted endothelial expression experiments. The single-cell analysis began with 31,444 cells from three PAH and six control samples and retained 28,906 cells after quality control. Trajectory analyses focused on 3,165 endothelial cells and identified an EC3 state with expression patterns consistent with mesenchymal transition and altered metabolism. Co-expression and marker-based selection nominated six EndMT pattern genes. Nine machine-learning approaches were compared using an internal split of GSE117261, and a glmNet-derived two-gene score, PETS, combined RARRES2 and TC2N. The reported internal testing-subset accuracy was 0.8333 and C-index was 0.8908; because that subset also guided algorithm comparison and model selection, these estimates may be optimistic. Targeted hypoxia experiments supported expression differences for selected genes, but the work did not demonstrate that the genes drive EndMT, establish a clinically validated serum assay, or show that a therapy directed at the score improves PAH. A secondary marker-count discrepancy between the article text and its supplementary table is retained as an explicit limitation.

What This Study Adds

  • It relates PAH-associated endothelial heterogeneity to an explicit EndMT-related transcriptional state using complementary computational trajectory methods.
  • It connects candidate-gene prioritization to an interpretable two-gene tissue-expression classifier and reports its internal model comparison.
  • It places limited expression experiments alongside retrospective computational evidence, creating testable hypotheses for prospective biomarker and mechanistic work.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • PAH-associated endothelial transcriptional heterogeneity and computationally inferred EndMT-related expression programs.
  • A candidate two-gene PETS classifier with internal test-set performance under the reported dataset and analysis procedure.
  • Selected expression changes in hypoxic endothelial experimental systems.

Does Not Establish

  • Direct lineage-tracing proof that the inferred trajectory is an in vivo cell-fate conversion.
  • A causal molecular mechanism from RARRES2 or TC2N to PAH or EndMT.
  • An independently externally validated or prospective diagnostic test, or demonstrated detection of early disease.
  • A validated serum PETS assay: the score was derived from transcriptomic expression, so discussion of serum translation remains a hypothesis.
  • Clinical efficacy or safety of chemerin/CMKLR1-targeted therapy, or any PETS-guided treatment benefit.

Limitations

  • Human datasets are retrospective and the single-cell analysis has few donor samples; thousands of cells do not imply thousands of independent patients.
  • Trajectory inference, pathway scoring, ligand-receptor inference and co-expression are computational associations, not direct causal mechanisms.
  • The machine-learning testing subset is an internal split of GSE117261 and was also used to compare the nine algorithms and select the preferred model. Reported performance may therefore be optimistic. No untouched final test set, independent external clinical validation or prospective validation was established.
  • PETS is based on transcript expression. Discussion of serum-marker applications should not be read as a validated blood test.
  • Main text reports 602 EC3 marker genes, but the retrieved original Table S4 contains 604 distinct gene rows. This unresolved ancillary count difference is not silently harmonized.
  • A Results sentence describes lower incoming and higher outgoing communications but labels the role change in the opposite direction; no sender/receiver direction claim is carried into this evidence page.
  • A main-text sentence refers to MSRB1 while the candidate list and primer list use MSRB3. The page avoids drawing a gene-specific conclusion from that inconsistent sentence.
  • Targeted expression experiments do not demonstrate a beneficial intervention. Experimental sample sizes and fold-change values are not invented from plots.

Q&A

How many people and cells were represented in the single-cell analysis?

The article reports three PAH and six control donor samples, initially containing 31,444 cells, with 28,906 retained after quality control. These are sample and cell counts, not interchangeable participant totals.

Evidence: KF1

Does pseudotime prove that endothelial cells became mesenchymal cells?

No. The ordering of 3,165 endothelial cells is inferred from cross-sectional expression. EndMT-related gene programs support a biological hypothesis, but direct lineage tracing or causal perturbation would be needed to establish cell conversion.

Evidence: KF2

What genes form the final PETS score?

The reported score is 2.6926723 × RARRES2 + 0.7410611 × TC2N expression. It was selected from a broader computationally nominated candidate set.

Evidence: KF3, KF4

What does the reported model performance mean?

In the GSE117261 internal testing subset, Table S7 reports glmNet accuracy 0.8333 and C-index 0.8908. The same subset was used to compare the nine algorithms and select the preferred model, so the reported performance may be optimistic. These are retrospective classification estimates, not prospective clinical accuracy or treatment benefit.

Evidence: KF4

Was PETS externally validated in an independent clinical cohort?

No independent external clinical validation is established by the reported 7:3 split. Discovery and testing came from GSE117261. The targeted expression experiments provide a different kind of evidence and do not substitute for clinical validation.

Evidence: KF4, KF5

Do the experiments demonstrate a therapeutic mechanism?

No. The reported experiments assess selected expression responses to hypoxia. They do not demonstrate that perturbing a candidate gene reverses EndMT or improves PAH outcomes.

Evidence: KF5

Are all ancillary gene counts internally consistent?

No. The main text reports 602 EC3 marker genes, whereas the retrieved Table S4 contains 604 unique gene rows. This page preserves the discrepancy and does not use that marker count as a confirmed scale metric.

Evidence: KF3
