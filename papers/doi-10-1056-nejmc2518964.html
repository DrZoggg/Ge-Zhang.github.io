Research Question

Does the timing of aspirin withdrawal help frame interpretation of early ischemic risk in NEO-MINDSET, and what should future de-escalation studies examine?

Author Evidence Summary

Li, Zhang and Liu interpret the NEO-MINDSET result through the timing of aspirin withdrawal. They highlight that 83% of monotherapy patients who had stent thrombosis experienced it within 30 days, as reported in their letter, and propose studying time-adaptive de-escalation and platelet-function testing. This is commentary on prior evidence, not a new trial or proof that testing-guided withdrawal improves outcomes. Only their signed letter is synthesized; the other letters and trialists' reply sharing this DOI are excluded.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Attribution of the timing argument and proposed research agenda to Li, Zhang and Liu.
  • Accurate reporting of the letter's early-event proportion with its restricted denominator.

Does Not Establish

  • New randomized efficacy or safety evidence.
  • A causal benefit of delaying aspirin withdrawal or of platelet-function-guided care.
  • A patient-specific stopping date or validated treatment threshold.

Limitations

  • The event proportion is reported by the correspondence; this synthesis does not independently reanalyze the NEO-MINDSET dataset.
  • The shared DOI includes separate letters and a reply. Their statistics and recommendations are not attributed to Li, Zhang and Liu.
  • Full text was read from the author's ResearchGate upload in an HTML text rendition; the PDF binary and a publisher-hosted full text were not retrieved.
