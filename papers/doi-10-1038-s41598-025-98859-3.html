Research Question

How did recovery, postoperative symptoms and reported oncological follow-up differ between endoscopic and laparoscopic resection in selected patients with 2–5 cm EGJ-GIST?

Author Evidence Summary

The study retrospectively reviewed treatment at one Chinese centre from December 2013 to November 2023. The original flowchart reports 176 screened patients, 85 eligible patients and 20 matched pairs. The authors used age, sex, tumour size, mitotic count and risk classification for propensity matching. In Table 2, the ER and LR columns report median postoperative fasting of 4.0 and 5.5 days, hospital stay of 6.0 and 8.5 days, and long-term symptom counts of 3/20 and 11/20. En bloc resection is reported in 18/20 and 19/20, with acute adverse events in 2/20 and 3/20. These are the published comparison results. The abstract reverses the eligible ER/LR allocation shown in the Results, flowchart and Table 1; Table 1 also labels the matched LR group n=20 while listing 10 males and 20 females. Matching balance and treatment superiority are therefore not certified. Follow-up averaged 42.3 months after matching; no recurrence was reported, with adjuvant imatinib given to high-risk patients. The study contributes site-specific procedural and outcome-reporting context, including the distinction between symptom reports and directly measured quality of life or EGJ function.

Evidence Snapshot

EGJ-GIST resection: reported recovery and symptoms after endoscopic or laparoscopic treatment

This single-centre retrospective study describes endoscopic and laparoscopic resection for selected 2–5 cm esophagogastric-junction GISTs. It reports recovery times, resection outcomes and postoperative symptoms in a 20-pair analysis; conflicting group allocations and matched-table counts remain explicit alongside the comparisons.

Counting note

The total eligible cohort is consistently reported as 85 and the matched analysis as 20 patients per treatment column (40 of those 85). These are not added. The abstract gives eligible ER 42/LR 43; Results, Figure 1 and Table 1 give ER 43/LR 42. The abstract also reports 176 reviewed patients as ER 82/LR 94, while Figure 1 is a screening flow with exclusions including other modalities. No corrected allocation is inferred. Matched Table 1 labels LR n=20 but lists 10 males plus 20 females; the 50% labels do not reconcile those counts. The published total 85 and matched 40 are described as reported sample sizes, without certifying participant-level matching integrity.

What This Study Adds

  • Provides a site-specific account of selection and procedural options for 2–5 cm EGJ-GIST, including endoscopic techniques and anatomical reasons for different laparoscopic resections.
  • Reports recovery, resection and symptom measures that can inform the choice of outcomes for future comparative studies.
  • Identifies why allocation accounting, verified matching, validated QoL instruments, direct functional assessment and longer risk-aware follow-up matter when interpreting this treatment comparison.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • The article’s eligibility criteria, anatomical definitions and stated propensity matching procedure.
  • Exact published Table 2 comparison values and symptom categories, attributed to their ER/LR columns.
  • Reported recurrence follow-up and high-risk adjuvant-treatment context.

Does Not Establish

  • A corrected unmatched treatment allocation or verified matched-group balance.
  • Causal recovery, safety, functional or oncological superiority of ER over LR; nonsignificant tests are not equivalence tests.
  • A validated quality-of-life improvement, directly measured EGJ functional preservation or recurrence-risk equivalence.
  • Generalization to larger/metastatic lesions, neoadjuvant-treated patients or routine operators outside the stated selection and expertise.

Limitations

  • The abstract reverses the eligible unmatched ER/LR allocation relative to the Results, Figure 1 and Table 1; no corrected allocation is supplied here.
  • Matched Table 1 lists 10 males and 20 females in an LR group labelled n=20. Its tumour-size row is labelled mm although the eligibility range is 2–5 cm. Group balance and corrected cell/unit values remain unresolved.
  • Treatment choice was retrospective and anatomically selected at one centre, with small matched columns and experienced ER operators. Matching cannot remove unmeasured confounding, and its integrity is itself unresolved here.
  • Long-term symptom records used different symptom categories and lacked validated structured QoL instruments or routine direct EGJ functional assessment. The reported difference does not certify functional or safety superiority.
  • Follow-up averaged about 3.5 years; high-risk patients also received adjuvant imatinib. Zero reported recurrence does not establish oncological equivalence, and prospective multicentre/longer follow-up plans in Discussion are proposals, not completed validation.

  • Verified balanced matching, causal ER superiority or safety/effectiveness equivalence.
  • Validated quality-of-life improvement or directly demonstrated preservation of EGJ physiology.
  • Oncological equivalence, a surgery-only recurrence effect or a general treatment recommendation beyond the studied selection.

Q&A

Which patients and anatomical setting does this study describe?

It is a single-centre retrospective study from December 2013 to November 2023 of pathologically confirmed, nonmetastatic 2–5 cm EGJ-GIST treated by ER or LR. The EGJ definition includes gastric lesions with an upper border within 5 cm of the esophagogastric line and esophageal lesions with a lower border less than 2 cm from it. Figure 1 reports 85 eligible patients and 20 matched pairs.

Evidence: KF1

How was propensity matching performed, and what is unresolved?

The authors describe 1:1 nearest-neighbour matching from logistic-regression propensity scores using age, sex, tumour size, mitotic count and risk classification, with the stated 0.1-SD/logit caliper. The abstract gives ER 42/LR 43, whereas Results, Figure 1 and Table 1 give ER 43/LR 42. After matching, Table 1 labels LR n=20 but prints 10 males and 20 females. These original inconsistencies leave matching balance and exact allocation unresolved.

Evidence: KF2

What procedures are represented by ER and LR?

ER includes ESD, ESE or EFTR selected by lesion-layer involvement and was performed by experienced endoscopists. LR may involve wedge/partial resection or proximal gastrectomy when the cardia cannot be preserved. The labels therefore represent anatomically selected procedural strategies, not a single standardized operation on every patient.

Evidence: KF3

What recovery results are printed in the matched comparison?

Table 2 reports median postoperative fasting of 4.0 days in ER versus 5.5 in LR (p=0.005), and hospitalization of 6.0 versus 8.5 days (p=0.002). Both procedure-time medians are 105 min (p=0.633). These are the published column-wise results; unresolved allocation and matching-table counts prevent certifying a treatment advantage.

Evidence: KF2, KF4

What resection and acute adverse-event counts are reported?

Table 2 reports en bloc resection in 18/20 ER and 19/20 LR patients (p=1.000), and acute postoperative adverse events in 2/20 and 3/20 (p=0.572). Table 3 supplies the corresponding five case descriptions. Small counts and nonsignificant tests do not demonstrate equivalent safety or equivalent resection outcomes.

Evidence: KF5

Do the long-term symptom results establish better quality of life or EGJ function?

The original table and Figure 3 report 3/20 ER patients with lack of appetite and 11/20 LR patients with acid reflux/heartburn (p=0.005 for the long-term outcome comparison). These are different reported symptom categories. The Discussion acknowledges no structured validated QoL instruments and no routine direct physiological assessment, so the symptom observations are not a verified QoL or anatomical-function benefit.

Evidence: KF5

How should the absence of reported recurrence be interpreted?

Results report no recurrence in the 85 eligible patients; matched follow-up averaged 42.3±20.5 months, with maximum 100 months reported in Discussion. All high-risk patients received postoperative adjuvant imatinib and imaging surveillance. These observations describe the treated cohort and do not establish oncological equivalence or isolate the effect of ER versus LR.

Evidence: KF6

How can this paper be used in a treatment-methods comparison?

It can support discussion of EGJ-specific patient selection, endoscopic versus laparoscopic procedural choices, and the recovery and symptom outcomes a retrospective comparison recorded. Quote numerical results as reported, preserve the allocation/table inconsistencies, and distinguish symptoms and co-treated follow-up from causal treatment benefit or validated QoL improvement.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3, KF4, KF5, KF6
