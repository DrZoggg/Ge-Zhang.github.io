Research Question

Which genetically proxied microbial traits were associated with AA and AAA in the MiBioGen–FinnGen summary-data analysis?

Author Evidence Summary

The study reports AA associations for Family XIII UCG001 and Bifidobacteriaceae, and an AAA association for Bilophila. These are genetic-proxy estimates under Mendelian-randomization assumptions, not effects of changing the microbiome. Alternative estimator intervals and unresolved endpoint, taxon-count and supplementary-verification limitations are retained.

Evidence Scale

Gut microbial genetic proxies and aortic aneurysm

A two-sample Mendelian randomization study linked MiBioGen microbial traits with FinnGen aneurysm outcomes. This page reports verified AA and AAA main-table estimates, excluding TAA-specific findings because its endpoint description is inconsistent.

Counting note

Exposure and outcome sources are not combined into a patient total. AA and subtype outcomes overlap. Methods state 196 retained taxa, while Results counts (9 phyla, 16 classes, 20 orders, 31 families, 119 genera) sum to 195; this remains unresolved.

Study Design & Analytical Framework

Study profile

Study Design
Two-sample Mendelian randomization of public microbial-trait and aneurysm GWAS summary statistics.
Evidence Type
Genetic-instrument association estimates under MR assumptions, with alternative estimator comparisons.
Population
MiBioGen multi-ethnic microbiome GWAS across 24 cohorts and FinnGen version 9 outcomes from a Finnish biobank population.
Primary Endpoint
Aortic aneurysm (AA) case-control GWAS outcome.
Secondary Endpoint
Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA); TAA was analyzed in the paper but is excluded here because of source endpoint ambiguity.
External dataset evaluation
No

Data modalities

  • 16S microbiome-trait GWAS summary associations
  • SNP instruments
  • FinnGen case-control GWAS summary statistics

What This Study Adds

  • Identifies hypothesis-generating microbial genetic-proxy associations with aneurysm outcomes.
  • Preserves differences between IVW and alternative-estimator uncertainty rather than treating all MR outputs as uniform causal evidence.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • The stated MiBioGen and FinnGen data-source design and AA/AAA estimates printed in main Table 1.
  • Different uncertainty across MR estimators for the same microbial trait and outcome.

Does Not Establish

  • Benefits from changing the microbiome, probiotics, antibiotics or aneurysm-prevention treatment.
  • A validated screening biomarker, patient-level risk score or clinical threshold.
  • Proof of absent pleiotropy, valid instruments, or independently reproduced supplementary sensitivity and full multiple-testing results.
  • A resolved TAA endpoint definition or reconciled screen-wide microbial-taxon count.

Limitations

  • The TAA Methods definition uses thoracoabdominal rather than thoracic outcomes; TAA-specific findings are excluded pending clarification.
  • The retained-taxon total is stated as 196, while Results category counts sum to 195; neither is presented as a reconciled screen-wide count.
  • Bonferroni correction is described by taxonomic level, but equation images and supplements were unavailable. Main-table estimates are reported as author-selected associations, not an independently reproduced screen-wide significance claim.
  • Alternative estimator intervals do not uniformly exclude the null; MR estimates remain assumption-dependent.
  • The exposure GWAS is multi-ethnic and the outcome source is Finnish. Portability, population structure and sample overlap require consideration; overlap was not quantified here.
  • Supplementary MR-PRESSO, Steiger, leave-one-out and instrument tables were not independently inspected. Non-significant diagnostic tests would not prove the absence of pleiotropy.
  • No intervention, prospective diagnostic validation or clinical treatment benefit was tested.

Q&A

What populations and statistical units were analyzed?

MiBioGen contributed microbial-trait summary associations from 18,473 people across 24 cohorts. FinnGen provided AA and AAA case-control summary statistics. These separate sources and overlapping aneurysm outcomes must not be summed as one cohort. Source: Methods, Data Source for Gut Microbiota and Data Source for Outcomes.

Evidence: KF1

Which AA estimates can be cited?

Table 1 reports IVW OR 1.33 (95% CI 1.14–1.55) for genus Family XIII UCG001 and 0.79 (0.70–0.89) for family Bifidobacteriaceae. These are genetic-proxy estimates, not effects of adding or removing bacteria. Source: Table 1, AA rows.

Evidence: KF2

Was the Bilophila–AAA result consistent across all MR methods?

The IVW OR was 1.36 (95% CI 1.17–1.57), but MR-Egger, weighted-median and weighted-mode intervals all included 1. The result is method-dependent rather than unanimously confirmed. Source: Table 1, AAA / Genus Bilophila rows.

Evidence: KF3

Does this demonstrate that microbiome treatment prevents aneurysm?

No. The study analyzed genetic instruments and summary case-control data, without testing treatment or prevention. Interpretation depends on MR relevance, independence and exclusion-restriction assumptions. Source: Methods, Statistical Analysis and Sensitivity Analysis; Table 1.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3

Why are TAA findings and strong robustness claims omitted?

Methods describe the TAA endpoint using thoracoabdominal outcomes, while the title and subtype labels say thoracic. Methods also report 196 taxa but the Results category counts sum to 195. Supplements and equation/figure assets were not retrievable for independent checking. These discrepancies remain open; no TAA estimate or verified absence of pleiotropy is asserted. Source: Methods, Data Source for Outcomes and Sensitivity Analysis; Results, Selection of Instrumental Variables for Gut Microbiomes.

Evidence: KF1
