## Evidence Scale

**AMI Olink proteomics: a five-protein candidate signature with plasma and public-data follow-up**

A targeted 92-protein Olink screen identified an AMI-associated protein profile and prioritized PCOLCE, FCN2, REG1A, DEFA1 and CRTAC1. Additional plasma ELISA measurements reproduced their higher abundance, while public gene-expression and genetic analyses supplied separate evidence layers.

| Evidence | Value |

| Olink discovery | 30 participants: 20 AMI and 10 controls; 92 predefined proteins |

### Counting note

The article explicitly reports local enrollment of 275 and separates the 30-person Olink set from the Methods/Table 1 ELISA set of 245. The Figure 5 AMI count conflicts with that decomposition, so no figure-specific cohort total is silently substituted. GEO samples and genetic summary-data counts are different evidence units and are not added to local participant counts. External validation here denotes public gene-expression evaluation, not an independently validated plasma diagnostic model.

## Research Question

Which cardiometabolic plasma proteins differ between patients with acute myocardial infarction and healthy controls, and how do prioritized candidates perform in an additional plasma assay, public ischemic-disease expression datasets and genetic association analyses?

## Author Evidence Summary

This study used the Olink Cardiometabolic panel to measure 92 plasma proteins in 20 patients with AMI and 10 healthy controls. The Results report 32 differential proteins, including 12 higher and 20 lower in AMI. Five candidates, PCOLCE, FCN2, REG1A, DEFA1 and CRTAC1, were selected for further study; all five showed higher levels by ELISA in the additional plasma cohort. Methods and Table 1 describe 125 AMI cases and 120 controls, whereas the Figure 5 caption gives 120 cases and 120 controls. Public GEO expression datasets were analyzed separately, with reported combined AUCs from 0.678 to 0.825 across the article-labeled AMI and ICM datasets; the GSE57388/GSE57338 accession mismatch and the absence of DEFA1 in ICM affect the interpretation of those analyses. Clinical correlations and enrichment analyses relate the protein pattern to cardiac function, metabolic measures and matrix/immune programs. Exploratory MR showed nominal IVW associations for FCN2 and DEFA1, with non-significant MR-Egger and weighted-median estimates. The contribution is a source-located candidate-selection framework connecting targeted protein discovery to several complementary evidence types.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Single-center cross-sectional targeted plasma-proteomics discovery and additional plasma ELISA assessment, followed by separate public gene-expression ROC and two-sample MR analyses. Coronary blood was collected before angiography, processed in Na-EDTA tubes at 3000g for 15 minutes at 4C and stored at -80C.
- Evidence Type: Targeted proximity extension assay; orthogonal ELISA; Pearson correlation; GO/KEGG enrichment; STRING/Cytoscape network analysis; public gene-expression ROC; exploratory MR.
- Population: Patients hospitalized with AMI at Taihe County People's Hospital from January to December 2023 who underwent angiography and PCI, plus healthy controls; separate public AMI/ICM expression datasets and European-ancestry genetic summary data.
- Primary Endpoint: Differential abundance of predefined cardiometabolic plasma proteins and follow-up of five prioritized protein candidates.
- Secondary Endpoint: Associations with clinical measures, enrichment/network context, discrimination in existing gene-expression datasets and genetically proxied associations with myocardial infarction.
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: Coronary-blood-derived plasma Olink NPX on a log2 arbitrary-unit scale, Additional plasma ELISA measurements, Clinical characteristics and cardiac-function measures, Public GEO gene-expression data, deCODE/FinnGen genetic summary data

## What This Study Adds

- Provides a defined cardiometabolic plasma-protein candidate set for AMI, with five candidates carried forward into a second assay.
- Connects protein measurements with clinical correlations and computational matrix/immune/metabolic context for candidate prioritization.
- Offers a concrete example for comparing evidence across plasma proteins, public gene expression and genetic summary data while retaining the identity and limits of each source.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Targeted-panel associations between the measured proteins and the AMI-versus-healthy-control contrast.
- Directionally consistent ELISA follow-up of five selected plasma proteins in an additional participant set.
- Source-specific clinical correlations, computational enrichment and separately reported public expression-data and MR results.

### Does Not Establish

- Prospective early-detection performance, a clinical decision threshold, incremental value over standard cardiac biomarkers or improved patient outcomes.
- External plasma-proteomic transportability from public gene-expression ROC analyses.
- Causal mechanisms or treatment benefit from protein correlations/enrichment, or definitive causality from nominal method-dependent MR estimates.

### Limitations

- The Olink discovery set contains 20 AMI cases and 10 healthy controls from a single center; prospective and multicenter diagnostic performance was not evaluated.
- Methods/Table 1 specify 125 AMI participants for ELISA, whereas the Figure 5 caption specifies 120; the analyzed figure-specific sample count is unresolved.
- The body/Figure 5 GSE57388 label differs from Table S1 GSE57338; DEFA1 is absent from the ICM datasets, and expression-data ROC is a different evidence modality from plasma ELISA.
- Methods specify FDR correction for differential expression, while the Results identify the 32-protein set using p<0.05 without a per-protein adjusted-value mapping.
- The FCN2 and DEFA1 MR associations are nominal and estimator-dependent; exposure units, precise source-instrument mapping and multiplicity handling are insufficiently specified for a definitive causal claim.

## Q&A

### What did the Olink screen measure in AMI?

The predefined Cardiometabolic panel measured 92 plasma proteins in 20 AMI cases and 10 healthy controls. It used a dual-antibody proximity extension workflow with PCR quantification and returned log2 NPX arbitrary units; it did not measure small-molecule metabolites or absolute protein concentrations. The Results report 32 differential proteins, 12 higher and 20 lower in AMI.

Evidence: KF1

### Which candidates were carried forward to ELISA, and what was replicated?

PCOLCE, FCN2, REG1A, DEFA1 and CRTAC1 were prioritized using fold changes and bioinformatics. All five showed higher plasma levels in AMI in the additional ELISA assessment, with P<0.01 reported in Results. This replicates the abundance direction using a second assay.

Evidence: KF2

### How many participants were included in discovery and ELISA follow-up?

Discovery comprised 20 AMI cases and 10 controls. Methods and Table 1 report an additional 125 AMI cases and 120 controls, consistent with the article-reported local enrollment of 275. The Figure 5 caption instead labels 120 AMI cases and 120 controls; that discrepancy remains unresolved.

Evidence: KF1, KF2

### How should the public GEO ROC results be used?

Use them as separate retrospective gene-expression evaluations across AMI and ICM. Reported combined AUCs are 0.782 for GSE66360, 0.780 for GSE48060, 0.825 for GSE5406 and 0.693 for GSE57345. The 0.678 result is assigned to GSE57388 in the body, while Table S1 lists GSE57338. DEFA1 was absent from the ICM datasets. These results should not be presented as external validation of an identical five-protein plasma assay.

Evidence: KF3

### Which clinical correlations are directly supported?

Table 2 reports positive correlations of all five prioritized proteins with BMI and LDL-C. For example, PCOLCE correlates with BMI at r=0.768 and with LVEF at r=-0.473; CRTAC1 correlates with LVEF at r=-0.550, all with reported p<0.01. The table also contains non-significant protein-measure pairs, so a general claim that every marker tracks every cardiac measure would be inaccurate.

Evidence: KF4

### What biological interpretation is supported by the pathway and network analyses?

The differential-protein set was enriched for extracellular-matrix/collagen, immune and metabolic annotations, including PI3K-AKT signaling. The article reports 91 GO terms, 52 KEGG pathways and a 22-protein interaction network. These analyses organize functional hypotheses; they are not experiments showing that changing a candidate protein alters AMI injury.

Evidence: KF5

### Do the MR results establish FCN2 and DEFA1 as causal AMI targets?

The study reports nominal IVW associations: FCN2 OR 1.514 (95% CI 1.095-2.095; p=0.0122; 24 SNPs) and DEFA1 OR 1.179 (95% CI 1.024-1.358; p=0.0224; 5 SNPs). In Table S2, both MR-Egger and weighted-median estimates are non-significant at 0.05. The evidence supports exploratory genetically proxied associations, with exposure scaling and instrument mapping still needing clarification for stronger causal interpretation.

Evidence: KF6

### When is this study useful in a biomarker methods comparison?

It is useful as a targeted-panel-to-candidate workflow: Olink screening, selection of five proteins, second-assay plasma follow-up, then distinct public expression and genetic analyses. Comparisons should retain the specimen, assay, disease phenotype and validation unit for each stage.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3, KF6
