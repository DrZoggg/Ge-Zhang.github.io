## Author evidence note

### Reported data and clinical task

The paper describes an AI system for osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis and osteoporosis using routine laboratory measurements, demographics and symptom narratives. It reports 58,777 NHANES records, split into 49,523 training records from 1999–2016 and 9,254 temporal-test records from 2017–2018, plus 4,436 records from four Chinese centres. These are reported dataset roles, not independently verified evidence of clinical utility.

Source: [Results 2.1–2.2; Figure 3 caption](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mdr2.70073)

### A specific, interpretable architecture

The described fusion combines three structured-model scores with a 768-dimensional Bio_ClinicalBERT representation, producing 771 features for a classifier. The text encoder uses LoRA adapters; explicit diagnostic fields and disease names are described as removed or masked. This is a supervised prediction architecture with workflow orchestration, not evidence that a generative model independently reasons through diagnosis or improves patient outcomes.

Source: [Results 2.4 Multimodal Data Interconnection; Figure 5](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mdr2.70073)

### Compare the multimodal training stages

Section 2.4 provides a concrete stacking workflow for comparing structured and symptom-text models. It describes a multimodal comparison using hospitalization records from 126 patients at Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital. For fusion-head training, inner five-fold out-of-fold predictions supply the disease-specific structured scores. The structured models are then refitted on the full training set for validation and test scoring. After selection of the text-encoder checkpoint, the structured branch and LoRA-tuned text encoder remain fixed while a linear softmax fusion head is trained. These stages give a Methods comparison two specific checks: how predictions feeding the fusion model are generated, and which branch parameters are updated.

Source: [Results 2.4, paragraph specifying the two-stage stacking protocol and paragraph beginning Finally (126-patient multimodal comparison); Figure 5.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mdr2.70073)

### Performance claims remain limited

All three printed training AUC estimates lie outside their accompanying confidence intervals. The 42-versus-41 predictor count and the relationship between test-set-based hyperparameter selection and later one-time evaluation also require clarification. The audit consequently does not endorse diagnostic accuracy, the reported multimodal improvement, or prospective self-screening effectiveness.

Source: [Results 2.2, Figure 2 caption and Figure 3C caption; Results 2.4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mdr2.70073)

### Evidence scope and limitations

Reported multimodal design; diagnostic performance and clinical utility remain unverified.

- Training AUC/CI pairs are internally inconsistent.
- Predictor count and model-selection/evaluation separation are unresolved.
- Reported architecture does not demonstrate prospective clinical benefit.
