## Official Abstract

Musculoskeletal disorders such as osteoarthritis (OA), rheumatoid arthritis (RA), and osteoporosis (OP) affect millions worldwide, often progressing silently until advanced stages. Accessible early risk stratification remains challenging because conventional diagnosis often depends on imaging, specialist evaluation, and fragmented clinical information. Herein, we developed Luma AI, a multimodal AI platform that integrates routine blood tests and demographic variables with curated patient-reported symptom narratives. The text branch uses a domain-specific clinical text encoder based on LoRA-adapter-fine-tuned Bio_ClinicalBERT, whereas the structured branch uses optimized classical machine learning models. The two branches are fused through an explicit prediction-score and text-representation pipeline for early screening of OA, RA, and OP. Performance was validated using the U.S. NHANES and multi-center Chinese cohorts. We evaluated more than 50 predictive models and applied Bayesian optimization to refine the structured models, achieving over 80% accuracy with structured data alone and exceeding 90% in the multimodal validation cohort when symptom narratives were included. The platform incorporates explainable AI tools to identify key risk factors and provides personalized post-screening recommendations for lifestyle, diet, and medical follow-up. This study demonstrates that Luma AI can support scalable low-cost early risk screening and personalized management of OA, RA, and OP while remaining positioned as a risk-stratification aid rather than a stand-alone diagnostic replacement.

## Research Question

## Author Evidence Summary

The study reports 58,777 NHANES records, with 49,523 from 1999–2016 for training and 9,254 from 2017–2018 for temporal testing. The Chinese structured evaluation used data from 4,436 patients across four centers. After benchmarking structured classifiers, the authors selected Extra Trees and used SHAP to examine which inputs drove predictions. Reported OA, RA and OP AUCs were 0.914, 0.841 and 0.954 in the temporal test set and 0.892, 0.805 and 0.915 in the Chinese cohorts. The article reports using both test sets to select hyperparameters and separately describes refitting before evaluation in its fusion protocol. Three Extra Trees scores and a 768-dimensional Bio_ClinicalBERT representation feed a 771-dimensional linear classifier. The architecture uses out-of-fold structured scores and curated, redacted symptom text. In a separate comparison using hospitalization records from 126 patients, the authors report 9.176% higher accuracy than blood-data-only models. The underlying counts and gain calculation are referred to Table S11. The platform also provides patient screening outputs, physician model training and SHAP, and post-screening information.

## Study Design & Model Development

### Study profile

- Study Design: Supervised model development using existing survey and hospital records, with temporal and multicenter structured evaluations, a separate symptom-text fusion comparison, and software workflow descriptions.
- Evidence Type: Reported classification/ROC estimates, model-selection and feature-attribution analyses, multimodal architecture and software-workflow descriptions.
- Population: NHANES records from 1999–2018, data from 4,436 patients across four Chinese clinical centers, and a separately described fusion comparison using Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital records from 126 patients.
- Primary Endpoint: OA/RA/OP classification or risk-screening outputs and reported discrimination/accuracy evaluations.
- Secondary Endpoint: Feature attributions, ablation observations, symptom-text fusion and platform workflow/recommendation functions.
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: Routine laboratory/blood-test measurements, Demographic variables, Curated patient-reported symptom narratives

### Model development

- Model Name: Luma AI
- Structured Branch: Disease-specific Extra Trees classifiers producing P(OA), P(RA) and P(OP); TabNet-based predictor screening and Bayesian hyperparameter comparisons are reported.
- Text Branch: Bio_ClinicalBERT as a clinical text encoder with LoRA adapters; fixed 768-dimensional final [CLS] representations after validation-checkpoint selection.
- Fusion Head: Concatenated 771-dimensional representation; linear softmax classifier trained with cross-entropy to output OA, RA, OP and other-category probabilities.
- Reported Training Controls: Inner fivefold out-of-fold structured scores, redacted narratives, a validation-selected text checkpoint and fixed branches during fusion-head training. The article reports patient-level separation across training, validation and testing.
- Evaluation Scope: Temporal and Chinese-cohort structured-model estimates, plus a separate comparison using hospital records from 126 patients. Results 2.2 used both structured test sets in model configuration selection.
- Intended Use: Risk stratification, self-screening support and clinical follow-up.

## What This Study Adds

- Provides a concrete fusion example: three disease-specific structured probabilities plus a clinical text-encoder representation, with explicitly described orchestration and training safeguards.
- Provides structured-model estimates from temporal and multicenter settings and reports a separate hospital comparison with symptom text.
- Connects model outputs with SHAP and patient/physician workflow tools, creating an applied context for studying interpretable, accessible-input screening support.

- Supporting Information S1 was unavailable for this summary. Tables S1–S2 are cited for cohort and predictor detail, S11 for the hospital accuracy comparison, S12–S13 for dietary synthesis, S15 for ablation and S20–S21 for complete fusion and code mapping.
- SHAP and ablation characterize model reliance. Prospective patient benefit, cost-effectiveness and effects of dietary interventions require separate evaluation.
