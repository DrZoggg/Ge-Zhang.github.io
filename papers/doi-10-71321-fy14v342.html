Research Question

Can circadian-related tumor expression distinguish LUAD molecular clusters with cross-cohort patterns and different retrospective biological and outcome associations?

Author Evidence Summary

The study applies NMF to 2091 reported CRD-related gene entries in TCGA-LUAD and assesses template-based cluster assignment in six named GEO cohorts. Their seven input counts sum to 2090, without participant-level deduplication. C2 generally shows more favorable survival and lipid-metabolic/circadian-related enrichment; C1 shows proliferation and genomic-alteration features, while C3 combines inferred infiltration with suppressive and heterogeneous profiles. Checkpoint expression, TIS, SubMap and cell-line-derived drug predictions generate hypotheses rather than measured subtype-directed treatment benefit. The abstract separately reports 79 immunotherapy records whose allocation remains unresolved. A 151-signature comparison reports TCGA-internal C-index values, with incomplete model attribution and no external performance claim. This supports retrospective molecular classification and association, not direct clock measurement, causal mechanisms or prospective clinical utility. The scientific PDF and retained site bibliographic identity are described separately in Provenance.

Study Design & Analytical Framework

Study profile

Study Design
Retrospective public bulk-transcriptomic classification, external NTP assignment and outcome/annotation analysis, with separate computational treatment-response hypotheses
Evidence Type
Human tumor-expression associations and cross-cohort assessment; genomic/immune annotations and computational drug/response prediction
Population
Public LUAD tumor-expression cohorts; separately reported immunotherapy reference records with unresolved composition. No prospective subtype-guided trial or new single-cell experiment.
Primary Endpoint
Expression-defined C1/C2/C3 cluster structure and cross-cohort template assignment
Secondary Endpoint
Retrospective survival, pathway, genomic and inferred immune differences; computational treatment hypotheses and within-TCGA signature comparisons
External dataset evaluation
Yes

Data modalities

  • Bulk tumor transcriptomics
  • Retrospective survival and clinical annotations
  • Somatic mutation and GISTIC copy-number annotations
  • GSVA/GSEA pathway scores
  • Bulk-expression immune inference
  • TIS and SubMap expression similarity
  • Cell-line-based drug-response prediction

What This Study Adds

  • Three circadian-related LUAD expression clusters with external template assessment.
  • A combined description of retrospective outcome, pathway, genomic and inferred immune differences.
  • Treatment and signature-comparison hypotheses with explicit computed-versus-clinical evidence boundaries.
  • Source-specific counting and locators that retain figure-caption and bibliographic-version discrepancies.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Retrospective LUAD expression clustering and cross-cohort NTP assessment under the reported confidence filtering.
  • Reported outcome, pathway, genomic and inferred immune associations for expression-defined clusters.
  • Computational treatment hypotheses and article-reported within-TCGA signature comparisons, with source gaps retained.

Does Not Establish

  • A prospective subtype-guided clinical trial, treatment benefit or clinical decision rule.
  • Direct measurement of patient circadian phase, sleep behavior or causal circadian mechanisms.
  • Immunotherapy response rates, benefit probabilities or chemotherapy/corticosteroid recommendations for the subtypes.
  • A deduplicated unique-patient total spanning LUAD and immunotherapy datasets.
  • Externally validated best-signature performance, calibration, decision benefit or resolved Chen EG model attribution.
  • Resolved author order, dates or pagination across the conflicting official bibliographic records.

Limitations

  • Retrospective and largely computational evidence; no prospective subtype-guided treatment trial or direct patient circadian-physiology experiment is reported.
  • The seven LUAD input counts sum arithmetically to 2090. Participant-level disjointness is not established, and the separately reported 79 immunotherapy records are not added to claim a unique total.
  • NTP confidence filtering and incomplete clinical information alter analysis denominators. The input cohort counts are not every downstream survival, genomic or prediction denominator.
  • Validation Results say five external cohorts while naming six GEO accessions. Six named cohorts are described; accession differences alone do not prove patient disjointness.
  • The adopted PDF swaps Figure 2/3 captions relative to visual content: PDF p. 6 / print p. 19 is enrichment; PDF p. 8 / print p. 21 is proportions/survival. Locators identify actual page and content.
  • Template-feature heatmap labels conflict with referenced cohort names: one says GSE64865 and another says GSE42127 where its caption says GSE41271. Their identity is not repaired by guess.
  • The 79 immunotherapy reference records lack a verified cohort split, adequately identified second source, tumor-histology breakdown and overlap crosswalk. Data availability cites references 60/61 absent from the supplied list ending at 52.
  • TIS, checkpoint expression and SubMap are indirect predictions or similarity analyses, not actual response rates, benefit probabilities or a recommendation to give immunotherapy to C3 patients.
  • Drug text/axis IC50 versus estimated-AUC and prednisone versus prednisolone discrepancies are retained. Predicted sensitivity does not establish clinical chemotherapy or corticosteroid benefit.
  • The 151-signature comparison ranks recalculated candidates within TCGA. The reported best C-index values do not establish external validation, calibration, decision benefit or resolved Chen EG model attribution.
  • Supplementary legends mention S3/S4 silhouette and survival analyses, but the supplied official ZIP contains only S1/S2 images. Their unavailable adjusted hazard ratios, confidence intervals and independent prognostic claims are not supplied.
  • Table S1 contains 2091 listed entries including apparent spreadsheet date-conversion tokens. They are not corrected or used to assert an independently reproduced clean gene signature.
  • IDENTITY_REVIEW_REQUIRED: scientific synthesis adopts the official-linked 16-author PDF, 2025-06-30 and print pages 14-28. Current 14-author site bibliographic metadata is preserved; authorship/order, publisher dates and e137 versus print pagination remain unresolved.

Q&A

Which cohorts contribute the reported LUAD input count?

The counts are TCGA-LUAD 497, GSE72094 442, GSE68465 462, GSE50081 127, GSE42127 133, GSE41271 183 and GSE31210 246. Their arithmetic sum is 2090, without participant-level deduplication. The separate 79 immunotherapy records are not added.

Evidence: KF1, KF6

How were the subtypes developed and assessed across cohorts?

NMF selected three TCGA clusters from the reported 2091 CRD-related gene entries. NTP assessed template assignment in the six named GEO cohorts with confidence filtering. Results say five cohorts while listing six; this remains retrospective expression assessment.

Evidence: KF1

What survival pattern was reported for C1, C2 and C3?

C2 generally had more favorable survival and C1 generally less favorable survival. Cohort-specific plots do not establish identical ranking everywhere. The TCGA log-rank P of 0.0062 is an association result, not a treatment effect or validated decision rule.

Evidence: KF2

Do circadian-related expression differences directly measure a patient clock?

No direct circadian-phase, sleep or metabolic-flux measurement is reported. Enrichment associates C1 with proliferation, C2 with lipid-metabolic and circadian sleep-wake gene sets, and C3 with immune programs; it does not establish circadian causation.

Evidence: KF3

What genomic features were associated with the clusters?

TCGA annotation analyses associated C1 with greater mutation and copy-number-alteration burdens. These comparisons and inferred stemness scores do not show that clock disruption caused TP53 changes, genomic instability or immune escape.

Evidence: KF4

Why can C3 show high infiltration and a suppressive immune profile?

Expression-based analyses report higher infiltration together with lower IPS, higher S-score and higher inferred heterogeneity for C3. Cell abundance and function are different questions; bulk scores do not directly prove suppressive cellular mechanisms.

Evidence: KF5

Did the study establish immunotherapy benefit for C3 patients?

Checkpoint expression, TIS and SubMap similarity support a response hypothesis. The separately reported 79 reference records are incompletely resolved by cohort and diagnosis, and no prospective subtype-guided response or benefit evaluation is reported.

Evidence: KF6

What can be concluded from predicted drug-sensitivity differences?

Computed displays suggest subtype-associated drug-response metrics. Their axes label estimated AUC while text says IC50, and steroid naming differs between text and supplement. They do not justify clinical chemotherapy or corticosteroid recommendations.

Evidence: KF7

How should the 151-signature C-index comparison be interpreted?

The article reports TCGA-internal C-index values 0.741 for C1 and 0.698 for C2 under Chen EG (2017), and 0.796 for C3 under Ma B (2020). These are retrospective candidate-selection results, with unresolved exact Chen-model attribution and no established external performance or clinical utility.

Evidence: KF8
