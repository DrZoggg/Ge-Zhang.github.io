## Evidence Base

**Recording results from single-patient trials**

This Science Letter argues that public trial registries and preclinical publications should retain a current, linked account of directly related human protocols and major outcomes in individualized genetic therapies. It proposes reporting practices, rather than presenting a new clinical trial or establishing treatment causality.

## Research Question

How should public records preserve major human outcomes when individualized genetic therapy research involves a single patient?

## Author Evidence Summary

The Letter responds to previously reported individualized base-editing research and a public registry entry that, according to the Letter, remained out of date after the reported participant death. The authors argue that an individual outcome may constitute the available human evidence in a single-patient study, making a current public record important to families, clinicians, ethics committees and other researchers. They distinguish confidential or regulatory safety reporting from public trial registries and journal publications. Their proposals include disclosure of directly related human protocols in preclinical submissions, updating those disclosures before acceptance, recording treatment-emergent deaths and life-threatening events separately from causal assessment, linking preclinical papers with trial records, and adding dated notices when major human outcomes occur after publication. These are the Letter's reporting and governance arguments, not a newly conducted experiment, demonstrated clinical benefit or determination of responsibility.

## Q&A

### What public-record problem does this Science Letter address?

The authors describe a gap between a previously reported human outcome and the registry or preclinical publication, and argue that directly related human protocols and major outcomes should remain visible in current public records. The case is contextual evidence from other sources, not a new study in this Letter.

Evidence: KF1

### Why can an outdated registry matter in single-patient research?

An individual outcome may be the available human evidence. The Letter argues that families, clinicians, ethics committees and other researchers can rely on the public entry when judging continued testing and emerging risks.

Evidence: KF2

### Does reporting to regulators ensure that the public record is updated?

The Letter distinguishes safety reports, trial registries and journal articles because their purposes and update schedules differ. Clinical information reaching an oversight body can still be absent from the public account of translation.

Evidence: KF3

### Which changes do the authors propose for registries and preclinical papers?

They propose disclosure and updating of directly related human protocols before preclinical manuscript acceptance, reporting of treatment-emergent deaths and life-threatening events separately from causal assessment, links between preclinical papers and trial records, and dated notices for major later human outcomes.

Evidence: KF4

### Does this Letter establish treatment causality or assign responsibility?

No. Its conclusion explicitly separates documenting subsequent human experience from assigning blame or settling causality. Its proposals concern reporting and governance, with no new clinical experiment or quantified treatment effect.

Evidence: KF5
