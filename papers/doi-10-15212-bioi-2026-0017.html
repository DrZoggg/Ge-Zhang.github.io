Official Abstract

Background

Insulin resistance (IR) and chronic kidney disease (CKD) are independent risk factors for cardiovascular disease (CVD). The triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index is recognized as a convenient marker for IR, while the estimated glomerular filtration rate based on both creatinine and cystatin C (eGFRcr-cys) is commonly used to evaluate kidney function. Therefore, the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio is introduced with the aim of obtaining a more effective predictor for CVD risk.

Method

This prospective cohort analysis included 261,865 UK Biobank participants with available information. Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate the associations of TyG, eGFRcr-cys, and the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio with incident CVD. Restricted cubic splines for model fitting with three knots placed at the 10th, 50th, 90th percentiles were used to determine the non-linear relationship between the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio and CVD. Furthermore, subgroup and sensitivity analyses were performed to illustrate the disparate associations across diverse groups and to reinforce the conclusions drawn, respectively. An independent single-center clinical cohort was assembled from the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University for external validation by enrolling consecutive patients between 2018 and 2020 with baseline TyG index and eGFRcr-cys measurements and up to 4 years of follow-up. External validation with major adverse cardiovascular events was further performed as the outcome and observed consistent dose–response associations and robust risk stratification for the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio.

Result

Following extensive covariate adjustment, the hazard ratios [HRs] (95% confidence intervals [CIs]) for total CVD across increasing quartiles of the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio, with the lowest quartile (Q1) as the reference, were 1.15 (1.10–1.21) for Q2 versus Q1, 1.17 (1.12–1.23) for Q3 versus Q1, and 1.24 (1.18–1.30) for Q4 versus Q1, respectively, for the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio. In addition, each standard deviation increase in the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio was associated with a higher risk of CVD, coronary heart disease, and stroke, corresponding to estimated increases of 249% [HR, 3.49; 95% CI, 2.94–4.15], 250% [HR, 3.50; 95% CI, 2.92–4.20], and 165% [HR, 2.65; 95% CI, 1.80–3.90], respectively, in the fully adjusted models. The non-linear relationship between the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio and CVD (P-value for the overall model < 0.001 and P-value for the non-linear model < 0.001) indicated that the CVD risk increased as the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio increased. Subgroup analysis results suggested that these associations were more pronounced in White individuals and females.

Conclusion

As the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio increased, the risk of CVD also increased with a higher likelihood in females and White individuals.

Research Question

Is an index combining the triglyceride–glucose marker with creatinine–cystatin-C-based estimated renal function associated with incident cardiovascular events, and is the association reproduced in an independent clinical cohort?

Author Evidence Summary

The study analyzed baseline metabolic and renal markers in UK Biobank and examined an independent hospital cohort. Higher ratio quartiles were associated with overall CVD and CHD; the fully adjusted highest-versus-lowest-quartile stroke association was not statistically significant. The external complete-case analysis reproduced a ratio–MACE association but was small and imprecise. The findings support further evaluation of a complementary cardiometabolic–renal marker. They do not establish causal mechanisms, a universal treatment threshold, improved decisions, or incremental prediction beyond established risk scores.

Evidence Scale

TyG:eGFRcr-cys and cardiovascular events: prospective UK Biobank association with independent clinical replication

A higher TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio was associated with incident CVD, particularly CHD, in a prospective UK Biobank analysis. A smaller independent clinical cohort supported an association with MACE. The study did not evaluate whether the ratio improves discrimination or reclassification beyond established cardiovascular risk factors or scores.

Study Design & Analytical Framework

Study profile

Study Design
Prospective observational UK Biobank analysis with Cox models, restricted cubic splines, subgroup/sensitivity analyses, independent single-center clinical association replication, and exploratory network analysis across ratio quartiles.
Evidence Type
Longitudinal observational associations and independent clinical association replication
Population
UK Biobank adults without baseline CVD; a separately assembled Zhengzhou University vascular-surgery clinical cohort with complete cases used for the fully adjusted external analysis.
Primary Endpoint
Incident fatal or nonfatal CHD or stroke in the primary cohort; MACE defined as fatal or nonfatal CHD or stroke in the external cohort.
Secondary Endpoint
CHD and stroke separately; dose–response patterns and exploratory network instability across exposure-defined strata.
External dataset evaluation
Yes

Data modalities

  • Baseline blood triglycerides and glucose
  • Creatinine and cystatin-C-based estimated GFR
  • Demographic, lifestyle, medication, and clinical covariates
  • Hospital and death-registry outcome codes
  • External clinical cohort follow-up

What This Study Adds

  • Examines a combined metabolic–renal marker rather than treating its metabolic and renal components as unrelated domains.
  • Separates prospective primary-cohort associations from an independent, smaller hospital-cohort analysis.
  • Reports outcome-specific associations and explicitly leaves incremental predictive value beyond established risk scores unresolved.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Prospective observational associations between the baseline ratio and incident CVD/CHD in the analyzed UK Biobank population.
  • Independent association replication for MACE in a small, complete-case hospital cohort.
  • Exploratory network analysis and further research into integrated cardiometabolic–renal markers.

Does Not Establish

  • A causal effect of the ratio on cardiovascular disease or a confirmed biological mechanism.
  • A statistically significant highest-versus-lowest-quartile stroke association in fully adjusted Model 3.
  • An independently calibrated individual-risk calculator, improved C-statistic/NRI/IDI, or superiority to established risk scores.
  • A universal clinical cut-off, treatment efficacy, or benefit from targeting the ratio.
  • That exposure-quartile network differences demonstrate within-person disease transitions.

Limitations

  • The observational design cannot establish causation and residual confounding remains possible.
  • UK Biobank selection and demographic composition limit generalizability.
  • The external cohort was from one clinical center; only a complete-case subset entered the fully adjusted analysis, limiting precision and representativeness.
  • The source reports different results for continuous, quartile, and spline contrasts; these should not be collapsed into one uniform association.
  • Incremental discrimination, calibration, reclassification, and clinical decision benefit beyond established risk scores were not established.
  • Exploratory network comparisons between exposure quartiles cannot demonstrate longitudinal within-person phase transitions.

Q&A

How many participants contributed to the analyses?

The UK Biobank analytic cohort contained 261,865 participants. The external hospital cohort initially contained 1,798 participants, but the fully adjusted external analysis used 328 complete cases, including 88 MACE outcomes. These populations and subsets should not be added into a synthetic study total.

Evidence: KF1

What does the TyG:eGFRcr-cys ratio combine?

It divides the triglyceride–glucose index by estimated GFR derived using both creatinine and cystatin C. It is intended to summarize metabolic and renal risk-marker information, rather than directly measure a causal process.

Evidence: KF1, KF2

What was the main prospective CVD association?

In fully adjusted Model 3, the highest ratio quartile had a CVD HR of 1.24 compared with the lowest quartile, with a 95% CI of 1.18–1.30. This is an observational hazard ratio.

Evidence: KF2

Were all cardiovascular outcomes significantly associated in the quartile comparison?

No. The highest-versus-lowest-quartile CHD HR was 1.30, while the stroke HR was 1.05 with a 95% CI of 0.96–1.16 and P=0.29. Outcome and exposure-contrast details must be preserved.

Evidence: KF3

What does external validation mean here?

It means that the ratio–MACE association was examined in an independent hospital cohort. The fully adjusted complete-case analysis was small and the confidence interval was wide; this does not establish a calibrated prediction model or improved clinical decision-making.

Evidence: KF4

Does the network analysis establish a pre-disease transition?

No. The analysis compared exposure-defined groups and was exploratory. It does not show that an individual crosses a validated early-warning threshold over time.

Evidence: KF5

Can the ratio replace established cardiovascular risk scores?

The article states that incremental discrimination and reclassification beyond established risk factors or scores were not evaluated. The ratio remains a candidate complementary marker requiring additional evaluation.

Evidence: KF2, KF4
