## Evidence Scale

**ABCA1 acts as a protective modulator in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis**

An integrative ALS study combined blood transcriptomic modeling, external dataset discrimination, cis-eQTL Mendelian randomization and a small serum ELISA series. A nine-gene expression classifier showed an external AUC of 0.75, while the ABCA1 genetic-instrument estimate indicated an inverse association with ALS risk. Serum ABCA1 was higher in 15 ALS patients versus 15 controls. These layers do not establish that raising ABCA1 is therapeutically protective.

| Evidence | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Signature | 9 genes selected by intersection of three feature-selection algorithms |
| External classifier dataset | GSE112680 independent external expression dataset |
| Serum clinical sample | 15 ALS cases and 15 controls, measured using ELISA |
| MR genetic instruments | 11 independent ABCA1-associated cis-eQTL SNPs in primary analysis |
| Mouse expression timepoints | Postnatal days 30, 70, 100 and 120 (public data; not a new mouse intervention) |

### Counting note

Transcriptomic cohort sizes, GWAS case-control summaries, public tissue/mouse series and 30 serum participants refer to different data resources. No synthetic total n or intervention trial is claimed.

## Research Question

What complementary evidence links ABCA1 with ALS blood transcription, diagnostic discrimination, genetically predicted disease susceptibility and protein measurements, and where do the methods stop short of causal therapeutic proof?

## Author Evidence Summary

The researchers integrated publicly available ALS blood and tissue expression resources, feature selection and random forest classification, two-sample Mendelian randomization, and an independent small serum ELISA group. Results are separated by data modality; the supplementary data contradict a published sentence claiming significant mouse-spinal-cord upregulation as early as postnatal day 70.

## Study Design & Model Development

### Study profile

- Study Design: Multi-dataset retrospective transcriptomic discovery/validation with gene-expression classifier development, cis-eQTL Mendelian randomization and a small in-house cross-sectional serum cohort.
- Evidence Type: Blood/tissue expression associations, retrospective classification performance, genetically instrumented association and laboratory serum comparison.
- Population: Patients with ALS and controls from public blood expression series; an independently sampled 15 ALS/15 control serum group; human postmortem tissues and a public SOD1-G93A mouse tissue series.
- Primary Endpoint: Retrospective ALS-versus-control blood expression classification and ABCA1 association with genetically instrumented ALS susceptibility.
- Secondary Endpoint: Serum ABCA1 difference, metabolic/functional correlations and tissue- or time-dependent expression differences.
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: Whole-blood microarray/transcriptomic datasets, Random forest and LASSO classifier discrimination, cis-eQTL and ALS GWAS summary statistics, Serum ELISA with clinical covariates, Human postmortem and publicly available SOD1-G93A tissue expression

### Model development

- Model Name: Nine-gene ALS blood expression classifier
- Model Family: Random forest; LASSO comparator
- Evaluation: GSE112676 training/internal testing and independent GSE112680 external expression cohort
- Performance Scope: RF AUC 1.00/0.91/0.75 (train/internal/external), not a clinical diagnostic utility trial
- Clinical Boundary: Not an established ALS diagnostic assay or an ABCA1-targeted therapy.

## What This Study Adds

- Aligns several observational/genetic/serum modalities around ABCA1 while showing that they interrogate different constructs.
- Documents the external classifier performance drop and a source-traced inconsistency in the published mouse day-70 significance statement.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- A reproducible blood expression-model signal with an external dataset AUC of 0.75 for the reported model.
- An instrument-based inverse ABCA1-expression/ALS-risk association under MR assumptions.
- An ALS-versus-control serum ABCA1 difference in the reported small cohort.
- Cross-compartment variation in human and public mouse expression resources.

### Does Not Establish

- That increasing ABCA1 expression or administering an ABCA1-targeted treatment prevents ALS.
- A fully validated clinical ALS diagnostic test, cut-off or demonstrated impact on patient outcomes.
- A statistically significant day-70 mouse spinal-cord increase, given source P=0.414.
- That ssGSEA score associations represent directly enumerated neutrophils or NK cells.
- A significant relationship between serum ABCA1 and ALSFRS-R functional score based on the published nonsignificant result.

### Limitations

- The external AUC is lower than the training/internal AUC, and cohort platform differences constrain diagnostic transportability.
- MR inferences rely on genetic instruments and cannot replace direct target perturbation and clinical outcome trials.
- The serum series contains only 15 patients and 15 controls, and multiple exploratory correlates require caution.
- The published mouse day-70 phrase disagrees with raw/adjusted statistics in Supplementary Table 14; day 70 must not be called statistically significant.
- Genetic susceptibility, whole-blood RNA, circulating protein and postmortem tissue are not interchangeable measures.

## Q&A

### How did the nine-gene ALS model perform externally?

The random forest signature reported AUC 1.00 in training, 0.91 internally, and 0.75 in independent GSE112680, reflecting lower transport performance; no prospective clinical impact test was performed.

Evidence: KF1

### Does MR prove ABCA1 upregulation treats ALS?

No. IVW MR estimated an inverse association with ALS susceptibility (OR 0.93; 95% CI 0.87–0.99) under genetic instrument assumptions; that is not a drug intervention or demonstrated treatment benefit.

Evidence: KF2

### How many people were in the serum cohort?

Fifteen ALS cases and fifteen controls (30 participants). The reported group comparison had P=0.006 but is too small to establish clinical diagnostic validity.

Evidence: KF3

### Was serum ABCA1 associated with functional disability?

The reported ALSFRS-R total correlation r=-0.190, P=0.497 was not statistically significant; the evidence summary does not claim functional prediction.

Evidence: KF3

### Is ABCA1 significantly upregulated in mouse spinal cord from day 70?

No. The supplied original Supplementary Table 14 reports mouse day-70 P=0.414 (adjusted P approximately 1). Stronger reported evidence appears on days 100 and 120; the published day-70 significant-onward phrase is inconsistent with its numbers.

Evidence: KF4

### Are neutrophil and NK findings direct cell counts?

No. The reported associations come from ssGSEA immune-cell transcriptomic signatures and should not be interpreted as flow-cytometric cell-number measurements.

Evidence: KF5
