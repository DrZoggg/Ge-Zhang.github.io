## Research Question

How are publications and bibliometric research topics distributed across cancer prevention, screening, diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation in a defined Web of Science corpus?

## Author Evidence Summary

The study retrieved English-language Web of Science Core Collection literature using title terms for cancer and five cancer-management areas. The eligible publication interval was 1 March 2014 to 1 March 2024, with 25 December 2024 stated as the database access date. After exclusions and deduplication, 17,293 documents were analyzed. The article reports publication, keyword, collaboration and co-citation analyses, using bibliometric tools to describe the literature. Treatment accounts for the largest reported topic category, while Table 9 provides frequencies for terms such as targeted therapy, immunotherapy and artificial intelligence. These are descriptive features of the retrieved documents. The study does not validate a treatment, quantify clinical benefit or establish that popular research topics are more reliable. This evidence page omits the inconsistent country-collaboration ranking and does not reproduce or independently interpret network-map images.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Descriptive bibliometric corpus analysis
- Evidence Type: Original analysis of bibliographic records and text-encoded frequency tables
- Corpus: English-language Web of Science Core Collection documents selected by a title-based oncology query; the unit of analysis is a document, not a patient.
- Primary analysis: Distribution of publications across cancer-management topic categories.
- Additional analysis: Descriptive keyword co-occurrence, co-citation, collaboration and timeline analyses; no clinical endpoint.
- Independent corpus evaluation: No
- Data modalities: Bibliographic metadata: titles, authors, affiliations, sources and publication dates, Keywords, abstracts and citation relationships, Descriptive publication and keyword frequency tables

## What This Study Adds

- A corpus-specific map of how published oncology literature is distributed across five cancer-management topics.
- A descriptive bibliometric entry point for locating research themes; primary clinical studies remain necessary for treatment-effect claims.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Description of the defined title-search corpus and its reported document counts.
- Attribution of topic allocation and selected keyword frequencies to this bibliometric study.
- Discussion of research attention within the specified database, language and date restrictions.

### Does Not Establish

- Clinical effectiveness, comparative treatment benefit or patient safety.
- Causal mechanisms, incidence or disease burden from publication frequencies.
- An exhaustive global oncology evidence inventory or a quality ranking of therapies, institutions or researchers.
- Independent validation of clinical results summarized from the article's cited literature.

### Limitations

- The corpus is restricted to English-language records in Web of Science Core Collection and a title-based query; it is not an exhaustive inventory of oncology research.
- Publication eligibility ends on 1 March 2024, while the Methods give 25 December 2024 as the database access date. The partial 2024 interval cannot be compared with complete years as an annual trend.
- The article states that records were deduplicated but does not provide a complete reproducible deduplication rule or a deposited corpus in its Availability of Data and Materials statement; this page does not independently regenerate the search.
- Bibliometric frequency, co-occurrence and co-citation are descriptive signals of the retrieved literature, not measures of therapeutic efficacy, causal mechanism or evidence quality.
- The Results description of the strongest country collaboration differs from the ranking implied by Table 2 frequencies. This page excludes that ranking rather than choosing a preferred account.
- Selected findings rely on formal body text and text-encoded tables. Network-map geometry and figure-derived effect estimates are not independently reinterpreted here.
