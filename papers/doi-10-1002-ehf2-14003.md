## Evidence Scale

**Shared COVID-19 and heart-failure transcriptomic signatures and inflammatory network hubs**

A cross-disease transcriptomic systems-biology analysis of independently profiled COVID-19 and heart-failure datasets, identifying 12 shared genes and prioritizing inflammatory network hubs for further study.

### Counting note

Dataset-level counts represent different biological and analytical units and are not summed into a synthetic participant total. GSE147507 contains multiple experimental systems; its human lung-biopsy component consists of two independent uninfected donors and technical replicates from one deceased COVID-19 donor. GSE26887 formally contains 19 HF and five control cardiac-tissue samples, but the precise grouping used for the article-reported 253 HF DEGs is not sufficiently specified in the accessible main text, so 24 is not asserted as the primary analytical contrast n. GSE164805 contains 10 COVID-19 and five healthy PBMC samples. GSE9128/GDS3115 contains 11 pooled PBMC microarray samples—eight HF pools and three control pools—derived from an original study population of 24 HF and 12 control individuals. Participants, pooled samples, technical replicates, and tissue samples are not interchangeable.

## Research Question

Which transcriptional signatures, inflammatory pathways, network hubs, and predicted regulatory or drug-target relationships are shared between independently profiled COVID-19 and heart-failure datasets, and do selected hub genes show consistent disease-associated expression patterns in external COVID-19 and HF datasets?

## Author Evidence Summary

This cross-disease multi-dataset transcriptomic systems-biology study compared independently profiled COVID-19 and heart-failure (HF) datasets. At |log2FC| > 1 and adjusted P < 0.05, the authors reported 102 COVID-19 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), 253 HF DEGs, and 12 shared genes. Enrichment analyses highlighted inflammatory and innate-immune processes, including neutrophil and monocyte chemotaxis, Toll-like receptor, NF-κB, chemokine, interleukin-17, and cytokine-related signaling. Protein-interaction topology prioritized ten hub genes, with TLR4, TLR2, IL10, and STAT3 recurring across seven cytoHubba algorithms. External public PBMC expression datasets supported prioritization of TLR4, TLR2, STAT3, IL1B, and CXCL8; the reported 96.0%, 93.2%, and 89.9% are first-two-component PCA explained-variance values, not diagnostic performance. Database-derived TF/miRNA networks highlighted FOXC1, STAT3, NFKB1, miR-181, miR-520, and miR-143, while DSigDB drug-signature enrichment nominated muramyl dipeptide and glutathione as hypothesis-generating candidates. The study did not enroll a dedicated cohort with simultaneous COVID-19 and HF, establish germline genetic correlation or causality, prospectively validate a clinical signature, experimentally validate the predicted regulatory edges, directly measure gut microbiome or circulating LPS, perform proteomics, or demonstrate treatment efficacy for the candidate compounds.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Cross-disease analysis of independent public COVID-19 and HF transcriptomic datasets, followed by enrichment, protein-interaction and regulatory-network analyses, and external expression-dataset evaluation
- Evidence Type: Public gene-expression differential-expression analysis, pathway enrichment, PPI topology, external PBMC expression evaluation, and computational TF/miRNA and drug-signature prioritization
- Population: Separate human COVID-19 lung-tissue and PBMC datasets and HF cardiac-tissue and pooled PBMC datasets; no dedicated cohort of patients simultaneously affected by COVID-19 and HF
- Primary Endpoint: Shared disease-associated expression signatures between independently profiled COVID-19 and HF datasets
- Secondary Endpoint: Inflammatory pathway and network-hub prioritization, external expression-pattern evaluation, and hypothesis-generating regulatory and compound candidates
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: lung-biopsy transcriptomics, left-ventricular cardiac-tissue transcriptomics, PBMC gene-expression microarrays, including pooled samples, functional and pathway enrichment, protein-protein-interaction network analysis, TF/miRNA network prediction, drug-signature enrichment

## What This Study Adds

- A hypothesis-generating cross-disease molecular map connecting independently observed COVID-19 and HF expression responses, without claiming a simultaneous-comorbidity cohort.
- Convergence across DEG overlap, inflammatory enrichment, PPI topology, and external expression datasets toward TLR4, TLR2, STAT3, IL1B and CXCL8.
- Computational TF/miRNA and drug-signature candidates for future experimental work, not validated mechanisms or therapies.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Shared transcriptional signals across independently profiled COVID-19 and HF datasets.
- Inflammatory and innate-immune pathway enrichment and network prioritization of TLR-associated genes.
- External public expression-dataset support for selected hub genes, with tissue-dependent IL1B interpretation.
- Computational TF/miRNA and candidate-compound hypothesis generation.
- Hypothesis generation around gut-heart and LPS biology, without direct measurement of microbiome or LPS.

### Does Not Establish

- Germline genetic correlation, inherited susceptibility, GWAS evidence, Mendelian randomization, or genetic causality.
- A causal SARS-CoV-2-to-HF mechanism or findings from a dedicated cohort simultaneously affected by COVID-19 and HF.
- Prospective clinical validation, diagnostic accuracy, or prognostic utility of a five-gene panel.
- Experimentally demonstrated TF/miRNA regulation or direct measurement of gut microbiome dysbiosis, intestinal permeability, or circulating LPS.
- Proteomic evidence or therapeutic efficacy of glutathione, muramyl dipeptide, or another intervention.

### Limitations

- Human evidence was assembled retrospectively from heterogeneous public expression datasets; no dedicated simultaneous COVID-19 and HF cohort or prospective clinical validation was reported.
- Lung tissue, cardiac tissue, and PBMC samples and their experimental platforms are not directly interchangeable.
- The GSE147507 human COVID-19 lung component had one deceased COVID-19 biological donor with technical replicates and two independent uninfected donors; total GEO Series entries are not a participant count.
- GSE9128/GDS3115 evaluated 11 pooled PBMC microarray samples rather than 11 independent participants, although the original population comprised 24 HF and 12 control individuals.
- The exact GSE26887 grouping that generated the article-reported 253 HF DEGs is not sufficiently explicit in the accessible main article; the formal Series contains 19 HF and five control cardiac-tissue samples.
- PPI, TF/miRNA and drug-signature findings are computational or database-derived and lack direct causal perturbation validation.
- Gut-microbiome composition, intestinal permeability and circulating LPS were not directly measured; gut-heart interpretation remains a hypothesis.
- No proteomics, candidate-compound treatment experiment, or demonstrated therapeutic efficacy for glutathione or muramyl dipeptide was reported.

## Q&A

### Which datasets identified the shared COVID-19 and HF genes?

The comparison used independent COVID-19 and HF transcriptomic datasets, principally GSE147507 for the human COVID-19 lung component and GSE26887 for HF left-ventricular tissue. These were separate disease datasets, not samples from a simultaneous-comorbidity cohort.

Evidence: KF1

### What were the 12 shared COVID-19 and HF genes?

S100A8, ABCB1, S100A11, PTGS2 (called COX2 in the article), LY96, XCL1, RORA, CCL11, CCL4, S100A12, BCL2 and AQP9 were shared at |log2FC| > 1 and adjusted P < 0.05 across the separate expression analyses.

Evidence: KF1

### Which genes were prioritized as network hubs?

The PPI analysis prioritized TLR4, TLR2, CXCL8, IL10, STAT3, IL1B, TLR1, TP53, CCL20 and CXCL10; seven cytoHubba algorithms converged on TLR4, TLR2, IL10 and STAT3. Network centrality is not proof of causal disease drivers.

Evidence: KF3

### Did the study analyze patients with both COVID-19 and HF, and what does ‘genetic links’ mean?

No dedicated cohort simultaneously affected by COVID-19 and HF was analyzed. In this paper, ‘genetic links’ refers to shared transcriptomic, gene-expression, molecular and predicted regulatory signatures across independent datasets, not germline genetic correlation, GWAS, Mendelian randomization or genetic causality.

Evidence: KF1, KF3, KF5

### Was the five-gene signature clinically validated, and do 96.0% and 93.2% mean diagnostic accuracy?

No prospective clinical validation or diagnostic-accuracy study was reported. The 96.0% COVID-19 and 93.2% HF values, alongside 89.9% for merged data, are variance explained by the first two PCA components in the five-gene analysis; they are not AUC, accuracy, sensitivity or specificity.

Evidence: KF4

### Did the study prove a gut-microbiome or circulating-LPS mechanism?

No. Gut-heart and LPS discussion is hypothesis-generating; stool microbiome composition, intestinal permeability and circulating LPS were not directly measured in this analysis.

Evidence: KF2, KF5

### Did glutathione or muramyl dipeptide treat COVID-19 or heart failure in this study?

No treatment efficacy was tested or demonstrated. Glutathione and muramyl dipeptide were DSigDB-prioritized in-silico drug-signature candidates based on five genes, requiring experimental and clinical evaluation before any therapeutic claim.

Evidence: KF6

### Why does Figure 1 mention GSE26687, and which HF dataset was used?

GSE26687 in the Figure 1 caption appears to be an accession typo. The Methods and Results, together with the formal GEO identity, identify GSE26887 as the HF cardiac-tissue dataset; the apparent typo is recorded for provenance and is not treated as a second dataset.

Evidence: KF1
