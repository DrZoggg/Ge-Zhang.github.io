Official Abstract

Ischaemic cardiomyopathy (IC) predominantly arises from prolonged deprivation of oxygen in the coronary arteries, resulting in compromised cardiac contractility or relaxation. This study investigates the role of disulfidptosis‐associated genes (DiGs) in IC. Through the analysis of datasets GSE5406 and GSE57338, we explored the association between DiGs and immune characteristics to identify crucial genes contributing to IC development. The support vector machine model emerged as the most effective, identifying key genes such as MYH9, NUBPL, MYL6, MYH10 and NCKAP1. Validation with independent datasets GSE57345, GSE48166 and single‐cell GSE145154 further supported these findings, demonstrating high predictive accuracy. Experimental validation in an IC mouse model, using Western blot, immunohistochemistry and RT‐qPCR, confirmed the altered expression of these core genes in myocardial ischaemic regions. This research not only elucidates the significance of DiGs in IC but also underscores the diagnostic potential of identified core genes.

Research Question

Which disulfidptosis-related expression candidates characterize IC, how do they relate to molecular clusters and cardiac cell types, and which patterns recur in a mouse myocardial-injury model?

Author Evidence Summary

The article combines GSE5406 and GSE57338, reported as 152 control and 203 IC expression samples. It describes 17 differential genes, two clusters among 203 IC profiles, and five prioritized candidates: MYH9, NUBPL, MYL6, MYH10 and NCKAP1. It reports SVM AUC 0.911 and additional dataset AUCs 0.914 and 0.967; these are retained as published evaluations with model-task and sample-independence qualifications in KF3. Single-cell analysis supplies cell-type context across 20 samples. In one-week LAD-ligation mouse experiments, MYH9, MYL6 and NCKAP1 mRNA are lower in both sexes. MYH10 mRNA is higher in males but lower in females; NUBPL is nonsignificant in male mRNA and higher in female mRNA. The Discussion's mirrored-sex description differs from those Results/figures. Male IHC/Western blot show higher MYH10/NUBPL and lower MYH9/MYL6/NCKAP1 proteins. Immune deconvolution, age/MI time-course analysis, metabolic-gene measurements and a predicted ceRNA network provide complementary hypotheses. The contribution is source-located candidate/expression evidence; independent human validation and causal cell-death mechanisms remain separate questions.

Counting note

Sample, patient, cell and animal-assay units are reported separately. The 152 controls/203 IC profiles and Table S1 counts describe the authors' analytical selections; unique patients were not independently audited. GSE57338 is a SubSeries of GSE57345, but accession membership does not establish which profiles were reused in this analysis. MI timepoint profiles and single cells are not added as independent patients. Animal allocation and endpoint-specific n do not resolve animal reuse or biological-versus-technical replicates. Additional-dataset analyses are reported; analytical nonoverlap and frozen-model transfer remain unresolved, so independent clinical validation is not established.

Study Design & Analytical Framework

Study profile

Study Design
Retrospective bulk myocardial-expression analysis with batch correction, candidate screening, clustering, CIBERSORT/GSVA, four models and additional dataset analyses; separate single-cell/MI time-course analyses; sham-versus-LAD-ligation mouse assays at one week.
Evidence Type
Bulk/single-cell transcriptomic association; published model-evaluation summaries; inferred immune/pathway/regulatory networks; mouse qPCR, IHC, Western blot, histology, LDH and echocardiography.
Population
Article-reported human IC/control expression samples and separate MI time-course samples; male/female C57 mice allocated to sham/control or LAD-ligation groups.
Primary Endpoint
Prioritization and expression characterization of disulfidptosis-related candidates in IC.
Secondary Endpoint
Clusters, reported model/nomogram evaluations, immune/cell-type context, age/time associations, predicted ceRNA network and mouse expression/injury readouts.
External dataset evaluation
Yes

Data modalities

  • Bulk myocardial expression
  • Public single-cell RNA sequencing
  • Separate MI time-course expression
  • Inferred immune proportions/pathway activity
  • Mouse myocardial mRNA/protein
  • Mouse injury histology, serum LDH and echocardiography

What This Study Adds

  • Nominates a five-gene IC candidate set and connects bulk expression with cell-type localization and experimental expression measurements.
  • Provides distinct bulk-cluster, model, immune, regulatory-network and mouse-assay evidence units for methods comparison.
  • Makes sex- and modality-specific expression observations available for follow-up design while preserving source discrepancies that affect diagnostic or mechanistic inference.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Source-specific bulk-expression differences, computational clusters and reported model-evaluation results.
  • Single-cell expression context and secondary human age/MI time-course associations.
  • Expression/injury observations in the one-week mouse model, with sex and assay distinguished.

Does Not Establish

  • Independently validated clinical accuracy, prospective prognosis, decision benefit or a ready-to-use patient assay.
  • Nonoverlapping external evaluation merely because an additional GEO accession is named.
  • Causal gene function in IC or direct experimental demonstration/rescue of disulfidptosis from expression changes alone.
  • A sex-invariant MYH10 mRNA response or interchangeable mRNA/protein directions for every candidate.

Limitations

  • Training/validation sample independence is unresolved: the previously verified GEO record identifies GSE57338 as a SubSeries of GSE57345, while the paper does not show a nonoverlapping analytical selection or clearly frozen external evaluation.
  • Internal modeling is described as IC-cluster discrimination, whereas additional ROC analyses are described as IC-versus-control. The model target, feature selection and evaluation pipeline require clarification before performance certification.
  • Results and original figures show opposite male/female MYH10 mRNA directions, while Discussion describes mirrored patterns. Male NUBPL also differs between mRNA and protein readouts.
  • Animal allocation and endpoint-specific n do not establish independent biological replicate counts or which assays share animals. The one-week ligation model is not a longitudinal model of chronic human disease.
  • Immune deconvolution, ceRNA prediction and expression changes do not establish cellular causation or direct disulfidptosis. Prospective clinical performance and utility require separate evidence.

Q&A

Which human datasets and counts underpin bulk discovery?

Methods merge GSE5406 and GSE57338 after batch correction and report 152 control and 203 IC expression samples. Table S1 gives 16/108 control/case samples for GSE5406 and 136/95 for GSE57338, reconciling arithmetically with those counts. They are the paper's analyzed-sample descriptions, not an independently audited unique-patient census.

Evidence: KF1

Which five genes were prioritized, and how did their bulk expression differ?

The paper prioritizes MYH9, NUBPL, MYL6, MYH10 and NCKAP1. In the bulk Results, NUBPL and MYH10 are higher in IC, while MYH9, MYL6 and NCKAP1 are lower. This is an expression association, not proof of a causal cell-death driver.

Evidence: KF1

What model performance is reported, and how independent is the validation?

Figure 4D reports SVM 0.911, RF 0.899, XGB 0.895 and GLM 0.881. Figure 5 gives model AUC 0.914 (95% CI 0.795-0.995) for GSE48166 and 0.967 (95% CI 0.944-0.987) for GSE57345. Internal Results refer to IC-cluster discrimination, while the additional analyses are described as IC-versus-control. The GSE57338/GSE57345 relationship and unspecified analytical nonoverlap/frozen evaluation prevent certifying independent clinical performance.

Evidence: KF3

What do the molecular clusters and immune analyses contribute?

The article divides 203 IC profiles into clusters of 116 and 87. CIBERSORT and GSVA associate them with different inferred immune proportions and pathway activity. These are useful biological hypotheses, rather than direct immune-cell counts or validated clinical IC subtypes.

Evidence: KF2

What additional evidence comes from single-cell analysis?

GSE145154 supplies expression context across 20 samples and eight annotated cell groups. MYH10 is prominent in fibroblasts, NUBPL in cardiomyocytes/fibroblasts, and NCKAP1 in endothelial, fibroblast and smooth-muscle groups. The 25,326 control and 56,189 IC cells are cells, not independent patients or a classifier-validation cohort.

Evidence: KF4

How were the mouse experiments designed, and what do the n values mean?

Methods allocate 16 mice per sham/control or IC group, with eight males and eight females in each, and examine tissue one week after LAD ligation. Captions specify n=8 for selected functional endpoints, n=6 for qPCR and n=4 for male Western blot. These endpoint-specific labels do not fully establish animal reuse or the biological-versus-technical replicate unit.

Evidence: KF5, KF6

Were MYH10 and NUBPL changes consistent across sex and assay?

Results 3.7 and the original plots show male MYH10 mRNA higher but female MYH10 mRNA lower in disease groups. Male NUBPL mRNA is nonsignificant, while female NUBPL mRNA is higher. Male IHC/Western blot show higher MYH10/NUBPL proteins. Discussion describes female patterns as mirrored, which differs from the MYH10 mRNA Results/figures. A sex-invariant or mRNA/protein-equivalent response is not asserted.

Evidence: KF5, KF6

What age and post-MI expression context was examined?

In GSE57338, MYH9 and MYL6 had reported age correlations r=-0.27 (p=0.0092) and r=-0.21 (p=0.039); human gene/sex comparisons were non-significant. Separate MI datasets examine 0-48 hours and Day 0/Day 30/Year 1. These secondary expression analyses are not prospective chronic-IC outcome validation.

Evidence: KF7

Does the ceRNA network demonstrate regulatory binding?

The reported network contains 219 nodes, comprising five candidate mRNAs, 54 miRNAs and 160 lncRNAs, with 268 predicted links. It organizes possible regulatory relationships but does not experimentally demonstrate binding or show that perturbing a link changes IC.

Evidence: KF8

How is this study useful for disulfidptosis or biomarker research?

It can support candidate selection, bulk/single-cell context and sex- or assay-specific expression follow-up. Its models and mouse expression findings should be cited at those evidence levels. Gene perturbation/rescue, independent clinical model evaluation and direct evidence of a disulfidptosis mechanism remain separate requirements.

Evidence: KF1, KF3, KF4, KF5, KF6, KF8

Source Note Dataset Identity
Methods 2.1 reports 152 controls/203 IC profiles. Table S1 gives GSE5406=16 controls/108 cases and GSE57338=136/95, reconciling with those counts. Registry-level series sizes and selected analytical profiles are different; sample selection was not reconstructed.
Source Note Validation
A previously accepted official GEO check established GSE57338 as a SubSeries of GSE57345. The paper does not demonstrate analytical nonoverlap or frozen model transfer. Additional dataset ROC estimates are retained as reported, not certified independent clinical validation.
Source Note Model Task
Results 3.3 describes Figure 4 as patient-cluster discrimination, then Figure 5 additional datasets as IC-versus-control. Captions invoke fivefold cross-validation; fixed coefficients and a fully specified frozen external pipeline are not established here. Figure 5 calls its model SVW in the caption.
Source Note Figure5 Panels
Actual pixels show Figure 5B decision-curve analysis and Figure 5C calibration, while the caption/text describe these in the opposite order. Model AUCs and intervals were read from panels D/F.
Source Note Sex And Modality
Results 3.7/Figure 8F show male MYH10 mRNA up and male NUBPL nonsignificant; Results 3.7/Figure S8D show female MYH10 down and NUBPL up. Discussion says female patterns mirrored males. Figure 9 male protein results are higher MYH10/NUBPL and lower MYH9/MYL6/NCKAP1; these are not substituted for mRNA results.
Source Note Animal Units
Methods 2.9 allocate 16 per group, eight males/eight females. Figures 8/S8 use endpoint caption n=8 or n=6; Figure 9B uses n=4. Replicate type and animal reuse are incompletely defined. The Figure 8 caption repeats label E for mRNA, while the actual image labels it F.
Source Note Temporal Context
Figure S7 was inspected as separate MI datasets with different time scales. This Gold reports the timepoint designs rather than certifying exact peak ordering from plotted summaries or treating them as longitudinal IC model validation.
Supporting Material
Data S1 contains Figures S1-S10 and Table S1. Original embedded Figures S6-S9 and Table S1 were inspected; S10 supplies uncropped gels/blots. Main Table 1 supplies qPCR primers.
Evidence Basis
Accepted complete original JATS and Data S1 DOCX reused unchanged; five same-version manifest-declared main figures (1,4,5,8,9) received with matching MD5s and visually read; original embedded supplementary images; accepted limited record/prior official GEO relationship check; protected publication/citation master.
Source Note Error Bars
Methods 2.17 states mean ± SD, while Figure 8 and Figure S8 captions state mean ± SEM; Figure 9B states mean ± SD. This Gold uses directions, caption n and explicitly printed model estimates rather than inferring precision from those error bars.
