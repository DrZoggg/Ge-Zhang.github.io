## Research Question

Can a systematically selected hybrid survival-machine-learning framework provide interpretable and externally validated postoperative overall-survival risk stratification for patients with rectal cancer undergoing robot-assisted proctectomy?

## Author Evidence Summary

This retrospective two-center study evaluated 506 unique patients undergoing robot-assisted proctectomy for rectal cancer. The NSCVRCS Meta cohort included 384 patients divided into development and internal-validation subsets; an independent SYEMH cohort included 122 patients. A comparison of 132 candidate survival-modeling schemes selected Surv.coxboost + Surv.aorsf and yielded an interpretable model with 10 predictors. RAP-AIscore showed internal time-dependent survival discrimination and separated patients into three prognostic states. Independent SYEMH evaluation was retrospective: its 1-year AUC was 0.587, with 2- and 3-year AUCs of 0.754 and 0.810. Prospective clinical utility and improved survival from model-guided care remain unestablished.

## Study Design & Model Development

### Study profile

- Study Design: Double-center retrospective cohort study with internal and independent external validation
- Evidence Type: Human postoperative prognostic survival-modeling study
- Unique total: 506
- Population: Patients with rectal cancer undergoing robot-assisted proctectomy
- Primary Endpoint: In-hospital and post-discharge overall survival
- Secondary Endpoint: Postoperative prognostic risk stratification and time-dependent survival discrimination
- External validation: Yes
- Data modalities: electronic health records, clinical and laboratory variables, operative and pathologic characteristics, longitudinal survival outcomes

### Cohort hierarchy

| Cohort | Role | n | Relationship |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| NSCVRCS Discovery | model development | 268 | Subset of NSCVRCS Meta |
| NSCVRCS Replication | internal validation | 116 | Subset of NSCVRCS Meta |
| NSCVRCS Meta | complete in-house/modeling cohort | 384 | Contains NSCVRCS Discovery, NSCVRCS Replication |
| SYEMH | independent external validation | 122 | Independent cohort |

### Model development

- Initial predictors: 53
- Algorithms: 12
- Modeling schemes: 132
- Selected algorithms: Surv.coxboost, Surv.aorsf
- Feature selection: Filter + sequential-forward-floating wrapper feature selection maximizing Harrell C-index
- Final predictors: 10
- Validation Strategy: 10 repetitions of 10-fold cross-validation, Monte Carlo cross-validation: 100 iterations, sampling rate 0.7, bootstrap: 1,000 iterations, sampling rate 0.7

### Final predictor set

1. pTNM
2. ROM
3. CEA
4. Ns
5. PostCEA
6. Ts
7. CA199
8. HLP
9. Postchem
10. Minutes

### Interpretability

- SHAP
- nomogram
- conditional inference tree
- t-SNE risk-state visualization

## What This Study Adds

- Retrospective development of an interpretable survival model for patients undergoing robot-assisted proctectomy.
- Systematic comparison of hybrid survival-modeling schemes.
- Internal time-dependent discrimination and independent retrospective external evaluation.
- Three-state postoperative prognostic stratification.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Retrospective development of an interpretable RAP-specific survival model.
- Systematic hybrid model selection.
- Internal time-dependent survival discrimination.
- Independent retrospective external evaluation, with modest 1-year AUC of 0.587.
- Three-state prognostic stratification.

### Does Not Establish

- Prospective clinical utility.
- Improved survival from RAP-AIscore-guided care or treatment efficacy.
- Causal effects of the 10 predictors.
- Universal generalizability to all RAP populations or settings.
- Replacement of clinician judgment or pathologic staging.

### Limitations

- The two-center study and independent external evaluation were retrospective.
- Prospective clinical utility and survival benefit from model-guided management were not established.
- External 1-year discrimination was modest, with AUC 0.587.
- Generalizability to all hospitals and RAP populations was not established.
- The Results text reports Replication low/intermediate/high counts of 68/20/19, which sum to 107 and do not reconcile with the stated Replication cohort size of 116. These subgroup counts are retained as article-reported values; the denominator discrepancy is unresolved and must not be interpreted as documented exclusions or a complete partition of all 116 patients.

## Q&A

### What population was RAP-AIscore developed for?

Patients with rectal cancer undergoing robot-assisted proctectomy; the study modeled postoperative overall survival retrospectively.

Evidence: KF1

### How many independent patients and cohorts were studied?

There were 506 unique patients: 384 in NSCVRCS Meta and 122 in independent SYEMH. NSCVRCS Discovery n=268 and Replication n=116 are subsets of Meta, not additional patients.

Evidence: KF1

### How were 132 modeling schemes compared?

The study systematically compared candidate survival-modeling schemes and selected Surv.coxboost + Surv.aorsf, which achieved the highest average C-index, 0.833.

Evidence: KF1, KF2

### What performance was observed internally?

At 12/36/60/72 months, AUCs were 0.851/0.849/0.902/0.915 in Replication, 0.911/0.924/0.931/0.948 in Meta, and 0.927/0.960/0.949/0.962 in Discovery. These are retrospective time-dependent discrimination results.

Evidence: KF3

### What happened in independent SYEMH validation?

In the independent retrospective cohort, 1-/2-/3-year AUCs were 0.587/0.754/0.810. Early discrimination was modest. High-versus-low survival differed at P < 0.0001 and three-state discrimination at P < 0.001.

Evidence: KF6

### Does RAP-AIscore have proven prospective clinical benefit?

No. The retrospective model-development, internal-validation and external-evaluation findings do not establish prospective clinical utility or survival benefit from model-guided care.

Evidence: KF1, KF3, KF6
