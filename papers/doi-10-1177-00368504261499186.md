## Author evidence note

### Research question and design

The study examined whether EASIX was associated with later mortality among 10,885 NHANES participants aged at least 30 years with cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome stages 0–3. It retrospectively analyzed survey cycles from 1999–2018, linking deaths through 2019. EASIX is calculated as lactate dehydrogenase multiplied by creatinine, divided by platelet count.

Source: [PubMed PMID 42893432, Abstract Objective and Methods: population, NHANES cycles, mortality linkage and EASIX definition.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42893432/)

### Principal quantitative findings

Over a median 97 months of follow-up, 1,122 participants died; 295 deaths were cardiovascular. Per one-unit higher EASIX, the adjusted hazard ratio was 1.41 (95% CI 1.24–1.61) for all-cause mortality and 1.30 (95% CI 1.13–1.49) for cardiovascular mortality. The abstract reports that the associations persisted after further lifestyle adjustment.

Source: [PubMed PMID 42893432, Abstract Results: follow-up, deaths, adjusted HRs and CIs, one-unit exposure scale and lifestyle adjustment.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42893432/)

### Cohort and model specification

Eligibility required a valid fasting-subsample weight and complete CKM-staging, EASIX, mortality-linkage and covariate information; participants were assigned their highest qualifying CKM stage, and stage 4 was excluded. Stage 3 included very-high-risk CKD or PREVENT 10-year total-CVD risk ≥20%; participants aged ≥80 years were assigned age 79 for PREVENT estimation. EASIX used LDH (U/L) × creatinine (mg/dL) / platelet count (10^9/L) and was modeled per one-unit increase and by quartiles. Analyses combined fasting-subsample weights with SDMVSTRA and SDMVPSU in survey-weighted Cox models. Model 2 added current smoking, BMI, systolic blood pressure, fasting glucose and antihypertensive, glucose-lowering and statin use to demographic adjustment. Follow-up ran from the baseline MEC examination to death or 31 December 2019; competing-risk analysis was a sensitivity analysis. These specifications support comparing cohort and model choices; the displayed Methods do not give the pooled-weight formula or exact cardiovascular-death codes.

Source: [Science Progress, published Methods Sections 2.1–2.5, covering cohort eligibility, CKM staging, EASIX, mortality linkage and statistical analysis.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00368504261499186)

### Scientific contribution and publication context

The analysis connects a composite endothelial-stress index with long-term outcomes among adults at CKM stages 0–3. It provides an observational comparator for epidemiologic discussions of endothelial stress, CKM staging and mortality; survey-weighted Cox models and spline analyses supply the reported association framework. Clinical risk-assessment use requires further validation. Electronic publication is recorded as 18 October 2026. The formal journal record is Science Progress, volume 109, issue 4 (2026), article 00368504261499186.

Source: [PubMed PMID 42893432, Abstract Methods and Conclusions: survey-weighted Cox models, restricted cubic splines, CKM stage scope and risk-assessment interpretation. PubMed ArticleDate DateType=Electronic 2026-10-18; Crossref volume 109, issue 4, article-number 00368504261499186 and published-print [2026,10].](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42893432/)

### Evidence scope and limitations

Author-maintained explanation of an observational mortality analysis in CKM stages 0–3, supported by the indexed abstract and source-located reading of the published Methods.

- The retrospective cohort associations support epidemiologic comparison; they do not establish that changing EASIX changes mortality or that EASIX is an externally validated clinical prediction model.
