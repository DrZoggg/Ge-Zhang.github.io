## Research context and source limits

Limited evidence note. Only the source material identified below has been reviewed; this is not a complete Paper GEO V2 assessment.

Original author-maintained explanation of an observational mortality analysis in CKM stages 0–3, based on the indexed abstract and verified publication metadata.

### Research question and design

The study examined whether EASIX was associated with later mortality among 10,885 NHANES participants aged at least 30 years with cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome stages 0–3. It retrospectively analyzed survey cycles from 1999–2018, linking deaths through 2019. EASIX is calculated as lactate dehydrogenase multiplied by creatinine, divided by platelet count.

Source: [PubMed PMID 42893432, Abstract Objective and Methods: population, NHANES cycles, mortality linkage and EASIX definition.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42893432/)

### Principal quantitative findings

Over a median 97 months of follow-up, 1,122 participants died; 295 deaths were cardiovascular. Per one-unit higher EASIX, the adjusted hazard ratio was 1.41 (95% CI 1.24–1.61) for all-cause mortality and 1.30 (95% CI 1.13–1.49) for cardiovascular mortality. The abstract reports that the associations persisted after further lifestyle adjustment.

Source: [PubMed PMID 42893432, Abstract Results: follow-up, deaths, adjusted HRs and CIs, one-unit exposure scale and lifestyle adjustment.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42893432/)

### Scientific contribution and use

The analysis connects a composite endothelial-stress index with long-term outcomes among adults at CKM stages 0–3. It provides an observational comparator for epidemiologic discussions of endothelial stress, CKM staging and mortality; survey-weighted Cox models and spline analyses supply the reported association framework. Clinical risk-assessment use requires further validation.

Source: [PubMed PMID 42893432, Abstract Methods and Conclusions: survey-weighted Cox models, restricted cubic splines, CKM stage scope and risk-assessment interpretation.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42893432/)

### Evidence limits

- This summary uses the indexed abstract rather than a full-methods audit. The retrospective cohort associations support epidemiologic comparison; they do not establish that changing EASIX changes mortality or that EASIX is an externally validated clinical prediction model.
