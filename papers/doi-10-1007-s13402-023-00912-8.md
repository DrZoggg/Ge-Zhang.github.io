## Research Question

How does omics-based molecular classification connect cancer diagnosis, treatment stratification and prognosis, and what limits its clinical translation?

## Author Evidence Summary

This narrative review organizes published work around molecular diagnosis, treatment stratification and prognosis. It covers genomic and epigenomic, transcriptomic and proteomic biomarkers, then discusses integrated and spatial omics as ways to characterize tumor heterogeneity. A separate section compares biomarker-oriented clinical-trial designs. The final sections address data integration, algorithm interpretability and the limitations of preclinical tumor models. These are a synthesis and research agenda: clinical effects and model performance described in the review belong to the cited studies, not to a new cohort or validation experiment conducted for this article.

## Q&A

### Which biomarker layers are distinguished in the review?

Its biomarker overview distinguishes genomic, epigenomic, transcriptomic and proteomic information. These are categories of prior molecular research, not four new validation cohorts.

Evidence: KF1

### Does this review validate a new multi-omics classifier?

No. It discusses published integration and spatial-profiling approaches. Performance or treatment-response claims for any particular classifier must be checked in the cited primary study.

Evidence: KF2

### Which clinical-trial design frameworks are compared?

The review compares basket, umbrella, platform and N-of-1 designs. This comparison provides context for molecularly informed trial design; it does not supply a pooled efficacy estimate.

Evidence: KF3

### What prevents a molecular classification from automatically becoming a treatment recommendation?

The review identifies complex data integration, the need for clinical interpretability and limitations of preclinical models. Its discussion of AI, organoids and three-dimensional models is a research agenda, not clinical proof of benefit.

Evidence: KF4
