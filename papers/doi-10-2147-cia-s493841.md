## Evidence Snapshot

**Post-EVAR mortality: a clinical, laboratory and CTA nomogram with internal evaluation**

This study integrates clinical history, blood measurements and CTA findings into an exploratory nomogram for 1- and 3-year all-cause mortality after elective EVAR. It reports a 304-patient single-centre cohort, selected predictors and internal evaluations, with source-specific performance and sample-accounting discrepancies retained.

### Counting note

The source cohort is 304, split as 213 plus 91; the parent and subsets are counted once. Table 1 reports 33 and 13 all-cause deaths, consistent with 46 overall. Its validation cell prints 13 (14.29%), whereas the performance Results print 13 (14.39%); neither is silently replaced. The original Figure 5 risk tables begin with 49+52=101 and 103+100=203, inconsistent with their caption-assigned training/testing subsets; no revised sample allocation is inferred. The supplement names datacox without defining membership, so the Table 3 Cox analytical denominator/event count is not assigned to 213 or 304.

## Research Question

Which routinely available clinical, laboratory and CTA features were associated with all-cause mortality after elective EVAR, and how did the proposed 1- and 3-year nomogram perform in the reported internal evaluations?

## Author Evidence Summary

This single-centre retrospective study enrolled 304 adults treated with elective EVAR from March 2015 to May 2023, reporting a random 213/91 training/internal-validation split. There were 46 all-cause deaths over a median 24-month follow-up. The authors combined LASSO selection and Cox regression to construct a six-variable nomogram: large AAA, stroke history, common iliac artery atherosclerosis, age, haemoglobin and monocytes. The reported adjusted hazard ratios for the three binary predictors were 3.09, 3.59 and 4.48, respectively; the Cox analytical population remains unspecified. The article supplies discrimination, calibration, Brier, decision-curve and risk-group analyses. These evaluations add methodological context, while inconsistent AUC values, validation mortality percentages and plotted risk-table counts prevent selecting a single certified performance account. The study contributes candidate prognostic associations and a multimodal post-EVAR modeling example. It does not establish an externally validated individual risk tool or show that intervening on a predictor improves survival.

## Study Design & Model Development

### Study profile

- Study Design: Single-centre retrospective observational cohort with a reported random 7:3 training/internal-validation split; LASSO-Cox nomogram development and internal evaluation.
- Evidence Type: Clinical prognostic associations and reported model development/evaluation, with unresolved analytic-denominator, scaling and performance-reporting questions.
- Unique total: 304
- Population: Adults undergoing elective EVAR for AAA at the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University, March 2015–May 2023; ruptured AAA and open repair excluded.
- Primary Endpoint: All-cause mortality after EVAR; the nomogram targets 1- and 3-year outcomes.
- Secondary Endpoint: Reported discrimination, calibration, time-dependent Brier scores, decision curves and risk-group survival separation are model evaluations rather than additional clinical endpoints.
- External validation: No
- Data modalities: Clinical history and demographics, Preoperative fasting blood laboratory measurements, Three-dimensional CTA anatomy, Clinic-based mortality follow-up

### Model development

- Target: 1- and 3-year all-cause mortality after elective EVAR for AAA.
- Candidate Information: Clinical comorbidity, demographics, fasting laboratory measurements and CTA findings.
- Selection Method: LASSO penalty selected with reported 10-fold cross-validation; seven variables carried to univariate/multivariate Cox analysis and AIC-stepwise refinement; six predictors in the nomogram.
- Validation Strategy: Reported random internal holdout and 1,000-resample calibration assessment. Independent external-cohort validation was not performed.
- Reported Discrimination: Training Abstract/Results: 0.84 at 1 year and 0.81 at 3 years; original Figure 4a: 0.84 and 0.82; Discussion: 0.88 and 0.87. Internal-validation text/Figure 4b: 0.71 and 0.80. These accounts are preserved separately.
- Calibration And Utility: Original calibration and decision-curve plots plus reported Brier scores supply internal evaluation context; outcome-axis wording and unspecified cohort attribution prevent certification of a clinical risk tool.
- Risk Group Selection: Youden score cutoff 155.1 selected in the total dataset. Original KM panels report separation but their baseline risk-table totals disagree with the caption-assigned 213/91 split.
- Reproducibility: Supplementary code illustrates mice/glmnet/coxph/timeROC/calibration/DCA calls. It does not construct datacox or fully specify analytical membership, continuous-predictor coding and fitted-model transfer. No patient risk calculator is reconstructed.

## What This Study Adds

- Combines routinely recorded comorbidity, blood measures and CTA anatomy in a site-specific post-EVAR prognostic modeling example.
- Supplies exploratory mortality associations and multiple internal-evaluation outputs for comparing endpoint, feature-selection, discrimination and calibration reporting.
- Provides concrete source-located distinctions between an internal split, full-dataset risk stratification, analytical membership, and the external evaluation needed for a transferable risk tool.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- The stated elective-EVAR population, all-cause endpoint, baseline measurement framework and reported cohort split.
- The six named predictors and exact published Cox estimates, with unspecified analytical N and continuous scaling retained.
- Source-specific reported AUCs, internal plots, Brier summaries and analysis-script choices.

### Does Not Establish

- A single reconciled training AUC, corrected validation percentage, or verified Figure 5 training/testing membership.
- A causal effect of a predictor, or survival benefit from modifying a predictor or treatment.
- Independently validated model performance, clinical decision benefit or an individual risk calculator reconstructed from the article.

### Limitations

- The Cox object datacox is not constructed in the supplementary outline. Patient/event membership, missing-data handling and continuous-predictor transformations/reference coding are incompletely specified.
- Training AUCs disagree across Abstract/Results, Figure 4a and Discussion. The validation mortality percentage differs between Table 1 and Results. No reconciled values are supplied here.
- Figure 5 baseline risk-table totals and caption-assigned cohorts disagree with the reported 213/91 split; the risk cutoff was selected in the total dataset. The nomogram-survival versus calibration-ACM axis wording is also unresolved.
- The retrospective single-centre sample has 46 all-cause deaths and limited information on endograft device types. Selection bias, residual confounding and model instability/generalizability remain relevant.
- Internal discrimination, calibration and modeled net benefit do not establish independent external performance, causal predictor effects or benefit from clinical use. The source’s post-hoc power claim does not replace that evaluation.

## Q&A

### Which patients and endpoint were studied?

The article describes 304 adults undergoing elective EVAR at one centre from March 2015 to May 2023, with ruptured AAA and open repair excluded. The endpoint is all-cause mortality, with median follow-up 24 months (IQR 16–33), and a reported 213/91 random internal split. Tables report 46 total deaths, comprising 33 training and 13 validation deaths. The validation percentage differs between Table 1 (14.29%) and Results (14.39%).

Evidence: KF1

### Which information entered the candidate predictor framework?

The study combines clinical history and demographics, fasting preoperative blood measurements and three-dimensional CTA anatomy. It defines large AAA using sex-specific 5.5 cm/5.0 cm thresholds and reports LASSO/Cox selection of large AAA, prior stroke, CIA atherosclerosis, age, haemoglobin and monocyte count.

Evidence: KF2

### What adjusted associations are printed in Table 3?

Large AAA, stroke history and CIA atherosclerosis have reported adjusted HRs 3.09 (1.43–6.72), 3.59 (1.67–7.70) and 4.48 (1.92–10.49). Age, haemoglobin and monocyte labels have HRs 1.54, 0.53 and 1.28, with their printed intervals in KF3. Their precise scaling and the datacox analytical membership are not established, so these values are not interpreted as per-year or per-laboratory-unit effects.

Evidence: KF3, KF7

### What model output does the nomogram display?

Figure 3 combines the six predictors into a point display with 1- and 3-year survival axes. The calibration panels are labelled as ACM probabilities/observed events, creating an outcome-orientation ambiguity. The display can be described as the authors’ proposed model; its individual probabilities are not reconstructed as a certified patient calculator.

Evidence: KF2, KF5

### Which AUCs are reported, and why are they kept separate?

Training Abstract/Results give 0.84 and 0.81 at 1 and 3 years; original Figure 4a gives 0.84 and 0.82; Discussion gives 0.88 and 0.87. Internal-validation text/Figure 4b give 0.71 and 0.80. These are source-specific reports; the conflicting training estimates are not averaged or silently selected.

Evidence: KF4

### Was there independent external validation?

No. The article reports a random internal holdout from the same centre and recruitment period, and explicitly acknowledges that independent external validation is outstanding. The LASSO 10-fold penalty-selection step and bootstrap calibration are separate internal procedures. The supplement does not establish the exact membership of every fitted Cox analysis.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF7

### What do calibration, decision curves and risk groups add?

They supply internal model-assessment context: calibration plots, reported Brier scores and modeled net benefit, plus risk-group separation after a score 155.1 cutoff selected in the total dataset. Figure 5 risk tables total 101 and 203 across its panels rather than the stated 213/91 allocation, so panel membership is unresolved. None of these analyses demonstrates better outcomes from using the model in care.

Evidence: KF5, KF6

### How can this paper support research writing or model design?

It can support comparison of elective-EVAR endpoints, clinical/laboratory/CTA predictor integration, exploratory associations and reported internal-evaluation workflows.
