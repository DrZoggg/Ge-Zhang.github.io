## Research Question

How can multimodal AI support cardiovascular mechanism research and updateable patient-specific digital models?

## Author Evidence Summary

The Correspondence outlines two linked directions for cardiovascular AI: investigating disease mechanisms and developing patient-specific digital twins. It discusses combining imaging and molecular information to generate network and disease-subtyping hypotheses, then considers updateable physiological models, image-to-mesh workflows and treatment-scenario simulation. Longitudinal data, computational capacity, prospective evaluation, privacy and governance are presented as requirements for translation. This page organizes those narrative arguments for research planning and methodological citation. The cited empirical examples remain evidence from other works; cross-modal integration does not itself establish causation, and simulated treatment scenarios are not observed therapeutic effects in patients.

### Does Not Establish

- A newly developed, validated therapeutic digital twin or completed prospective clinical validation in this Correspondence.
- Causation established by combining imaging and molecular data alone.
- Observed human treatment effects or randomized clinical-trial evidence inferred from simulation.
- Proven efficacy of a drug, surgery or commercial digital twin product, patient-outcome improvement, guideline status or regulatory approval.

### Limitations

- This Correspondence is a narrative discussion, not a systematic review or meta-analysis with a reported systematic search protocol.
- The cited empirical examples were not comprehensively revalidated for this page and should be checked in their primary sources.
- The author-deposited full article supports narrative locators; every word was not compared with the current ScienceDirect full text.
- Proposed mechanistic hypotheses and simulation strategies require appropriate empirical and prospective evaluation before clinical benefit can be claimed.
