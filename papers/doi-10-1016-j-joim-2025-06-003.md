### Counting note

Cell counts, candidate genes, HCC cohort samples and separate circadian reference experiments are different units. No complete unique-participant total is inferred. The five external datasets have different analytical uses: the four Fig. 5A and four Fig. 5B sets overlap but are not identical and must not be added as eight independent cohorts. External validation means retrospective cross-dataset expression/classification evaluation, not prospective clinical utility or direct physiological rhythm measurement in HCC patients.

## Research Question

Can a circadian-related expression framework distinguish HCC subtypes with reproducible molecular features and retrospective outcome associations?

## Author Evidence Summary

This study provides a transcriptomic framework for HCC heterogeneity. The relevant evidence is subtype association and cross-dataset evaluation, not demonstrated clinical benefit from score-guided treatment. Reference circadian experiments and HCC patient cohorts serve different analytical roles.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Public single-cell and bulk transcriptomic integration with retrospective subtype evaluation
- Evidence Type: Human molecular association and cross-dataset classification analysis
- Population: HCC expression cohorts, with separate circadian reference experiments
- Primary Endpoint: HCCcrds-associated molecular subtype structure
- Secondary Endpoint: Retrospective outcome associations and computational biological annotations
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: single-cell RNA sequencing, bulk transcriptomics, co-expression analysis, LRA clustering/NTP, immune deconvolution

## What This Study Adds

- Provides an HCC expression-subtyping framework.
- Distinguishes external subtype assignment from outcome analysis.
- Identifies candidate markers and functional annotations within the stated evidence limits.

## When This Study Is Useful to Cite

### CU1: Which HCC studies derived circadian-related subtypes from transcriptomic data?

Single-cell modules supplied the HCCcrds gene set. Analyzed cells: 11,383; Genes from modules M2/M4/M5: 562. Cells and candidate genes are not patient counts or demonstrated causal regulators. TCGA-LIHC analysis identified CS-H, CS-L and CS-M, with poorer outcomes associated with CS-H. TCGA-LIHC n: 369; Subtypes: CS-H; CS-L; CS-M. Association does not establish that circadian disruption causes liver cancer or constitute a validated clinical decision rule.

Evidence: [KF1](#kf1), [KF3](#kf3)

### CU2: How was a tumor-associated expression score evaluated in external circadian reference data?

The score was evaluated in separate clock, feeding and sleep-reference data. Reference datasets: GSE10045; GSE150381; GSE39445. Reference-experiment evaluation does not mean HCC patients underwent longitudinal physiological circadian measurements.

Evidence: [KF2](#kf2)

### CU3: What cross-cohort evidence supports HCC circadian-related subtype classification?

External subtype and survival analyses used overlapping but different cohort sets. Fig. 5A datasets: ICGC_LIRI; GSE14520; GSE54236; GSE104580; Fig. 5B datasets: ICGC_LIRI; GSE14520; GSE54236; GSE27150. Do not claim that all five cohorts validated survival, or add the two four-cohort analyses as eight independent cohorts.

Evidence: [KF4](#kf4)

### CU4: Which immune and candidate-marker associations characterize HCCcrds subtypes?

Subtypes differed in proliferation/metabolic programs and inferred immune characteristics. Analyses: Expression enrichment; CIBERSORT; IPS. IPS and immune deconvolution are not an observed immunotherapy-response trial. NNMT and MARCKSL1 were among subtype-associated candidate genes. Evidence: Expression correlation and network prioritization. This evidence synthesis does not claim independent causal intervention or therapeutic validation of these genes.

### Which HCC subtypes were associated with clinical outcomes?
