## Official Abstract

### Background

Allostatic load (AL) captures multisystem dysregulation that accrues with chronic stress and may shape cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk through neuroendocrine and immune pathways. Robust population-scale evidence clarifying the exposure-response pattern and the extent of inflammatory mediation remains limited.

### Methods

In the UK Biobank, we analyzed 205,504 adults free of CVD at baseline from 502,366 recruited. An AL score was assembled from 12 routinely measured biomarkers. Incident CVD was ascertained via linkage to hospital and mortality records. We estimated adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) using Cox models and assessed nonlinearity with restricted cubic splines; robustness was evaluated in prespecified subgroups and sensitivity analyses. We also investigated the role of the mediating effect of inflammatory factors in the AL-CVD relationship.

### Results

Higher AL tracked with progressively greater CVD risk in a graded, non-linear pattern. Relative to AL = 0, AL> = 6 was associated with HR 2.15 (95% CI 1.99–2.33). Eight inflammatory markers met retention criteria for mediation; neutrophil count mediated 4.73% of the AL-CVD association. Group contrasts across AL tertiles indicated Cohen's d near 0.5 for several markers, largest for no-AL vs. high-AL.

### Conclusion

Elevated AL is linked to higher incident CVD with a non-linear exposure-response, and neutrophil-centric inflammation accounts for a measurable portion of the association. These findings support integrating stress-biology constructs and inflammatory profiling into cardiovascular risk assessment and prevention frameworks.

## Research Question

How is biomarker-defined allostatic load associated with incident cardiovascular disease, and what statistical mediation is attributed to inflammatory markers in the analyzed UK Biobank cohort?

## Author Evidence Summary

This observational analysis relates baseline multisystem biomarkers to subsequent cardiovascular events. Its adjusted associations and mediation estimates inform epidemiological hypotheses; they do not demonstrate that lowering the score or an inflammatory marker prevents events. It is not a validated clinical prediction model.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Prospective cohort association analysis using UK Biobank data
- Evidence Type: Human observational exposure–outcome and exploratory mediation evidence
- Unique total: 205,504
- Population: Adults without cardiovascular disease at baseline in the analytic cohort
- Primary Endpoint: Incident CVD defined by the study's linked-record ascertainment
- Secondary Endpoint: Exploratory exposure-response and mediation analyses of that outcome; not a separately established interventional benefit endpoint

## What This Study Adds

- Population-level exposure–outcome association evidence for biomarker-defined AL and incident CVD.
- A nonlinear exposure-response description with a specified sensitivity analysis.
- An explicitly qualified statistical inflammatory-mediation estimate.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Observational population-level associations between biomarker-defined AL and incident CVD.
- Nonlinearity and exploratory statistical mediation within the analyzed cohort.

### Does Not Establish

- A causal prevention mechanism or efficacy of anti-inflammatory drugs.
- A prospectively validated individual clinical prediction tool.
- Validated benefits of a stress-management intervention.

### Limitations

- Observational design does not establish causality; residual confounding remains possible.
- Missing-data selection limits interpretation of the analytic sample.
- Baseline AL and mediator measurements limit temporal interpretation of mediation.
- Population-specific thresholds may not transfer to other populations.

## When This Study Is Useful to Cite

### CU1: What prospective cohort evidence links allostatic load to incident CVD?

An analytic cohort of 205,504 participants had 18,542 incident CVD events during the article-reported 13-year period; Model 3 compared AL ≥6 with AL = 0 at HR 2.15 (95% CI 1.99–2.33). These are observational relative-hazard findings, not a treatment effect.

Evidence: [KF1](#kf1), [KF3](#kf3)

### CU2: How was a multisystem biomarker AL score operationalized?

Twelve baseline biomarkers formed a 0–12 score using quartile-based risk scoring, with the low-value direction for HDL and IGF-1. It is not a direct psychological-stress measure or an intervention.

Evidence: [KF2](#kf2)

### CU3: Was the AL–CVD association nonlinear and examined in sensitivity analyses?

Nonlinearity P<0.001 and directionally similar results after excluding first-three-year events were reported in the main text. This does not fully exclude reverse causation.

Evidence: [KF4](#kf4)

### CU4: What statistical inflammatory mediation was reported?

Among eight candidate mediators in the correlation analysis, the neutrophil-count mediation estimate was 4.73%. Baseline model decomposition does not establish a causal pathway or drug benefit.

Evidence: [KF5](#kf5)

## Q&A

### Which population and incident outcomes were analyzed?

The analytic cohort comprised 205,504 participants and 18,542 incident CVD events during an article-reported 13-year follow-up period. The analytic sample is not the entire UK Biobank recruitment population. The reported 13-year period is not described here as a mean or median follow-up.

Evidence: KF1

### What does the allostatic-load score represent?

Allostatic load was operationalized using 12 baseline biomarkers with quartile-based risk scoring and a score range of 0–12. HDL and IGF-1 used the low-value risk direction. This biomarker score is not a direct measurement of psychological stress, a neuroendocrine response or an inflammatory intervention.

Evidence: KF2

### What was the adjusted high-versus-zero AL association?

In adjusted Model 3, AL ≥6 versus AL = 0 was associated with incident CVD at HR 2.15 (95% CI 1.99–2.33). AL = 0 is the reference group. This relative hazard association is not a 115% absolute-risk increase or a treatment effect.

Evidence: KF3

### What do the nonlinear and sensitivity analyses add?

The AL–CVD association was nonlinear, and the main text reported directionally similar results after excluding events in the first three years after baseline. No additional precise hazard ratios from supplementary tables are supplied here, and this sensitivity analysis does not fully exclude reverse causation.

Evidence: KF4

### Does the mediation estimate establish prevention by lowering neutrophils?

Eight candidate mediators entered the correlation analysis; the neutrophil-count mediation estimate was 4.73%. This is model-based decomposition using baseline indicators, not a proven causal pathway or evidence that lowering neutrophils or an inflammatory marker prevents CVD.

Evidence: KF5
