Official Abstract

Background

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is typically an asymptomatic disease closely associated with immune mechanisms. A deep understanding of cellular responses within AAA tissues, particularly the molecular changes in T-cell populations, is critical for disease diagnosis and treatment. However, the specific mechanisms inducing T-lymphocyte fate imbalance in AAA remain to be elucidated.

Results

The analysis revealed the core mechanisms driving T-lymphocyte fate imbalance in AAA. We successfully established a comprehensive regulatory map encompassing T-cell infiltration regulatory features, critical transcription factors, and dysregulated immune signaling pathways. Machine learning algorithms identified transcription factors FOSB and JUNB as key biomarkers. Validation across multiple independent datasets and clinical samples confirmed the feasibility and accuracy of FOSB and JUNB as clinical diagnostic biomarkers for AAA.

Conclusions

Through the analysis of single-cell and bulk data, hallmarks of human AAA cellular landscape and T-cell comprehensive developmental relationships were recapitulated. This study identified important roles of T-cell and the molecular mechanisms for the dynamic T-cell infiltrating process, which could characterize disease status and landscape of human AAA microenvironment. Using the deep learning algorithms, FOSB and JUNB were demonstrated as pivotal biomarkers of AAA, together with screening the potential pharmacologic agents targeting T-cell polarization. Taken together, this expands the current understanding of AAA pathogenesis and may provide a feasible immune-targeted therapeutic strategy.

Research Question

Can integrated human single-cell and bulk transcriptomic analyses reconstruct T-cell state imbalance in abdominal aortic aneurysm and identify reproducible T-cell-linked molecular markers across public datasets and independent clinical samples?

Author Evidence Summary

The study combined human AAA single-cell profiles, public bulk transcriptomic cohorts and independent clinical samples to describe T-cell heterogeneity and infer transcriptional state relationships. Four aneurysmal aortic samples yielded 7,343 post-QC cells, including 3,841 T cells assigned to eight phenotypes. Monocle inferred a branching trajectory; it did not directly follow individual cells over time. Integrative gene-set analysis derived a 50-gene TIRS, and LASSO plus SVM-RFE prioritized FOSB, JUNB, CST7 and TBC1D4. Marker discrimination varied across public cohorts and was evaluated in small independent aortic-wall samples (5 AAA, 4 controls) and peripheral blood (24 AAA, 15 controls). BAPN + AngII mouse experiments provided supportive FOSB/JUNB expression evidence. These findings support transcriptomic associations and candidate-marker evaluation, while prospective diagnostic utility, human causal mechanisms and treatment benefit remain unestablished.

What This Study Adds

  • A human AAA single-cell description of T-cell heterogeneity and inferred state relationships.
  • A 50-gene TIRS derived from complementary transcriptomic analyses.
  • Cross-dataset candidate-marker evaluation with small independent human-sample assessment.
  • Supportive mouse FOSB/JUNB expression observations.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Human AAA single-cell T-cell heterogeneity.
  • Inferred T-cell transcriptional state and fate structure.
  • Multicohort AAA-associated transcriptomic patterns and TIRS derivation.
  • FOSB/JUNB/CST7/TBC1D4 marker discrimination in the analyzed datasets.
  • Small independent in-house human validation, including aortic wall n=5 AAA and n=4 controls.
  • Supportive mouse expression evidence.

Does Not Establish

  • Pseudotime as directly observed longitudinal cell fate.
  • 7,343 cells as 7,343 participants.
  • FOSB/JUNB as established causal drivers of human AAA.
  • Prospective diagnostic performance or approved standalone clinical tests.
  • Predicted pharmacologic agents as effective AAA therapy.
  • Mouse expression evidence as human treatment evidence.

Limitations

  • The in-house aortic-wall sample size was small: 5 AAA and 4 controls.
  • Pseudotime is an inferred transcriptional ordering, not direct longitudinal lineage observation.
  • Candidate-marker discrimination does not establish prospective clinical diagnostic performance.
  • Mouse expression observations do not establish human causal mechanisms or therapeutic benefit.

Q&A

What single-cell evidence was analyzed?

Four aneurysmal aortic samples yielded 7,343 post-QC cells, 16 populations and nine main cell types. The T-cell analysis included 3,841 cells and eight phenotypes.

Evidence: KF1, KF2

What does pseudotime establish and not establish?

Monocle inferred a bifurcated trajectory with two terminal fates and three infiltration states. It reconstructs transcriptional relationships and does not directly observe individual cells changing fate over time.

Evidence: KF2

How was TIRS derived?

The analysis used 968 cell-state DE genes, 437 BEAM fate leader genes and 375 trajectory-ordering genes, with a 374-gene intersection and a final bulk-overlapping TIRS of 50 genes.

Evidence: KF4

Which candidate biomarkers were selected?

The LASSO and SVM-RFE intersection selected FOSB, JUNB, CST7 and TBC1D4.

Evidence: KF5

How did they perform across public cohorts?

In FOSB/JUNB/CST7/TBC1D4 order, AUCs were 0.911/0.917/0.926/0.955 in Dataset 1, 0.982/0.911/0.893/0.857 in Dataset 2 and 0.956/0.887/0.506/0.514 in Dataset 3. Discrimination varied by marker and dataset.

Evidence: KF5

Were they evaluated in independent human samples?

Yes: aortic wall from 5 AAA and 4 controls, and peripheral blood from 24 AAA and 15 controls. Aortic-wall AUCs were 1.000/1.000/0.600/0.800 and blood AUCs were 0.989/0.906/0.933/0.736 in FOSB/JUNB/CST7/TBC1D4 order. The aortic-wall sample was small.

Evidence: KF6

Does this study establish a clinical diagnostic test or therapy?

No. The reported associations, marker discrimination, small human-sample evaluation and supportive mouse expression do not establish prospective diagnostic utility, approved standalone tests or human treatment efficacy.

Evidence: KF5, KF6, KF7
