## Evidence Snapshot

**Estimated glucose disposal rate, hyperuricemia and gout: cross-sectional NHANES associations**

A survey-weighted analysis of 29,340 NHANES participants examined eGDR associations with hyperuricemia and self-reported gout. Fully adjusted continuous associations were inverse, but the 4–6 mg/kg/min gout contrast was not statistically significant. The evidence does not establish prevention or a treatment effect.

| Evidence | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Unique total | n=29,340 |
| NHANES 2007–2018 analytic sample | n=29,340 |

## Research Question

How is estimated glucose disposal rate associated with prevalent hyperuricemia and self-reported gout in NHANES adults after survey weighting and measured-covariate adjustment?

## Author Evidence Summary

The study analyzed adults from six NHANES cycles covering 2007–2018. The exposure eGDR combines waist circumference, hypertension and HbA1c; hyperuricemia was defined from serum uric acid using sex-specific thresholds, while gout relied on self-reported professional diagnosis. In Model 3, each 1 mg/kg/min higher eGDR was associated with lower odds of both outcomes. For gout, the 4–6 mg/kg/min category did not differ significantly from the <4 reference after full adjustment. This exception is explicit in the body and Table 3 and prevents adopting the abstract's universal category-significance claim. The analysis concerns prevalent disease, with residual confounding, self-report and temporal-order limitations.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Secondary cross-sectional analysis of six NHANES survey cycles, 2007–2018
- Evidence Type: Survey-weighted observational association study; no intervention or prospective incidence evaluation
- Unique total: 29,340
- Population: NHANES adults aged at least 20 years, excluding pregnant participants and those missing eGDR, gout or uric-acid information
- Primary Endpoint: Prevalent hyperuricemia: serum uric acid >7.0 mg/dL in males or >6.0 mg/dL in females
- Secondary Endpoint: Self-reported prior diagnosis of gout by a doctor or other healthcare professional
- External validation: No
- Data modalities: NHANES examination and serum biomarker data, Questionnaire-reported diagnoses and lifestyle measures, Survey-design variables and sampling weights

## What This Study Adds

- Places a composite insulin-resistance proxy alongside measured hyperuricemia and self-reported gout in one weighted population survey analysis.
- Separates continuous association from category-specific evidence; Table 3 does not support a universal significant gout-category effect.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Adjusted prevalence-odds associations in the selected NHANES 2007–2018 adult sample.
- The explicit nonsignificant fully adjusted 4–6 mg/kg/min gout contrast.

### Does Not Establish

- That increasing eGDR prevents hyperuricemia or gout, or reduces future disease incidence.
- Causal mediation by insulin resistance or benefit from an eGDR-targeted intervention.
- A statistically significant adjusted gout association for every eGDR category.
- Prospective prediction accuracy or a clinical treatment threshold.

### Limitations

- Known source discrepancy: Abstract Results implies all higher eGDR categories significantly reduce both outcomes. Table 3 and Results §3.3 instead identify the fully adjusted gout 4–6 contrast as nonsignificant (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.63–1.04, P=0.093). This page follows the mutually consistent body/table contrast and does not claim the abstract has been formally corrected.
- Cross-sectional associations cannot establish causality, incident risk reduction or prevention; odds ratios are not prevalence ratios.
- Gout ascertainment is self-reported; eGDR estimates insulin resistance rather than directly measuring clamp-derived glucose disposal.
- Covariate adjustment and imputation do not exclude residual confounding or selection bias.
- Supplementary subgroup, interaction and sensitivity estimates are not used as independently verified key findings in this bounded evidence page.

## Q&A

### How many participants and which outcomes were analyzed?

One analytic sample of 29,340 adults was used for both outcomes; Tables 2 and 3 list 5,433 hyperuricemia and 1,346 self-reported gout cases. These case counts must not be substituted for survey-weighted prevalence.

Evidence: KF1

### Was every higher eGDR category significantly associated with less gout?

No. Table 3 Model 3 gives OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.63–1.04 and P=0.093 for 4–6 versus <4 mg/kg/min. Results §3.3 states this loss of significance. The abstract's broader all-category statement is not adopted.

Evidence: KF3

### What did the continuous adjusted analyses report?

Per 1 mg/kg/min higher eGDR, Model 3 reports OR 0.83 (95% CI 0.81–0.85) for hyperuricemia and OR 0.83 (0.79–0.87) for gout. These are prevalence-odds associations.

Evidence: KF2, KF3

### Does this demonstrate that raising eGDR will prevent disease?

No. The cross-sectional design cannot establish temporal order or a preventive intervention effect. Gout is self-reported and eGDR is a calculated proxy.

Evidence: KF1, KF4
