Official Abstract

Background

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have revolutionized the treatment of many malignant tumors. However, ICI-induced hyper-immune activation causes cardiotoxicity. Traditional treatments such as glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants have limited effectiveness and may even accelerate tumor growth. This study aimed to identify approaches that effectively reduce cardiotoxicity and simultaneously preserve or enhance the antitumor immunity of ICI therapy.

Methods

ICI injection in melanoma-bearing C57BL/6J female mice was used to simulate cardiotoxicity in patients with tumor undergoing immune therapy. MCC950 was used to block nod-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome activity. Echocardiography, immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, and reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction were used to assess cardiac function, immune cell populations, and inflammatory factor levels. Bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing was used to detect the changes in cardiac transcriptome and immunological network.

Results

NLRP3 inhibition reduced inflammatory response and improved cardiac function. Notably, NLRP3 inhibition also resulted in a pronounced suppression of tumor growth. Single-cell RNA sequencing elucidated that MCC950 treatment reduced the cardiac infiltration of pathogenic macrophages, cytotoxic T cells, activated T cells, and their production of inflammatory cytokines, while enhancing the presence of reparative macrophages and naive T cells. In addition, MCC950 attenuated cardiotoxicity induced by dual programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) immunotherapy and promoted tumor regression, and showed efficacy in treating established cardiotoxicity.

Conclusions

Our findings provide a promising clinical approach for preventing and treating cardiotoxicity induced by ICIs, dissociating the antitumor efficacy of ICI-based therapies from their cardiotoxic side effects.

Research Question

Does NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition mitigate immune-checkpoint-inhibitor cardiac injury while reshaping cardiac immune-cell states and preserving or enhancing antitumor activity in the tested preclinical melanoma models?

Author Evidence Summary

The study tested NLRP3 inhibition with MCC950 in female C57BL/6J mice bearing B16F10 melanoma during anti-PD-1 or dual anti-PD-1 plus anti-CTLA-4 treatment. Cardiac biomarkers, echocardiography, histology, immune profiling and transcriptomics supported preclinical effects on cardiac injury and inflammatory states. Cardiac single-cell analysis comprised 68,058 cells from a pooled design with six hearts per sample; these cells are not independent mouse replicates. MCC950 altered macrophage and T-cell states, and the experiments included macrophage depletion, a dual-ICI comparison with n=8 per group, and a mouse treatment-after-injury protocol. Public GSE180045 human PBMC data supplied supportive NLRP3/IL1B associations only. Cardiac and tumor effects in these mouse models do not establish human treatment efficacy, safety or approved clinical use.

What This Study Adds

  • Experimental evidence for NLRP3 involvement in ICI-associated cardiac injury in tested mouse models.
  • Multimodal preclinical evaluation of MCC950 cardiac and tumor phenotypes.
  • Cardiac macrophage/T-cell state profiling and mechanism-supporting depletion experiments.
  • Dual-ICI and mouse treatment-after-injury experiments, alongside supportive human PBMC associations.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Experimental NLRP3 involvement in ICI-associated cardiac injury in the tested mouse models.
  • Preclinical cardiac effects of MCC950.
  • Cardiac macrophage/T-cell state remodeling.
  • Preclinical dual-ICI experiments.
  • Supportive human PBMC single-cell association.
  • A preclinical mouse treatment-after-injury experiment.

Does Not Establish

  • MCC950 efficacy in human ICI myocarditis or cardiotoxicity.
  • Safety in humans or approved clinical use.
  • Human antitumor efficacy or universal effects across tumor models.
  • Clinical efficacy of combining MCC950 with ICIs.
  • Independent biological replication from each of the 68,058 scRNA cells.
  • Definitive attribution of all effects to one canonical NLRP3 pathway.

Limitations

  • The intervention evidence comes from tested preclinical melanoma models, with no human interventional cohort.
  • The paper notes that ICI dose/frequency may exceed typical clinical dosing and calls for additional tumor models and clinical evaluation.
  • Pooled cardiac single-cell samples limit interpretation of cell counts as biological replication.
  • GSE180045 provides supportive human association, not independent validation of MCC950 treatment efficacy.
  • The findings do not definitively attribute all effects to one canonical NLRP3 pathway.

Q&A

What model was used to study ICI cardiotoxicity?

B16F10-bearing female C57BL/6J mice were studied under anti-PD-1 treatment; the study also included a dual anti-PD-1 plus anti-CTLA-4 experiment. Human GSE180045 data provided supportive associations.

Evidence: KF1, KF6

What cardiac effects were observed after MCC950 in mice?

The tested anti-PD-1 mouse experiments reported mitigation of cardiac injury across cTnT, echocardiography, immune infiltration, apoptosis, fibrosis and inflammatory/chemokine readouts.

Evidence: KF2

What does the human GSE180045 analysis contribute?

Public human PBMC single-cell data show supportive NLRP3/IL1B patterns in monocytes/neutrophils associated with ICI use/myocarditis. These are associations, not a human MCC950 intervention or efficacy-validation cohort.

Evidence: KF1

What did cardiac scRNA-seq show?

The analysis described 68,058 cells, 14 clusters and eight immune classes. Anti-PD-1 increased Macrophages1/3, while MCC950 reduced them and increased reparative Macrophages2. Six hearts per sample were pooled; cells are not independent mice.

Evidence: KF4

What did macrophage depletion contribute mechanistically?

Clodronate depletion effects and TNF/CXCL/CCL communication changes supported a macrophage/T-cell inflammatory network in the mouse model, without definitively assigning every effect to one canonical NLRP3 pathway.

Evidence: KF5

What happened in the dual-ICI mouse experiment?

With n=8 per group, MCC950 mitigated cardiac phenotypes during anti-PD-1 plus anti-CTLA-4 treatment and was associated with reduced tumor burden in the tested B16F10 model.

Evidence: KF6

Does this study establish MCC950 as a human treatment?

No. The intervention, tumor and treatment-after-injury findings are preclinical. The latter was a mouse treatment-after-injury experiment with ameliorated echocardiographic impairment, and does not establish human efficacy, safety or approved use.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3, KF6, KF7
