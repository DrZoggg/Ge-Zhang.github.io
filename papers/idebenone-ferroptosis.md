## Official Abstract

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the leading causes of mortality worldwide. As a type of CVDs, myocardial infarction (MI) induces ischemia hypoxia, which leads to excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS), resulting in multiple cell deaths and contributing to the subsequent development of heart failure or premature death. Recent evidence indicates that ROS-induced lipid peroxidation promotes autophagy and ferroptosis, leading to the loss of healthy myocardium and resulting in the dysfunction of cardiac tissue. Theoretically, cardiac function would be preserved after MI by inhibiting autophagy and ferroptosis. As an analog of coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) and a clinically approved drug, idebenone would be used to inhibit ferroptosis and preserve cardiac function due to its capacity to improve mitochondrial physiology with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Here, we confirmed that the addition of idebenone inhibited H2O2-induced and RSL3-induced ferroptosis. Furthermore, the ROS-AMPK-mTOR pathway axis was identified as the signaling pathway that idebenone stimulated to prevent excessive autophagy and consequent ferroptosis. In the MI animal model, idebenone demonstrated a cardioprotective role by regulating ROS-dependent autophagy and inhibiting ferroptosis, which paves the way for the future clinical translation of idebenone in MI management.

## Research Question

What cellular and mouse evidence supports idebenone-associated reduction of ferroptotic injury, and how does pretreatment timing limit translation to post-infarction clinical treatment?

## Author Evidence Summary

This preclinical study used H9c2 injury models, pharmacologic pathway probes and LAD-ligation mouse experiments. Oxidative-injury, ferroptosis and autophagy readouts are distinguished from cardiac-function readouts. The animal regimen included dosing before surgery and is not evidence for treatment initiated only after infarction. The findings do not establish human myocardial-infarction efficacy, safety or an approved clinical indication.

## How the Study Was Done

### Study profile

- Study Design: Cellular and mouse intervention study with pharmacologic pathway probes
- Evidence Type: Preclinical functional and mechanism-supporting evidence
- Population: H9c2 cellular models and experimental myocardial-infarction mice
- Primary Endpoint: Experimental cell injury and mouse cardiac function
- Secondary Endpoint: Ferroptosis/autophagy-associated and histological readouts
- External dataset evaluation: No
- Data modalities: cell viability, oxidative/iron/lipid-peroxidation assays, protein and autophagy readouts, mouse echocardiography, histology

## What This Study Adds

- Links cellular and animal experimental observations while retaining their different evidence levels.
- Makes the pretreatment-containing intervention timing explicit.
- Separates functional outcomes from mechanistic support.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Protective observations under the specified cellular conditions.
- Experimental support for mechanism involvement.
- Mouse cardiac-function and tissue readouts under a regimen that includes preoperative dosing.

### Does Not Establish

- Human MI efficacy, safety or an approved MI indication.
- Clinical rescue efficacy when treatment begins only after myocardial infarction.
- That all autophagy should be inhibited or all cell death is ferroptosis.
- Complete exclusion of other protective mechanisms or direct drug binding to AMPK or mTOR.
- Human survival benefit inferred from mouse EF changes.

### Limitations

- Evidence is cellular and animal, not proof of human MI efficacy, safety or an approved MI indication; background use in other diseases is not an MI indication.
- Preoperative dosing limits extrapolation to treatment begun only after infarction.
- Pharmacologic and marker-based support does not show that all autophagy should be suppressed, that all cell death is ferroptosis or that other mechanisms are excluded.
- The experiments do not establish direct idebenone binding to AMPK or mTOR.
- EF percentages are measured functional values, not relative survival gains or clinical utility estimates.
- Detailed findings are attributed to the DOI-bearing author-deposited full text; not every PDF image or supplementary dataset was independently reviewed.
- An open-license label on ResearchGate alone is not treated as publisher-abstract reuse authorization; no Official Abstract is copied in this batch.

## When This Study Is Useful to Cite

### CU1: What preclinical studies link idebenone to ferroptotic cardiac injury?

H9c2 H2O2-injury experiments reported pretreatment-associated improvements in viability, ROS and lipid-peroxidation readouts. RSL3, GPX4/FTH1/FPN1 and DFO/ferrostatin-1-related controls support ferroptosis involvement, without making all oxidative cell death exclusively ferroptotic or establishing a complete specific causal chain.

Evidence: [KF1](#kf1), [KF2](#kf2)

### CU2: What pharmacological evidence connects autophagy and AMPK-mTOR?

Metformin/rapamycin experiments, p-AMPK/AMPK and p-mTOR/mTOR readouts, and LC3/p62 observations support the proposed pathway. Pharmacologic perturbations do not replace full gene-specific validation or imply that every instance of ferroptosis requires autophagy.

Evidence: [KF3](#kf3)

### CU3: How does pretreatment timing affect interpretation of cardioprotection?

The LAD-ligation regimen included idebenone 24 h and 2 h before surgery, then continued dosing; it was not exclusively post-infarction initiation. Four-week EF was reported as 21.82% for MI and 33.76% for MI+idebenone, with Fig. 8 n=5 and qualitative histological/fibrosis improvement. These are EF values in a pretreatment-containing mouse experiment, not relative survival improvements or proven clinical rescue efficacy.

Evidence: [KF4](#kf4), [KF5](#kf5)

### CU4: What myocardial readouts accompany the experimental functional effect?

Myocardial ROS/MDA/Fe2+, GPX4/FTH1/FPN1, LC3/p62 and tissue observations changed with intervention. Different materials and timepoints do not form a verified continuous causal chain in an individual patient; no new effect sizes are recalculated from ambiguous fold-change wording.

Evidence: [KF6](#kf6)
