Evidence Scale

A redox-responsive reversible photoacoustic molecular probe for visualizing atherosclerotic plaque redox status in vivo

A BODIPY-peptide redox probe was evaluated in solution and a small atherosclerosis mouse experiment, using reversible PA intensity changes at 740 nm and accompanying histology. The evidence supports the tested preclinical imaging approach, without human diagnostic, treatment or safety validation.

Research Question

Can a reversibly redox-responsive BODIPY-peptide probe generate interpretable PA signal changes under controlled superoxide/GSH conditions and track aortic-region changes after LPS challenge and dexamethasone treatment in atherosclerotic mice?

Author Evidence Summary

The study synthesizes ReP from a BODIPY precursor and a hydrophilic peptide, retaining catechol-based redox-responsive sites. Solution spectroscopy and calculations support the authors' oxidation/reduction interpretation; superoxide increases PA signal and GSH decreases it. The reported aqueous hydrodynamic size is approximately 69 nm for ReP versus 378 nm for the precursor. At 740 nm, displayed PA calibrations have R-squared values of 0.977 for superoxide and 0.982 for GSH, and three alternating response cycles are shown. The animal comparison uses male ApoE-/- mice after 16 weeks of high-fat feeding, with n=3 per main group. The aortic-region PA signal is higher after an intranasal LPS challenge and lower after the reported dexamethasone regimen. Lung wet/dry measurements and CD68, caspase-3 and collagen staining provide accompanying tissue observations. These are chemical and animal results: the study contains no cell-culture validation or human clinical cohort, does not give an absolute in vivo superoxide calibration, and does not establish clinical plaque-risk prediction, dexamethasone benefit in patients or human probe safety.

Key Findings

KF1 ReP combines a BODIPY scaffold, a hydrophilic peptide segment and catechol-based redox-responsive sites; the authors attribute its reversible optical response to oxidation by superoxide and reduction by GSH.

The response mechanism is supported by the depicted molecular transformation, solution spectra and molecular-orbital calculations. It is not a demonstrated intracellular signaling pathway or proof of superoxide-specific causal biology in patients.

How the Study Was Done

Study profile

What This Study Adds

  • A linked chemical-to-animal evaluation of a BODIPY-peptide probe designed for reversible redox-sensitive photoacoustic imaging.
  • Solution concentration-response relationships and three switching cycles, reported separately from relative aortic-region signal measurements.
  • A small experimental comparison of the probe response during inflammatory challenge and an anti-inflammatory regimen, accompanied by lung and plaque tissue readouts.
  • Explicit separation of reported experimental imaging results from absolute in vivo analyte quantification, rupture prediction, human therapeutic efficacy and established safety.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • The reported solution response to superoxide/GSH, bounded by the stated solvent, concentrations, wavelength and interferent panel.
  • Relative aortic-region PA signal differences in the stated small ApoE-/- mouse comparison.
  • The displayed ex vivo lung and plaque staining observations and limited short-term tolerability observations.

Does Not Establish

  • Absolute superoxide concentration in living plaques or a validated dual-wavelength PA ratio.
  • Clinical sensitivity, specificity, risk discrimination, prediction of plaque rupture or reduced cardiovascular events.
  • Human treatment efficacy of dexamethasone or clinical safety, regulatory approval or routine applicability of ReP.
  • A universal biological selectivity claim or complete causal demonstration of every proposed lung-to-plaque mechanism.

Limitations

  • The evidence consists of chemical solution experiments and mouse imaging/histology. No cell-culture validation, human participants or clinical diagnostic-validation cohort is reported.
  • The main AS comparison has n=3 mice per group. Assay-specific measurements, repeated imaging days, tissue sections and the additional S7 sequence must not be summed into a synthetic unique animal total.
  • The PA calibration and in vivo result panels use intensity at 740 nm. Although introductory text uses the term ratiometric, a validated two-wavelength normalization ratio is not specified for the displayed experiments.
  • In vivo signals are relative aortic-region observations, not measured absolute superoxide concentrations. Probe distribution, tissue optical properties and endogenous signal are not eliminated as potential contributors by a universal biological-specificity test.
  • Solution selectivity applies to the reported 80 micromolar challenge panel. It does not establish complete selectivity in all tissues, across all reductants, or under every biological condition.
  • The figures supply calibration R-squared values but no explicit PA-calibration replicate count, limit of detection, limit of quantification, exact regression slope or calibration uncertainty. No values are reconstructed from plotted bars.
  • Fig. 6 heatmap percentages have no stated per-animal dispersion or statistical test. They are descriptive annotations, not independently calculated means, confidence intervals or significance claims.
  • Random assignment is described in the supplement; blinding, allocation concealment and an a priori sample-size calculation are not reported in the available text.
  • LPS/dexamethasone intervention supports experimental group contrasts; it does not independently prove every step of the proposed lung-to-systemic-oxidative-stress-to-plaque cascade, prevent rupture or demonstrate patient benefit.
  • Short-term body-weight and organ-histology observations have an unreported independent sample count and do not establish chronic, repeated-dose or human safety.
  • Supplementary affiliation/equal-contribution formatting differs from the final main-PDF author footnotes. Full author order, title, DOI and publication date agree between the main PDF, publisher and Crossref; the main version of record governs identity.
  • Supplementary synthetic-route temperature labeling differs from the prose description. That synthesis-temperature detail is not promoted into the evidence summary, and no unsupported protocol reconciliation is made.

Q&A

What experimental systems were actually studied?

Controlled chemical solutions and mice were studied. The main atherosclerosis model uses male ApoE-/- mice after 16 weeks of high-fat feeding; the study does not report a cell-culture validation experiment or a human cohort.

Evidence: KF3, KF5, KF7

How does ReP respond to superoxide and glutathione?

The authors assign the optical switching to catechol-based oxidation by superoxide and reduction by GSH. Superoxide increases the 740 nm PA response and GSH attenuates it; three alternating cycles are shown in solution.

Evidence: KF1, KF4

What quantitative PA calibration was reported?

The displayed superoxide PA calibration spans 0 to 80 micromolar and has R-squared 0.977. The GSH panel spans 0 to 300 micromolar and has R-squared 0.982. The separate superoxide absorption titration extends to 125 micromolar; the ranges must not be conflated.

Evidence: KF3

Was an absolute or ratiometric plaque redox measurement validated?

The displayed animal metric is relative PA intensity in an aortic-region ROI at 740 nm. Although introductory text uses ratiometric language, the figures do not define a validated two-wavelength ratio or convert animal signal into an absolute plaque superoxide concentration.

Evidence: KF3, KF5

Which inflammatory and treatment conditions were tested in mice?

The main groups are AS plus PBS, AS plus LPS, and AS plus LPS followed by dexamethasone, with n=3 per group. LPS is 50 micrograms in 50 microliters PBS intranasally on days 0 and 1. Dexamethasone is 2 mg/kg/day intraperitoneally from day 2 to day 8; ReP is 300 microliters at 200 micromolar via tail vein.

Evidence: KF5

What did histology add to the imaging comparison?

Lung wet/dry measurements and H&E support the pulmonary-inflammatory model. Plaque CD68, caspase-3 and Masson staining show group differences; the Fig. 6 percentages are descriptive annotations without supplied per-animal uncertainty or inferential tests.

Evidence: KF6

Does the study show fewer ruptures or clinical treatment benefit?

No rupture-prevention or human cardiovascular-event trial is reported. PA signal and tissue staining in these mouse groups do not establish dexamethasone benefit in patients, clinical diagnostic performance or event prediction.

Evidence: KF5, KF6

What safety conclusion can be supported?

The authors report seven-day body-weight observations and major-organ histology in mice. The independent tolerability n is not supplied; these descriptive results do not establish chronic, repeated-dose or human safety.

Evidence: KF7
