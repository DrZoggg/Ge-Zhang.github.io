### Counting note

The two database totals are not added because reporting-system coverage can overlap and no cross-database patient deduplication is established. Reports, PT/SOC entries, outcome categories and the timed subset are different units. S4 Preferred Term!I2:I2073 sums to 17,094, and I+J is 17,094 for each populated PT row; this does not establish 17,094 unique patients or reports. Time-bin percentages use either 2,209 usable-onset reports or all 5,596 FAERS reports, and their denominators are retained explicitly. Cross-database comparison is not independent external validation of a clinical risk estimate.

## Research Question

What suspected-event reporting patterns, organ-system and preferred-term signals, and reported onset times characterize aliskiren in FAERS and WHO-VigiAccess, and which evidence units govern their interpretation?

## Author Evidence Summary

This retrospective pharmacovigilance study describes 5,596 aliskiren-associated FAERS reports and 5,549 VigiAccess reports. It applies ROR, PRR, BCPNN and MGPS signal-detection approaches and organizes terms using MedDRA. The published summaries identify shared signals in investigations, cardiac, renal/urinary, vascular and metabolism/nutrition categories. Frequency-ranked terms include blood pressure increased and blood creatinine increased, while very large printed reporting ratios can arise from rare terms with only a few reports. Among 2,209 reports with usable onset dates, the median reported onset was 62 days (IQR 7-282), with 861 reports within 30 days. This provides a source-located example of cross-database safety-signal research. The public supplements also show why units matter: VigiAccess preferred-term counts sum to 17,094, distinct from its 5,549-report descriptive total. Published window, outcome-count and some signal-estimate discrepancies remain explicit when interpreting individual results. The findings generate pharmacovigilance hypotheses rather than estimates of event incidence among aliskiren users.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Retrospective cross-database analysis of aliskiren-associated spontaneous reports. FAERS extraction is described through OpenVigil 2.1 with primary-suspect selection and latest-FDA_DT deduplication for the same CASEID. MedDRA 26.0 standardization precedes descriptive, disproportionality and time-to-onset analyses.
- Evidence Type: Descriptive spontaneous-report characteristics; ROR/PRR and Bayesian BCPNN/MGPS reporting-signal measures; report-based time-to-onset summaries.
- Population: Aliskiren-associated suspected-event reports in FAERS and public WHO-VigiAccess. Primary hypertension is the treatment context; this is not an enrolled or exposure-denominated hypertension patient cohort.
- Primary Endpoint: Database-specific SOC and PT reporting patterns and disproportionality signals.
- Secondary Endpoint: Submitted-report demographics/outcomes and onset-time distribution among records with usable dates.
- External dataset evaluation: No
- Data modalities: FAERS spontaneous adverse-event reports, Public VigiAccess aggregate reporting data, MedDRA preferred terms and system organ classes, Report characteristics and outcome categories, Reported treatment-start/event-onset dates

## What This Study Adds

- Places aliskiren-associated spontaneous reporting in a comparative FAERS/VigiAccess framework with explicit SOC/PT organization.
- Provides a useful frequency-versus-signal-strength example, including rare terms with high reporting ratios and wide intervals.
- Connects report-level timing and demographic summaries to the underlying observation units, enabling more precise methodological comparison and follow-up hypothesis design.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Source-specific descriptions of submitted aliskiren-associated reports and the article's published reporting-signal summaries.
- Comparison of the reported organ-system patterns, PT frequency/intensity tables and a complete-date onset subset.
- Methodological discussion of spontaneous-report denominators, missingness, multiple signal algorithms and cross-source reproducibility.

### Does Not Establish

- Incidence, absolute or comparative patient risk, a causal adverse-drug reaction, or the relative safety of aliskiren versus another treatment.
- A pooled independent cohort from the two database totals, or event-probability/hazard estimates from cumulative percentages of submitted reports.
- New prescribing restrictions, screening recommendations or proof that an unlisted event is an established aliskiren adverse reaction.

### Limitations

- Spontaneous reports are subject to missingness, reporting differences, confounding and possible overlap between databases; there is no exposed-patient denominator for incidence or causal risk.
- The main FAERS window is internally inconsistent: Methods 2004 Q1-2025 Q3, Table 1 caption Q4 2023-Q4 2024 and annual entries 2005-2025 in S3.
- Table 1 reports 1,037 Serious records but 1,735 Hospitalization entries and 3,080 Other Serious Medical Events; the report-versus-outcome relationship is not sufficiently explained to reconcile these counts.
- S4 preferred-term comparison cells use a 17,094 target-drug term-count total rather than the 5,549 descriptive reports, and some stored estimates differ from printed main-table values; exact quantitative signal ranking requires clarification.
- Onset dates are missing or invalid for 3,387 FAERS reports (60.53%). Unequal or open-ended time bins and a cumulative report-time curve do not establish a bimodal patient hazard.

## Q&A

### What is the observation unit in this cross-database study?

The main descriptive totals are 5,596 FAERS and 5,549 VigiAccess aliskiren-associated spontaneous reports. They are not exposed-patient denominators and should not be pooled as an independent patient cohort. A report can contribute more than one event term or outcome category.

Evidence: KF1

### How were the reports standardized and signals identified?

The article describes FAERS primary-suspect selection, latest-record deduplication by CASEID/FDA_DT and MedDRA 26.0 PT/SOC standardization. It uses ROR, PRR, BCPNN and MGPS. Main text thresholds include a lower 95% ROR limit above 1, PRR at least 2 with chi-square at least 4, IC025 above 0 and EBGM above 2. S3 additionally specifies at least 3 reports and EBGM05 above 2, so the point-estimate versus lower-bound criterion remains source-specific.

Evidence: KF1

### Which organ-system reporting patterns were shared?

The published tables identify investigations, cardiac, renal/urinary, vascular and metabolism/nutrition disorders as the shared signal categories. For example, the printed renal/urinary RORs are 3.30 (95%CI 3.12-3.50) in FAERS and 3.27 (95%CI 3.06-3.50) in VigiAccess. These are reporting associations, with the comparison-cell and denominator issues retained in provenance.

Evidence: KF2

### Why should frequency and reporting-ratio rankings be read separately?

Blood pressure increased is frequent in both tables, with 395 FAERS and 465 VigiAccess entries. In contrast, the FAERS paradoxical pressor response estimate is based on 3 reports but has a printed ROR of 775.96 and a wide 95% CI 216.46-2781.64. High disproportionality is not the same as common occurrence or greater clinical severity.

Evidence: KF3

### What does the reported 62-day onset median mean?

It describes 2,209 reports with usable onset dates, with IQR 7-282 days. The 861 reports within 30 days are 38.98% of that subset but 15.39% of all 5,596 FAERS reports. Figure 6 plots cumulative percent of reported onset times; there is no exposed-user cohort or at-risk follow-up denominator for a cumulative-incidence interpretation.

Evidence: KF4

### Can the demographic tables identify higher-risk age or sex groups?

They describe the demographics of submitted reports. Age is missing or unknown in 49.32% of FAERS and 46.17% of VigiAccess reports. Without age/sex-specific exposure denominators and control of reporting differences, the older-age and female reporting counts cannot establish which aliskiren users have higher clinical risk.

Evidence: KF5

### Which source discrepancies matter for reproducing the analyses?

Methods give FAERS 2004 Q1-2025 Q3, whereas Table 1's caption gives Q4 2023-Q4 2024 and S3 contains annual rows 2005-2025. Table 1's Serious count 1,037 is not reconciled with its Hospitalization count 1,735. S4 uses a 17,094 term-count total; for blood pressure increased it stores ROR 13.005587 at M2, while main Table 5 prints 16.73 with the same n 465. These are recorded separately rather than averaged or silently corrected.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3

### How is this study useful for a safety-research discussion?

It is useful for discussing cross-database signal generation, frequency versus disproportionality, report missingness and the need to preserve the unit and denominator of each safety summary. Suspected signals can motivate further clinical or epidemiologic investigation, while this design itself does not establish drug causation or a prescribing recommendation.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3, KF4, KF5
