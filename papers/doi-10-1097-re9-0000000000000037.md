## Author evidence note

### Review question

This review examines how microbial processes and host characteristics can be combined to study cardiovascular disease, separating vascular and cardiac pathways.

Source: [Publisher HTML, Abstract, sole paragraph, sentences 1-5.](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/cares/fulltext/10.1097/re9.0000000000000037~multimodal-artificial-intelligence-for-microbiome-informed)

### What the review contributes

It organizes integration of microbial profiles, genomics and metabolomics with imaging and repeated clinical observations, and compares approaches for combining these data. A central distinction is between support for a mechanism and the maturity of a biomarker. The review prioritizes reproducible cardiovascular subtypes and microbial candidates for subsequent intervention studies.

Source: [Publisher HTML, Abstract, sole paragraph, sentences 5-7 and 10.](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/cares/fulltext/10.1097/re9.0000000000000037~multimodal-artificial-intelligence-for-microbiome-informed)

### Choose a fusion strategy

Table 2 compares feature-level early fusion, prediction-level late fusion and intermediate or hybrid representations. Early fusion suits well-aligned, largely complete modalities with adequate sample size; late fusion supports heterogeneous data, intermittent missingness and modular deployment. Intermediate or hybrid fusion targets cross-modal interactions when sample size and missing-modality training support the design. The choice depends on alignment, dimensionality, modality completeness and validation rather than a universally best architecture.

Source: [Publisher accepted manuscript, PDF p. 66, Table 2; p. 20, section 5.2, Data preparation, multimodal fusion, and model development.](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/cares/pdf/10.1097/re9.0000000000000037~multimodal-artificial-intelligence-for-microbiome-informed#page=66)

### Benchmark incremental value

For microbiome-informed cardiovascular model development, the review recommends starting with a clinical baseline, adding relevant modalities step by step, and testing a simple multimodal model before higher-capacity AI. Clinical, unimodal and simple multimodal models should use the same data partitions. Evaluation includes discrimination, calibration, uncertainty, subgroup stability and decision utility; external validation examines transport across populations, sites, assay platforms and clinical settings, including performance when modalities are missing.

Source: [Publisher accepted manuscript, PDF pp. 20-21, sections 5.2-5.3, including Benchmarking, interpretability, and external validation.](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/cares/pdf/10.1097/re9.0000000000000037~multimodal-artificial-intelligence-for-microbiome-informed#page=20)

### Evidence scope and limitations

Source-bound narrative review summary using the publisher abstract and the publisher-hosted accepted manuscript, including multimodal fusion and model-evaluation guidance.

- This is a narrative review drawing on seminal and directly relevant literature available through August 2026, without a formal PRISMA selection process. Its fusion and validation guidance synthesizes existing evidence rather than reporting a newly trained model or an intervention trial.
