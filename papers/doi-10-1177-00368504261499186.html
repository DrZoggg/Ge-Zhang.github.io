Research Question

Is baseline EASIX associated with long-term all-cause and cardiovascular mortality among adults at CKM stages 0-3, and how do those associations vary by exposure shape, CKM group and analytic adjustment?

Author Evidence Summary

The study links NHANES 1999–2018 data for 10,885 adults aged at least 30 years to mortality through 31 December 2019. Over a median 97 months, there were 1,122 deaths, including 295 cardiovascular deaths. In survey-weighted Model 2, each raw EASIX unit was associated with an all-cause mortality HR of 1.41 (95% CI 1.24–1.61) and a cardiovascular mortality HR of 1.30 (1.13–1.49). The highest versus lowest sample quartile HRs were 1.55 (1.22–1.96) and 2.51 (1.54–4.07), respectively. The estimated cardiovascular association was steeper at lower values. EASIX generally had numerically higher discrimination than its individual components earlier in follow-up, whereas creatinine had higher AUCs at the longest times. Full-cohort interactions across CKM stages did not reach statistical significance. Positive associations were also reported after lifestyle and eGFR adjustment and in an age-restricted analysis.

Evidence Snapshot

EASIX and long-term mortality in CKM stages 0-3: a survey-weighted NHANES analysis

Among 10,885 NHANES adults at cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) stages 0–3, higher baseline endothelial activation and stress index (EASIX) was associated with all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. The study describes the associations across CKM stages and compares the index with its individual laboratory components.

Study Design & Analytical Framework

Study profile

Study Design
Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected NHANES 1999-2018 baseline data linked to mortality through 2019; complete-case primary survey-weighted Cox analysis with spline, subgroup and sensitivity analyses.
Evidence Type
Population-based observational mortality association; descriptive time-dependent discrimination against individual laboratory components.
Unique total
10,885
Population
US civilian noninstitutionalized adults aged ≥30 years with valid fasting-subsample weight, baseline CKM stages 0-3, EASIX components, linked mortality and complete primary covariates. CKM stage 4 excluded.
Primary Endpoint
The two mortality outcomes were all-cause and cardiovascular death, tracked from the baseline examination to death or 31 December 2019. Cardiovascular death used the linked underlying-cause classification.
Secondary Endpoint
Additional analyses characterized the shape, discrimination and CKM-group estimates for the same two mortality outcomes.
External validation
No

Data modalities

  • Survey demographics, medical history and examination
  • LDH, creatinine, platelet count and metabolic/renal laboratory measures
  • Prescription-medication inventory
  • Public-use linked mortality outcomes

What This Study Adds

  • Extends EASIX research to linked long-term mortality across CKM stages 0-3, using a readily computed three-laboratory index.
  • Provides survey-weighted continuous/quartile estimates and complementary shape, component and CKM-group analyses for risk-marker comparison.
  • Examines several adjustment/selection sensitivities while identifying renal overlap and the further evidence needed to establish clinical value.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Observed adjusted associations of baseline EASIX with linked all-cause and cardiovascular deaths in the selected NHANES cohort.
  • The reported spline shape, descriptive marker-discrimination comparisons and stage-specific estimates with their interaction/precision limits.
  • Main-text sensitivity summaries under additional adjustment, missing-data and age/competing-event analyses.

Does Not Establish

  • A specific endothelial mechanism or a causal effect of EASIX.
  • Incremental prediction over an established risk model, a clinical threshold or improved decisions and outcomes.
  • Applicability to CKM stage 4, younger adults, institutionalized populations or populations outside the United States.

Limitations

  • EASIX is a single baseline measurement combining LDH, creatinine and platelets. Residual confounding, including physical activity, and shared creatinine in EASIX and renal staging limit a specific endothelial interpretation.
  • The primary analysis excluded 1,037 otherwise eligible participants for missing covariates. Misclassification is possible for survey-derived CKM stages, self-reported clinical disease and cause-of-death coding.
  • Participants aged 80 or older were assigned age 79 for PREVENT. The age-restricted sensitivity analysis does not verify those older individual stage assignments.
  • Exact AUC points and subgroup values reported only in figures were unavailable for this summary. Supplemental Tables S1–S7 and Figure S1 were also unavailable. This limits verification of supplemental covariate distributions, full sensitivity tables and the competing-risk curve. The primary article reports the estimates cited above.

When This Study Is Useful to Cite

    The selected national cohort, EASIX definition, linked follow-up and explicitly adjusted mortality associations.

    Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF4

  2. CU2: What design details matter when interpreting an NHANES mortality association?

    Fasting-subsample survey design, raw versus weighted reporting, raw exposure units, adjustment sets, staging and age handling.

    Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3

  3. CU3: What do nonlinear and stage-specific EASIX findings add to risk-marker research?

    Reported spline shape, descriptive component discrimination and stage-specific estimates with actual interaction tests and precision.

    Evidence: KF5, KF6

  4. CU4: How robust are reported EASIX associations to alternative analytic choices?

    The reported main-text early-death, missing-data, lifestyle, eGFR, age-restriction and competing-risk sensitivity results.

    Evidence: KF7

What This Study Should Not Be Cited to Claim

  • An endothelial causal mechanism or benefit from changing EASIX.
  • A clinical screening threshold or prospective improvement over an established risk model.
  • A confirmed difference in prognostic performance across CKM stages.

Q&A

Who was analyzed and how long were outcomes tracked?

The primary cohort included 10,885 NHANES 1999–2018 participants aged at least 30 years at CKM stages 0–3. Mortality follow-up extended to 31 December 2019. Median follow-up was 97 months, with 1,122 deaths, including 295 cardiovascular deaths.

Evidence: KF1

How is EASIX calculated and scaled in the models?

EASIX is LDH (U/L) × creatinine (mg/dL) ÷ platelet count (10⁹/L). The components were measured at the baseline examination. Continuous HRs are per one raw unit. Sample-quartile boundaries were 0.340, 0.457 and 0.616.

Evidence: KF2, KF1

How were CKM stages and older ages handled?

The researchers assigned the highest qualifying stage using the 2023 AHA framework. Stage 3 used very-high-risk CKD or a 10-year PREVENT base total-CVD risk of at least 20%. Stage 4 used self-reported clinical CVD and was excluded. Participants aged 80 or older were assigned 79 for the primary PREVENT calculation. A sensitivity analysis restricted ages to 30–79.

Evidence: KF2, KF7

Which weights and covariates define the primary models?

The Cox models used combined fasting-subsample weights, strata, sampling units and design-based linearization variance. Model 1 included age group (under 60 or at least 60 years), sex, race and ethnicity, marital status, education and poverty-income ratio. Model 2 added current smoking, body mass index, systolic blood pressure, fasting glucose and antihypertensive, glucose-lowering and statin use.

Evidence: KF3

What mortality associations are reported in Table 2?

Model 2 gives per-unit HRs of 1.41 (95% CI 1.24–1.61) for all-cause and 1.30 (1.13–1.49) for cardiovascular mortality. The highest versus lowest quartile HRs were 1.55 (1.22–1.96) and 2.51 (1.54–4.07), respectively.

Evidence: KF4

What did the splines and AUC comparisons show?

The nonlinearity P value was 0.466 for all-cause and 0.008 for cardiovascular mortality. The text describes a steeper cardiovascular association at lower EASIX. EASIX generally had higher AUCs than its individual components early and midway in follow-up, while creatinine had higher AUCs at the longest times. These were descriptive comparisons.

Evidence: KF5

How did the mortality estimates vary across CKM groups?

In the full cohort, interaction P values were 0.062 for all-cause and 0.057 for cardiovascular mortality. The cardiovascular HR in stage 3 was 1.01 (95% CI 0.78–1.31), and stages 0–1 had 17 cardiovascular deaths. For ages 30–79 the P values were 0.047 and 0.068, respectively. The authors treat the age-restricted all-cause subgroup pattern as exploratory.

Evidence: KF6

What did additional adjustment and age restriction show?

After adjusting for alcohol, sleep and diet quality in a common complete-case subset, the HRs were 1.66 (95% CI 1.38–1.99) for all-cause and 1.43 (1.08–1.91) for cardiovascular mortality. With additional eGFR adjustment they were 1.40 (1.23–1.59) and 1.24 (1.08–1.43). The positive associations also persisted at ages 30–79. Renal function contributes to both EASIX and CKM staging, which limits attribution to a specific endothelial mechanism.

Evidence: KF7, KF2

Figure captions and corresponding main-text descriptions support the reported study flow, spline shape, discrimination comparison and subgroup methods. Exact graphical AUC points and additional plot-only values were unavailable.
Supporting Material Scope
The publisher lists supplemental Tables S1–S7 and Figure S1. These assets were unavailable for this summary. The article’s main Results supplies its reported sensitivity estimates and direction of the competing-risk comparison.
Source Note Scale And Models
Continuous HRs are per one raw EASIX unit. Model 1 uses age below 60 or at least 60 years. Table 2 lists the additional Model 2 covariates.
Source Note Stage Interpretation
Full-cohort CKM-stage interaction P values were 0.062 for all-cause and 0.057 for cardiovascular mortality. The exploratory age-restricted result is reported separately.
