## Official Abstract

### Background

The triglyceride-glucose (TYG) index is a novel and reliable marker reflecting insulin resistance. Its predictive ability for cardiovascular disease onset and prognosis has been confirmed. However, for advanced chronic heart failure (acHF) patients, the prognostic value of TYG is challenged due to the often accompanying renal dysfunction (RD). Therefore, this study focuses on patients with aHF accompanied by RD to investigate the predictive value of the TYG index for their prognosis.

### Methods and Results

717 acHF with RD patients were included. The acHF diagnosis was based on the 2021 ESC criteria for acHF. RD was defined as the eGFR < 90 mL/(min/1.73 m 2 ). Patients were divided into two groups based on their TYG index values. The primary endpoint was major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), and the secondary endpoints is all-cause mortality (ACM). The follow-up duration was 21.58 (17.98–25.39) months. The optimal cutoff values for predicting MACEs and ACM were determined using ROC curves. Hazard factors for MACEs and ACM were revealed through univariate and multivariate COX regression analyses. According to the univariate COX regression analysis, high TyG index was identified as a risk factor for MACEs (hazard ratio = 5.198; 95% confidence interval [CI], 3.702–7.298; P < 0.001) and ACM (hazard ratio = 4.461; 95% CI, 2.962–6.718; P < 0.001). The multivariate COX regression analysis showed that patients in the high TyG group experienced 440.2% MACEs risk increase (95% CI, 3.771–7.739; P < 0.001) and 406.2% ACM risk increase (95% CI, 3.268–7.839; P < 0.001). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that patients with high TyG index levels had an elevated risk of experiencing MACEs and ACM within 30 months.

### Conclusion

This study found that patients with high TYG index had an increased risk of MACEs and ACM, and the TYG index can serve as an independent predictor for prognosis.

## Research Question

Is the admission triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index associated with subsequent major adverse cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality in patients with advanced chronic heart failure and renal dysfunction?

## Author Evidence Summary

This study examined hospitalized patients with advanced chronic heart failure and renal dysfunction at the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University. Of 815 patients meeting the reported selection criteria, 98 were lost to follow-up and 717 were analyzed. Renal dysfunction was defined as eGFR below 90 mL/min/1.73 m², and the median follow-up was 21.58 months. Higher admission TyG was associated with the broad composite adverse-event endpoint and with all-cause mortality after multivariable adjustment. The adjusted hazard ratios for high versus low TyG were 5.402 for the composite endpoint and 5.062 for mortality. Outcome-specific thresholds of 8.48 and 8.50, respectively, were selected from ROC analyses in this same cohort. These observations support further study of a readily available metabolic marker in this patient group; they do not establish that lowering TyG improves outcomes or that these thresholds work in other settings.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Retrospective single-center observational cohort; admissions from September 2019 through December 2020, with follow-up completed in March 2022
- Evidence Type: Admission laboratory biomarker association with longitudinal outcomes, using outcome-derived ROC thresholds, Cox regression and Kaplan-Meier analyses
- Unique total: 717
- Population: Adults with advanced chronic heart failure, eGFR below 90 mL/min/1.73 m², and at least two heart-failure hospitalizations at the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University; 717 analyzed after 98 losses among 815 initially included
- Primary Endpoint: Reported MACEs composite: cardiovascular or non-cardiovascular death, worsening-HF rehospitalization, heart transplantation, mechanical circulatory support, malignant arrhythmia, myocardial infarction, or unstable-angina rehospitalization; this is broader than a conventional three-point MACE endpoint
- Secondary Endpoint: All-cause mortality during follow-up; reported median follow-up 21.58 months (interquartile range 17.98–25.39)
- External validation: No
- Data modalities: Retrospective inpatient and outpatient records, Admission fasting triglyceride and glucose measurements, Telephone and clinic follow-up, Clinical laboratory values and medication history

## What This Study Adds

- It examines a practical metabolic index in the specific setting of advanced chronic HF with renal dysfunction, a group in which metabolic biomarkers may be difficult to interpret.
- It reports distinct event and mortality thresholds with both discrimination estimates and adjusted associations, while retaining the same underlying cohort.
- It provides a hypothesis for externally validated cardiorenal risk research without demonstrating a benefit from a TyG-directed intervention.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- A prognostic association between admission TyG and subsequent outcomes in this selected retrospective cohort.
- Outcome-specific discrimination and hazard-ratio estimates under the study definitions and modeling choices.

### Does Not Establish

- Causality between insulin resistance and the observed events.
- That lowering triglyceride, glucose or TyG reduces mortality or rehospitalization.
- External validity of the same thresholds, prospective clinical utility, or superiority to a validated clinical decision pathway.
- A conventional three-point MACE endpoint: the study composite explicitly includes non-cardiovascular death and multiple HF-related outcomes.

### Limitations

- Single-center retrospective selection and residual confounding restrict causal and transportability claims.
- Thresholds were selected using the outcomes in the same dataset; no independent external validation was reported.
- Ninety-eight initially included patients were lost to follow-up, which may introduce attrition bias.
- The broad study-specific composite should not be treated as interchangeable with a standard three-point MACE outcome.
- Some narrative table cross-references do not match the displayed table captions. This page locates adjusted MACEs and ACM estimates by the actual Table 5 and Table 6 titles and anchors.
- A hazard ratio does not provide an absolute probability or demonstrate benefit from changing TyG.

## Q&A

### Who was included in the analyzed cohort?

The analysis included 717 adults with advanced chronic HF, study-defined renal dysfunction and repeated HF hospitalization at one center. The 815 initially included patients also contained 98 who were lost to follow-up.

Evidence: KF1

### Were the MACEs and mortality groups independent cohorts?

No. The two outcome-specific cutoffs regrouped the same 717 analyzed patients. Their group sizes must not be added together as independent participants.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF4

### What was the reported MACEs association?

High versus low TyG at the 8.48 threshold had an adjusted hazard ratio of 5.402 (95% CI 3.771–7.739). This is a relative hazard association, not an absolute event rate.

Evidence: KF2, KF3

### Was the composite limited to cardiovascular death, infarction and stroke?

No. The Methods define a broad composite including non-cardiovascular death, worsening-HF rehospitalization, transplantation, circulatory support and other events. Interpretation should preserve that study-specific definition.

Evidence: KF1, KF3

### Was the mortality association evaluated separately?

Yes. At the mortality-specific cutoff of 8.50, the adjusted hazard ratio was 5.062 (95% CI 3.268–7.839), with ROC AUC 0.700 in the same cohort.

Evidence: KF4

### Does the study show that treatment to lower TyG improves survival?

No. It is a retrospective observational association study, with thresholds chosen from the source cohort. Prospective and external studies are needed before using the findings to direct treatment.

Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3, KF4
