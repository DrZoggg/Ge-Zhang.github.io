Counting note

Counts have different denominators: human tissue samples, mice, pooled single-cell libraries, independent cultures or experiments. They cannot be summed as unique participants or independent biological replicates. Five mice pooled to one library per genotype are not five independent libraries; the generated single-cell experiment has conflicting Results and Figure 3E diet durations.

Research Question

Does KIF13B restrain experimental atherosclerosis by preserving macrophage MERTK-dependent efferocytosis through a post-translational ITCH/CBL regulatory network, and can pharmacologic intervention rescue impaired efferocytosis and atherosclerotic phenotypes in experimental models? Human tissue observations are associative; causal and pharmacologic tests are preclinical.

Author Evidence Summary

The study combined associative human arterial and carotid-plaque observations with mouse atherosclerosis genetics, public and study-generated single-cell/transcriptomic analyses, macrophage experiments, genetic rescue and preclinical pharmacologic intervention. KIF13B expression was lower in advanced human arterial/plaque tissue and progressing mouse plaques. Global Kif13b loss worsened atherosclerosis in Ldlr-deficient mice but increased circulating lipids in that model. Myeloid-specific deletion and Kif13b-deficient bone-marrow transplantation worsened lesion burden, necrotic cores, macrophage and apoptotic-cell accumulation without corresponding plasma-lipid changes, supporting a local myeloid/hematopoietic contribution in mice. Kif13b-deficient macrophages showed impaired efferocytosis and lower MERTK protein; restoring MERTK in deficient BMDMs improved efferocytosis. Unchanged Mertk mRNA, reduced MERTK protein stability, proteasome dependence, increased MERTK ubiquitination, KIF13B/ITCH interaction and ITCH/CBL/MERTK rescue results support a post-translational regulatory model, although the exact proposed ITCH-mediated CBL degradation step remains incompletely demonstrated. The article reported that NX-1607 improved MERTK/efferocytosis phenotypes in macrophages and reduced atherosclerotic phenotypes in mice. The article describes NX-1607 as a CBL inhibitor, whereas external authoritative drug-development sources identify it as a CBL-B/CBLB inhibitor; CBL and CBLB are distinct genes. Thus NX-1607 is reported as preclinical pharmacologic rescue, not unambiguous target validation of endogenous CBL. No human atherosclerosis treatment efficacy was tested.

Study Design & Analytical Framework

Study profile

Study Design
Mechanistic translational preclinical study combining associative human arterial and plaque observations with mouse atherosclerosis genetics, public and study-generated transcriptomics, macrophage experiments, genetic rescue and pharmacologic intervention.
Evidence Type
Human tissue association; causal mouse and macrophage experiments; public dataset reanalysis; preclinical pharmacologic rescue.
Population
Human arterial-wall and carotid-plaque tissue samples; Ldlr-deficient and Apoe-deficient mouse models; mouse bone-marrow-derived macrophages and THP-1 cells. No treated human atherosclerosis cohort.
Primary Endpoint
Experimental atherosclerotic lesion burden and macrophage efferocytosis across genetic and pharmacologic models.
Secondary Endpoint
Plaque necrotic core, macrophage and apoptotic-cell accumulation, circulating lipids, MERTK protein regulation and reported safety observations.
External dataset evaluation
Yes

Data modalities

  • Human tissue expression and immunostaining
  • Mouse plaque histology and plasma lipids
  • Public and pooled aortic single-cell RNA sequencing
  • BMDM RNA sequencing
  • Macrophage efferocytosis and protein assays
  • Genetic rescue and preclinical pharmacology

What This Study Adds

  • Connects lower KIF13B in human arterial and plaque tissue with experimental genetic evidence from mouse atherosclerosis without treating human associations as causal.
  • Separates the lipid-confounded global Ldlr-deficient knockout from myeloid conditional and marrow-transfer phenotypes that occurred without corresponding plasma-lipid changes.
  • Links KIF13B loss to impaired macrophage efferocytosis and lower MERTK protein, with cell-level MERTK restoration supporting functional involvement.
  • Proposes a post-translational KIF13B–ITCH–CBL–MERTK regulatory network while acknowledging that the precise ITCH-mediated CBL degradation step remains incompletely proven.
  • Reports preclinical NX-1607 rescue while explicitly separating article-described CBL inhibition from external CBL-B/CBLB target identification and from clinical efficacy.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • Association between lower KIF13B and advanced human arterial or carotid-plaque tissue, and progression in mouse plaque observations.
  • Causal worsening of mouse plaque phenotypes after global, myeloid or hematopoietic Kif13b perturbation within their respective experimental models.
  • A myeloid or hematopoietic contribution without corresponding plasma-lipid differences in conditional and transplantation models, separate from the lipid increase in global Ldlr-deficient knockout mice.
  • Reduced MERTK protein and efferocytosis after Kif13b loss and improvement of BMDM efferocytosis following MERTK restoration.
  • Evidence consistent with post-translational ITCH/CBL/MERTK regulation and article-reported NX-1607 rescue in macrophages and mice.

Does Not Establish

  • Human KIF13B causality, prediction of clinical outcomes or human atherosclerosis treatment efficacy.
  • That the global Ldlr-deficient knockout plaque phenotype is lipid independent.
  • Macrophage-exclusive effects of Lyz2-Cre deletion or bone-marrow transplantation, or five independent single-cell libraries from five pooled mice.
  • One definitive diet duration for the study-generated pooled aortic single-cell comparison, because Results and Figure 3E disagree.
  • That MERTK restoration is an effective in vivo or human therapy, or that every proposed ITCH-to-CBL ubiquitination and degradation step has been directly resolved.
  • That NX-1607 reverses 20 weeks of established disease, has proven human atherosclerosis benefit, or unequivocally validates endogenous CBL rather than the distinct CBLB target described by external sources.

Limitations

  • Human arterial-wall and carotid-plaque results are associative tissue observations with small carotid groups, not causal human intervention evidence.
  • The global Kif13b-deficient Ldlr model had higher circulating cholesterol and triglycerides, limiting attribution of its plaque phenotype solely to local plaque biology.
  • Lyz2-Cre targets myeloid lineages and donor-marrow transplantation changes hematopoietic compartments; neither isolates macrophages exclusively.
  • Study-generated single-cell data pooled five mice to one library per genotype, limiting independent biological replication at the library level.
  • The Results and Figure 3E give discordant 20-week Western-diet and 12-week high-fat-diet descriptions for the pooled aortic single-cell experiment.
  • GSE155513 Figure 3 time points use one single-cell library each; sample and library counts must not be conflated.
  • MERTK restoration rescue was demonstrated in Kif13b-deficient BMDMs, not as an in vivo MERTK treatment.
  • The exact ITCH-mediated CBL ubiquitination/degradation step in the proposed network remains incompletely demonstrated; MERTK regulation is not fully assigned to one mechanism.
  • The article's CBL-inhibitor description of NX-1607 conflicts with external CBL-B/CBLB target identification, preventing unambiguous pharmacologic validation of endogenous CBL.
  • NX-1607 was tested preclinically during the final 16 of 20 Western-diet weeks, not as reversal after 20 weeks of established disease; no human atherosclerosis efficacy was assessed.
  • Mouse NX-1607 measurements found no reported ALT/AST liver-toxicity signal but did report lower white blood cells and lymphocytes, so safety is not established.

When This Study Is Useful to Cite

  1. CU1: What evidence links macrophage KIF13B to atherosclerosis?

    Human tissue associations and mouse genetic/single-cell experiments link lower KIF13B to atherosclerosis; human association is not human causality and pooled libraries are not independent mice.

    Evidence: KF1, KF2, KF3, KF4

  2. CU2: How does KIF13B regulate macrophage efferocytosis through MERTK?

    KIF13B-deficient macrophages showed impaired efferocytosis and reduced MERTK protein, while MERTK restoration improved efferocytosis in deficient BMDMs.

    Evidence: KF5

  3. CU3: What evidence supports post-translational regulation of MERTK by the KIF13B–ITCH–CBL network?

    Accepted protein-turnover and rescue evidence supports this model, but the exact proposed ITCH-mediated CBL degradation step remains incompletely demonstrated.

    Evidence: KF6

  4. CU4: Does myeloid KIF13B affect experimental atherosclerosis independently of plasma lipid changes?

    Myeloid conditional deletion and marrow-transfer phenotypes occurred without corresponding lipid changes in the tested mouse models; the global Ldlr knockout is lipid-confounded.

    Evidence: KF2, KF4

  5. CU5: What preclinical pharmacologic rescue evidence was reported for impaired KIF13B/MERTK efferocytosis?

    NX-1607 produced macrophage and mouse rescue in the reported experiments; evidence is preclinical and CBL versus CBLB target identity remains qualified.

    Evidence: KF7

What This Study Should Not Be Cited to Claim

  • Human tissue association is not human KIF13B causality or treatment efficacy.
  • The global Ldlr-deficient knockout model is confounded by increased circulating lipids.
  • Five pooled mice in one single-cell library are not five independent library replicates.
  • The exact proposed ITCH-mediated CBL degradation mechanism remains incompletely demonstrated.
  • CBL and CBLB are distinct and must not be conflated when interpreting NX-1607.
  • NX-1607 rescue is preclinical; no human atherosclerosis treatment efficacy was established.

Q&A

What is the human evidence for KIF13B in atherosclerosis?

KIF13B was lower in 40 atherosclerotic versus 40 non-atherosclerotic arterial-wall samples in GSE40231 and in advanced versus early carotid lesions (n=6 tissue samples per group). These are tissue associations; no human KIF13B intervention or causal human effect was tested.

Evidence: KF1

Did Kif13b loss worsen mouse plaques independently of circulating lipids?

Global Kif13b deletion in the Ldlr-deficient model worsened plaques and increased total cholesterol and triglycerides, so that comparison is lipid-confounded. Lyz2-Cre myeloid deletion and Kif13b-deficient donor-marrow transfer worsened lesion, necrotic-core, macrophage and apoptotic-cell outcomes without corresponding plasma-lipid changes in their tested mouse models. These approaches are not macrophage-exclusive.

Evidence: KF2, KF4

What do the single-cell experiments show, and how many independent libraries were there?

Public datasets and the study-generated aortic comparison implicated macrophage state changes with lower Kif13b. The generated experiment pooled five mice into one library per genotype, not five independent libraries. Its Results describe 20 weeks of Western diet while Figure 3E describes 12 weeks of high-fat diet, so this page does not resolve the duration. Figure 3 uses one GSE155513 library per 8-week and 26-week time point.

Evidence: KF3

Is MERTK involved in the efferocytosis phenotype, and was it rescued in vivo?

Kif13b-deficient BMDMs had lower MERTK protein and impaired efferocytosis. Restoring MERTK in deficient BMDMs improved efferocytosis, supporting functional involvement in cells. This was not a demonstrated in vivo MERTK-restoration therapy or human treatment.

Evidence: KF5

How certain is the proposed KIF13B–ITCH–CBL–MERTK mechanism?

Unchanged Mertk mRNA, faster MERTK protein loss, proteasome-sensitive degradation, increased MERTK ubiquitination, KIF13B–ITCH interaction and ITCH overexpression rescue support a post-translational model. The precise proposed ITCH-mediated CBL ubiquitination/degradation step was not fully demonstrated; alternative routes including CBL self-ubiquitination remain possible.

Evidence: KF6

What did NX-1607 do in cells and mice, and what safety findings were reported?

In BMDMs, NX-1607 at 50 nM increased MERTK and 50–100 nM improved efferocytosis. In mice, 5 mg/kg/day was given only for the final 16 weeks of a 20-week Western-diet protocol, reducing plaque-related outcomes without reported lipid change. ALT/AST showed no reported liver-toxicity signal, but white blood cells and lymphocytes declined. This is preclinical intervention, not reversal after 20 completed weeks of established disease.

Evidence: KF7

Has NX-1607 shown clinical benefit for atherosclerosis?

No human atherosclerosis treatment efficacy was tested. NCT05107674 is a Phase I advanced-malignancy study, not an atherosclerosis trial; its existence does not establish clinical vascular benefit.

Evidence: KF7

Does NX-1607 prove the endogenous CBL mechanism?

No. The article describes NX-1607 as a CBL inhibitor, whereas external authoritative drug-development sources identify it as a CBL-B/CBLB inhibitor. CBL and CBLB are distinct genes. The observed preclinical rescue cannot by itself unambiguously assign the drug effect to the endogenous CBL mechanism proposed in this paper.

Evidence: KF6, KF7
