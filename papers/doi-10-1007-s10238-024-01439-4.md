## Evidence Scale

**ERAS: endoplasmic-reticulum-stress gene signatures in lung adenocarcinoma**

Retrospective transcriptomic modeling defined a 10-gene ERAS signature, evaluated it across TCGA and GEO datasets, and assessed two model genes in 10 paired LUAD specimens. Treatment-response analyses were computational.

### Counting note

Datasets and specimen pairs are distinct units. No unique pooled participant total is derived. The Results text calls the external survival datasets six but enumerates seven; Methods and Figure 4C identify TCGA plus seven GEO datasets. External evaluation here means retrospective public-dataset assessment, not prospective clinical validation; cross-dataset participant overlap was not independently reconstructed.

## Research Question

Can an endoplasmic-reticulum-stress gene signature stratify overall-survival prognosis in retrospective LUAD transcriptomic cohorts?

## Author Evidence Summary

The study combined LUAD single-cell and bulk transcriptomic datasets to investigate endoplasmic reticulum stress. Twenty-two survival-associated genes were reduced to a 10-gene ERAS signature using LASSO in TCGA. The authors examined survival stratification in TCGA and seven named GEO datasets, and combined ERAS with age and stage in a nomogram. The reported nomogram AUCs were 0.740, 0.754 and 0.754 at 1, 3 and 5 years. Expression of SERPINH1 and GPR37 was assessed by qPCR in 10 paired tumor and adjacent specimens. Immune scores and drug sensitivities were inferred from molecular data; this does not establish that using ERAS to choose treatment improves outcomes.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Retrospective public-dataset prognostic modeling with tissue expression experiments
- Evidence Type: Human transcriptomic associations and limited expression validation
- Population: LUAD bulk transcriptomic cohorts, nine treatment-naive patients represented in a single-cell dataset, and paired clinical tissue specimens
- Primary Endpoint: Overall survival association and prognostic discrimination
- Secondary Endpoint: Inferred immune states, computational drug sensitivity and tissue gene expression
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: bulk transcriptomics, single-cell transcriptomics, clinical survival data, qPCR

## What This Study Adds

- A stress-related gene score developed retrospectively and assessed across public LUAD transcriptomic datasets.
- An explicit distinction between prognostic modeling, tissue-expression validation and inferred drug-response hypotheses.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Retrospective LUAD prognostic signature development and public-dataset evaluation.
- Reported nomogram discrimination within the analyzed data.
- Limited tumor-adjacent expression validation.

### Does Not Establish

- Prospective clinical utility or improved survival from ERAS-guided care.
- Observed benefit from immunotherapy or chemotherapy selected using ERAS.
- A causal tumor-promoting effect inferred from expression or survival association alone.

### Limitations

- Retrospective public data and heterogeneous molecular datasets limit clinical transportability.
- No prospective ERAS-guided intervention was evaluated; public-dataset survival separation and retrospective AUCs do not establish clinical utility.
- The Results text calls seven named GEO cohorts six. Methods and Figure 4C identify the seven named GEO datasets; no pooled participant total is inferred.
- Survival separation was not uniformly significant: Figure 4C reports a log-rank P value of 0.091 for GSE29016. The study should not be cited as significant prognostic validation in every external dataset.
- Immune-infiltration and TMB group differences described in Results include nonsignificant comparisons; computational immune and drug-sensitivity estimates are not observed treatment-response validation.
- The qPCR experiment assesses two genes in 10 paired tissue specimens; it does not validate the complete survival model or establish that these genes cause tumor progression.
- The selected figures were checked for labels, reported values and direction, without reanalysis of participant-level or raw qPCR data.
