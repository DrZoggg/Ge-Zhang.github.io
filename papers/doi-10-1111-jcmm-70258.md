## Official Abstract

Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), a form of non‐ischaemic myocardial disease, is characterised by structural and functional cardiac abnormalities. As defined by the World Health Organisation, DCM constitutes a significant cardiac pathology, leading to increased morbidity and mortality due to complications such as heart failure and arrhythmias. The diagnostic process for DCM predominantly employs echocardiography and MRI, with biomarkers like NT‐pro BNP and troponin providing supportive, yet non‐specific, evidence. Exosomes, small extracellular vesicles, play a critical role in intercellular communications by transferring biomolecules including lipids, proteins, messenger RNA (mRNA) and non‐coding RNA (ncRNA) to target cells, thereby influencing key cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, angiogenesis and immune modulation. Within the ncRNA category, circular RNAs (circRNAs) are notable for their cellular specificity and evolutionary conservation and are often implicated in the regulatory mechanisms underlying DCM and heart failure. This investigation employed next‐generation sequencing technology to analyse plasma exosomal circRNA profiles in DCM patients with chronic heart failure (CHF), compared to healthy controls. The analysis revealed distinct circRNA expression patterns, identifying 49 uniquely expressed circRNAs in the DCM cohort with CHF. These circRNAs were associated with several critical biological pathways, including the sequestration of extracellular ligands from receptors, N‐acetyltransferase activity, histone acetyltransferase activity and endocytic vesicle membrane composition. The findings of this study provide valuable insights into the pathophysiological mechanisms of DCM and offer evidence for improving clinical diagnostic methodologies.

## Research Question

What plasma exosomal circRNA expression differences and predicted regulatory relationships were reported in DCM with heart failure, and how far does the small external assay evaluation support them?

## Author Evidence Summary

The article compares DCM-HF and healthy plasma vesicle-associated RNA, with a discovery group of 3 patients and 5 controls and an external group of 6 patients and 4 controls. It reports 49 differential circRNAs, 27 upregulated and 22 downregulated, and computational enrichment and interaction analyses. A material platform-reporting difference is retained: Methods 2.7 describes the LC Human ceRNA Array V1.0 microarray, whereas the abstract and other passages call the work next-generation or ceRNA sequencing. This evidence page therefore uses expression profiling without treating the assay as definitively verified RNA sequencing. Results 3.7 and Fig. 5 describe nine externally assessed circRNAs; Methods 2.9 lists ten. Expression-profile differences in these small groups are exploratory evidence, not a prospectively validated DCM diagnostic test or proof of a causal circRNA mechanism.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Small human case-control plasma vesicle RNA profiling with an external qRT-PCR comparison
- Evidence Type: Exploratory human expression association, predicted regulatory networks and external assay evaluation
- Population: DCM with chronic heart failure and healthy controls
- Primary Endpoint: Between-group plasma exosomal circRNA expression differences
- Secondary Endpoint: Predicted targets/pathways and external hub-circRNA expression-profile assessment
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: plasma vesicle characterization, ceRNA microarray as described in Methods 2.7; sequencing terminology elsewhere is inconsistent, external qRT-PCR, target and PPI network prediction, enrichment and PCoA

## What This Study Adds

- A DCM-HF plasma vesicle-associated circRNA expression comparison complementary to miRNA profiling.
- An article-reported 49-feature differential circRNA set and computational target/pathway hypotheses.
- Small external qRT-PCR follow-up presented together with the relevant platform, count and naming limitations.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- Exploratory circRNA expression differences in the analyzed DCM-HF and healthy samples.
- A reported final set of 49 differential circRNAs.
- Computational target, PPI and pathway prioritization.
- An external qRT-PCR comparison of the panel described in Results and Fig. 5.

### Does Not Establish

- A definitively verified RNA-sequencing platform across all source descriptions.
- A fully reconciled network hub count or a ten-circRNA validation result.
- Direct biochemical circRNA-miRNA-target binding or a causal DCM mechanism.
- Prospective diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, AUC or clinical utility.
- Cardiac tissue origin of every circulating vesicle-associated circRNA.
- Clinical treatment efficacy from the identified circRNA candidates.

### Limitations

- The discovery (3 versus 5) and external (6 versus 4) groups are small.
- Methods 2.7 describes a ceRNA microarray, while the abstract and other passages describe sequencing; neither description is silently erased.
- The Results/Fig. 5 nine-circRNA panel differs from the ten candidates listed in Methods 2.9.
- Results 3.6, Fig. 4 and Table 2 contain differing node/hub descriptions; no corrected hub total is invented.
- The source uses the name hsa-circ-76,771 in some passages and hsa-circ-0076771 in Table 1. These are not silently assigned an authoritative database identity.
- Expression differences and predicted networks do not establish prospective diagnostic utility, tissue of origin or a causal mechanism.

## Q&A

### Was this DCM circRNA study performed by sequencing or microarray?

Methods 2.7 explicitly describes an LC Human ceRNA Array V1.0 microarray, but the abstract and other passages use sequencing terminology. The discrepancy is unresolved; the evidence summary calls it expression profiling.

Evidence: KF1

### How large were the human discovery and assay groups?

The discovery comparison is 3 DCM patients versus 5 controls; the external comparison is 6 versus 4. The phases are not converted into an independently deduplicated total.

Evidence: KF1, KF6

### How many differential circRNAs were reported?

The final Results/Fig. 2 set contains 49 circRNAs, 27 upregulated and 22 downregulated, among 865 detected circRNAs.

Evidence: KF3

### What do the functional analyses establish?

They provide predicted target and pathway relationships. They do not demonstrate direct binding, target regulation or causal function in human DCM.

Evidence: KF4, KF5

### Were nine or ten circRNAs evaluated externally?

Results 3.7 and Fig. 5 describe nine, while Methods 2.9 lists ten. The website preserves the Results count with the discrepancy stated explicitly.

Evidence: KF6

### Does external expression-profile separation make this a validated diagnostic test?

No. The small external assay group and expression-distance/PCoA separation do not establish prospective diagnostic performance or clinical utility.

Evidence: KF6
