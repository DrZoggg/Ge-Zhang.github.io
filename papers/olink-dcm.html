Official Abstract

In this study, we utilized the Olink Cardiovascular III panel to compare the expression levels of 92 cardiovascular-related proteins between patients with dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF) (n = 20) and healthy normal people (Normal) (n = 18). The top five most significant proteins, including SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur, were selected by Olink proteomics. These proteins were further validated using ELISA in plasma samples collected from an additional cohort. ELISA validation confirmed significant increases in SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur in DCM-HF patients compared to healthy controls. GO and KEGG analysis indicated that NT-pro BNP, SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, Plaur, BLM hydrolase, CSTB, Gal-4, CCL15, CDH5, SR-PSOX, and CCL2 were associated with DCM-HF. Correlation analysis revealed that these 13 differentially expressed proteins have strong correlations with clinical indicators such as LVEF and NT-pro BNP, etc. Additionally, in the GEO–DCM data sets, the combined diagnostic value of these five core proteins AUC values of 0.959, 0.773, and 0.803, respectively indicating the predictive value of the five core proteins for DCM-HF. Our findings suggest that these proteins may be useful biomarkers for the diagnosis and prediction of DCM-HF, and further research is prompted to explore their potential as therapeutic targets.

Counting note

The article reports 103 local participants across two separate cohorts: 38 participants in the Olink discovery cohort and 65 participants in the ELISA validation cohort. These local counts can be combined because the article explicitly reports 50 DCM-HF patients and 53 healthy controls overall. The three external GEO datasets are different evidence units: article-reported analyzed sample counts differ from native or independently documented dataset composition for several accessions. External dataset counts are therefore preserved as reported in provenance and are not aggregated into a derived external total n.

Research Question

Can targeted plasma proteomics identify DCM-HF-associated proteins that replicate in an independent plasma ELISA cohort and retain diagnostic discrimination as corresponding gene-expression markers across external human cardiac transcriptomic datasets?

Author Evidence Summary

This study used targeted plasma proteomics to identify DCM-HF-associated protein signals and then evaluated their reproducibility across an independent plasma assay and external cardiac transcriptomic datasets. Among 103 locally recruited participants, 38 individuals (20 DCM-HF and 18 healthy controls) formed the Olink discovery cohort, in which 92 cardiovascular-related proteins were profiled. Thirty-three proteins differed at p<0.05, and 13 showed marked higher abundance in DCM-HF, remained significant after FDR correction, and were all upregulated in the discovery analysis. Five candidates—SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1 and PLAUR—were prioritized and subsequently measured by ELISA in an independent 65-participant plasma cohort (30 DCM-HF and 35 controls), where all five were higher in DCM-HF (P<0.001). The corresponding five-gene expression panel also showed diagnostic discrimination across three external human cardiac transcriptomic datasets, with combined AUCs of 0.959, 0.773 and 0.803. These findings support a cross-assay biomarker-discovery framework for DCM-HF, but they do not establish prospective disease prediction, clinically validated diagnostic utility, causal mechanisms, or treatment benefit.

What This Study Adds

  • Establishes a targeted plasma-proteomics discovery layer for DCM-HF by systematically evaluating 92 cardiovascular-related proteins rather than focusing on a single candidate biomarker.
  • Adds independent local plasma replication using ELISA in a separate participant cohort and a different assay platform.
  • Connects circulating-protein findings to corresponding gene-expression signals across three external human cardiac transcriptomic datasets while preserving the distinction between protein-level and transcript-level evidence.
  • Places the biomarker signals into clinical and biological context through correlation, functional-enrichment and protein-interaction analyses, generating hypotheses for larger validation and mechanistic studies.

Evidence Scope

Supports

  • A predefined 92-protein cardiovascular Olink panel detected differential plasma protein abundance between the studied DCM-HF and healthy-control groups.
  • Thirteen proteins showed marked higher abundance in the Olink discovery analysis and remained significant after FDR correction.
  • SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1 and PLAUR showed directionally consistent higher plasma levels in an independent local ELISA cohort.
  • The corresponding five-gene expression panel showed diagnostic discrimination across three external human cardiac transcriptomic datasets.
  • Several DCM-HF-associated plasma proteins were correlated with clinical indicators including NT-proBNP, LVEF, CRP and eGFR in the studied cohort.
  • Functional-enrichment and protein-interaction analyses provide biological hypotheses involving inflammatory, remodeling and related processes.
  • The five prioritized markers constitute candidates for larger biomarker-validation studies.

Does Not Establish

  • That the Olink analysis provides an unbiased whole-proteome survey; it used a predefined targeted cardiovascular panel.
  • That the 13 differentially abundant proteins cause DCM-HF or directly determine disease severity.
  • That SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1 or PLAUR are clinically validated diagnostic tests for DCM-HF.
  • That external cardiac transcriptomic evaluation constitutes independent plasma-proteomic replication.
  • That ROC discrimination in existing case-control/transcriptomic datasets predicts who will develop DCM-HF prospectively.
  • That the five-marker panel has demonstrated incremental clinical utility over echocardiography, cardiac MRI, NT-proBNP, troponin, or standard diagnostic assessment.
  • That correlation, GO/KEGG enrichment or PPI-network analysis establishes a causal molecular mechanism.
  • That any of the identified proteins is a validated therapeutic target or that biomarker-guided treatment improves clinical outcomes.
  • That this publication generated original mouse-model evidence; the Methods and Results do not describe an animal experiment.

Limitations

  • The Olink discovery cohort was small, with 20 DCM-HF patients and 18 healthy controls.
  • The Olink assay was a predefined 92-protein cardiovascular panel rather than an unbiased whole-proteome measurement.
  • The local discovery and ELISA analyses were cross-sectional and do not establish prospective incident-disease prediction or longitudinal prognostic performance.
  • The external GEO evaluation used cardiac transcriptomic data rather than independent plasma-proteomic cohorts, so it supports corresponding gene-expression discrimination rather than direct protein-level replication.
  • Article-reported sample counts for the external GEO datasets differ from native or independently documented dataset composition, and the exact filtering logic is not fully specified.
  • Clinical correlations were exploratory and were not independently replicated for each protein-clinical-variable relationship.
  • GO/KEGG, STRING, Cytoscape and related analyses are computational and do not establish causal mechanisms or therapeutic efficacy.
  • The article reports ChiCTR2100051469, but publicly indexed registry information for that identifier appears to correspond to a different study; the linkage to this DCM-HF proteomics study is therefore unresolved.
  • Although the Introduction refers to human plasma and mouse models, the study Methods and Results do not describe an original animal experiment; mouse evidence discussed in the article derives from cited prior studies.

Q&A

How many participants were included in the DCM-HF Olink proteomics study?

The article reports 103 local participants: 50 DCM-HF patients and 53 healthy controls. Thirty-eight participants formed the Olink discovery cohort, and a separate 65 participants formed the ELISA validation cohort.

Evidence: KF1, KF3

How many proteins were profiled with Olink in DCM-HF?

The Olink Cardiovascular III panel measured 92 targeted cardiovascular-related plasma proteins in 20 DCM-HF patients and 18 healthy controls.

Evidence: KF1

Which plasma proteins were prioritized for validation?

SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1 and PLAUR were prioritized from the differential-protein analysis for focused validation.

Evidence: KF2

Were the five Olink biomarkers replicated with another plasma assay?

Yes. In an independent cohort of 30 DCM-HF patients and 35 healthy controls, ELISA showed higher plasma levels of SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1 and PLAUR, with P<0.001 for the reported group comparisons.

Evidence: KF3

Were the five plasma proteins independently validated in external proteomic cohorts?

No. The external GEO evidence came from human cardiac gene-expression datasets. It supports the corresponding five-gene transcriptomic signal rather than independent plasma-protein replication.

Evidence: KF4

What diagnostic discrimination did the five-marker gene panel show in external datasets?

The combined five-gene AUCs were 0.959 in GSE116250, 0.773 in GSE141910 and 0.803 in GSE165303.

Evidence: KF4

Does this study show that these proteins predict future DCM-HF?

No. The study evaluated cross-sectional or case-control discrimination and external transcriptomic classification. It did not test incident DCM-HF prediction in a prospective longitudinal cohort.

Evidence: KF4

Are SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1 or PLAUR validated therapeutic targets for DCM-HF?

No. The study used association, enrichment and network analyses and did not experimentally test target inhibition, target activation, or biomarker-guided treatment. Therapeutic implications remain hypothesis-generating.

Evidence: KF5, KF6

Source Note External Counts
The article reports GSE116250 as 15 controls and 38 DCM, GSE141910 as 162 controls and 162 DCM, and GSE165303 as 48 controls and 74 DCM. Native or independently documented dataset composition differs: GSE116250 contains 14 non-failing, 37 DCM and 13 ischemic-cardiomyopathy samples; GSE141910 is documented as a 366-sample left-ventricular resource including 166 non-failing and 166 DCM samples plus HCM and PPCM; GSE165303 is a multi-assay heart-tissue series whose RNA-seq subset is independently documented as 51 non-failing and 50 DCM hearts. The exact filtering logic used to obtain the article-reported analyzed counts is not fully specified, so this evidence page preserves the discrepancy and does not aggregate an external total n.
Source Note Registration
The article reports ChiCTR2100051469. Publicly indexed information for that identifier describes a magnetocardiography/myocardial-ischemia diagnostic study rather than this DCM-HF proteomics study, so the registration linkage is treated as unresolved and should not be represented as a verified ClinicalTrial entity or trial sameAs link.
Source Note Mouse Models
