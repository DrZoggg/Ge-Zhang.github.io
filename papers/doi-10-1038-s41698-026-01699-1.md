## Official Abstract

Circadian rhythm disorders represent an abstract concept lacking standardized quantitative metrics. Existing circadian indicators, including traditional rhythm parameters and a limited set of clock gene or physiological biomarkers, are insufficient to robustly capture steady-state endogenous circadian homeostasis in complex disease contexts, thereby constraining quantitative assessment of circadian disruption and limiting its translational applicability. Chronic circadian rhythm disruption is associated with various diseases, including metabolic disorders and malignancies. However, the mechanisms by which circadian disruption influences tumor microenvironment formation and colorectal cancer progression remain incompletely understood. This study employs systems biology analysis to decipher the molecular characteristics of circadian rhythm disruption in colorectal cancer progression. We analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing data from 13 CRC tissue samples and 12 normal mucosal samples, combined with 3733 samples from 34 public batch RNA, microarray, and single-cell RNA sequencing cohorts. We developed and validated the ClockProCRC system, which detects and quantifies intrinsic circadian misalignment in CRC. The ClockProCRC score elucidates how circadian misalignment drives CRC progression trajectories, shapes clinical phenotypes, regulates disease manifestations, and reshapes the tumor microenvironment. SYNE1 gene was identified as a key mediator of circadian misalignment, promoting tumorigenesis by driving epithelial-like phenotypic conversion and demonstrating therapeutic potential in colorectal cancer management. This study establishes a foundation for integrating rhythmic information into clinical practice and advances circadian biology research in the field of CRC.

### Counting note

The article reports heterogeneous evidence units. The 25 tissue samples in the primary GSE166555 single-cell cohort yielded 94,683 post-QC cells and must not be conflated with participant counts. The Methods reports an aggregate 3,758 samples across 35 public datasets, while the Abstract describes the primary 25-sample single-cell cohort plus 3,733 samples from 34 additional cohorts. ## Research Question

Can a transcriptomic scoring framework derived from colon circadian genes and colorectal-cancer single-cell co-expression structure quantify endogenous circadian misalignment across heterogeneous datasets, capture CRC progression and tumor-microenvironment states, and identify experimentally testable regulators such as SYNE1?

## Author Evidence Summary

This study developed ClockProCRC as a transcriptomic framework for quantifying endogenous circadian misalignment in colorectal cancer (CRC). The article reports 3,758 samples across 35 public GEO datasets overall. Its primary GSE166555 single-cell cohort comprised 13 CRC tissue samples and 12 normal mucosal samples, yielding 94,683 cells after quality control. A 906-gene colon circadian reference was projected into the CRC single-cell data, followed by KNN meta-cell aggregation and high-dimensional co-expression analysis. Nine circadian modules were identified; four CRC-associated modules (M1, M3, M4 and M6) contributed a 559-gene circadian transcriptional-driver signature. ClockProCRC used expression-matched random-background correction with 100 expression bins and 1,000 random samplings and was evaluated across multiple human, mouse and non-human-primate circadian perturbation datasets. Higher ClockProCRC values were associated with CRC progression, worse survival in the analyzed retrospective cohort, and distinct single-cell microenvironmental states. Gene-regulatory-network analysis prioritized the NFATC2-SYNE1 axis, and SYNE1 silencing in HCT116 and SW480 cells reduced migration, invasion and colony formation while shifting epithelial/mesenchymal marker expression. CLOCK and SYNE1 perturbation experiments further linked ClockProCRC-defined circadian disruption to SYNE1 rhythmicity, with partial molecular restoration after melatonin treatment. The study supports a systems-level circadian framework and an experimentally testable SYNE1 hypothesis, but it does not establish prospective clinical utility, treatment efficacy, or in-vivo therapeutic validation.

## Study Design & Analytical Framework

### Study profile

- Study Design: Multicohort systems-biology study integrating public bulk transcriptomics, microarrays, single-cell RNA sequencing, cross-species circadian perturbation datasets, gene-regulatory-network analysis, and in-vitro CRC-cell perturbation experiments
- Evidence Type: Public transcriptomic and single-cell cohort analysis, expression-matched circadian scoring, cross-species rhythmicity evaluation, survival and clinical-association analysis, single-cell cell-state and ligand-receptor analysis, gene-regulatory-network analysis, and in-vitro functional perturbation
- Population: Public human colorectal-cancer and normal-mucosal datasets, public human/mouse/non-human-primate circadian time-series and perturbation datasets, and HCT116/SW480 colorectal-cancer cell models
- Primary Endpoint: Development and multicohort evaluation of ClockProCRC as a transcriptomic measure of endogenous circadian misalignment
- Secondary Endpoint: Association of ClockProCRC with CRC progression, survival and tumor-microenvironment states, plus prioritization and functional testing of SYNE1 as a circadian-associated CRC regulator
- External dataset evaluation: Yes
- Data modalities: single-cell RNA sequencing, bulk RNA sequencing, microarray expression profiling, cross-species circadian time-series transcriptomics, gene-regulatory-network analysis, CRC-cell perturbation RNA sequencing, qRT-PCR, Transwell and wound-healing assays, colony-formation assays, Western blotting

## What This Study Adds

- Provides a systems-level transcriptomic framework for quantifying circadian misalignment in CRC rather than relying on a single clock gene or a small biomarker panel.
- Connects a colon circadian reference, CRC single-cell co-expression architecture, cross-species circadian perturbation datasets, retrospective clinical associations and tumor-microenvironment analysis within one evidence framework.
- Links computational gene-regulatory-network prioritization to functional CRC-cell perturbation by testing SYNE1 in two independent colorectal-cancer cell lines.
- Creates a mechanistically testable bridge among CLOCK perturbation, ClockProCRC-defined circadian dysregulation, SYNE1 rhythmicity and CRC-cell phenotypes, while leaving prospective clinical utility and in-vivo therapeutic efficacy unresolved.

## Evidence Scope

### Supports

- The article defines a 906-gene colon circadian reference and derives CRC-associated circadian co-expression structure from a 94,683-cell single-cell dataset.
- ClockProCRC uses expression-matched random-background correction and varies in expected directions across multiple experimentally or behaviorally perturbed circadian datasets.
- Higher ClockProCRC values are associated with CRC progression-related states and poorer overall survival in the analyzed retrospective/public cohorts.
- ClockProCRC-defined states are associated with distinct CRC single-cell composition and intercellular-communication patterns.
- SYNE1 is prioritized by gene-regulatory-network analysis and shows circadian perturbation-associated expression/rhythmicity changes across independent datasets.
- SYNE1 knockdown in HCT116 and SW480 cells reduces migration, invasion and colony formation and shifts E-cadherin, ZO-1 and N-cadherin expression.
- CLOCK knockdown and melatonin rescue conditions alter ClockProCRC and SYNE1 rhythmicity in CRC-cell experiments.

### Does Not Establish

- That ClockProCRC has been prospectively validated in a multicenter clinical cohort.
- That ClockProCRC is a clinically approved diagnostic, prognostic, screening or treatment-selection test.
- That the cohort-specific median or single-cell 75th-percentile grouping rules define a universal clinical cutoff.
- That higher ClockProCRC causally drives CRC progression in humans; most clinical and tumor-microenvironment evidence is observational or retrospective.
- That ClockProCRC adds incremental clinical utility over established CRC staging, molecular markers or treatment-response assessment.
- That SYNE1 is a validated therapeutic target in patients with CRC.
- That SYNE1 inhibition improves tumor control, metastasis or survival in vivo; the study reports cell-line perturbation but no SYNE1-directed animal efficacy experiment.
- That melatonin treatment is effective for CRC patients; melatonin was used as a molecular rescue condition in cell experiments.
- That AUC=0.915 represents CRC diagnosis or patient-level prediction; it refers to discrimination of CLOCK-knockdown experimental conditions by SYNE1 expression.
- That single-cell ligand-receptor and pathway analyses prove causal tumor-microenvironment remodeling.

### Limitations

- Most human clinical evidence derives from retrospective/public transcriptomic datasets rather than a prospectively enrolled multicenter clinical validation cohort.
- The study integrates heterogeneous evidence units across human, mouse and non-human-primate datasets; sample, tissue, cohort and cell counts are not interchangeable.
- ClockProCRC grouping uses data-context-specific thresholds (75th percentile for single-cell analyses and commonly the cohort median for bulk cohorts), not a universal clinical cutoff.
- The manuscript distinguishes the 906-gene colon circadian reference from the 559-gene four-module CTD signature. The ClockProCRC scoring Methods refers to circadian rhythm disorder genes (CRGs), so this evidence page does not infer that the 559 CTD genes are identical to the final score-input gene set.
- The Results text contains the accession GSE200977, whereas the Methods and Figure 4 legend identify the CRC single-cell cohort as GSE200997; the evidence page uses GSE200997 and records the Results-text accession as a manuscript typo.
- The manuscript contains several wording/locator inconsistencies, including knockdown described once as knockout, Transwell language that conflates proliferation with migration/invasion, and a scratch-assay timing inconsistency; the evidence page follows the experimental Methods and figure legends.
- The public ClockProCRC GitHub repository is an associated code resource but does not exactly mirror the accepted manuscript: the README reports an earlier 32-cohort/3,075-sample configuration, the current scoring script defaults to 50 bins rather than the manuscript's 100 bins, and DiffCircadian.R contains a placeholder scoring function.
- SYNE1 functional evidence is in vitro; no SYNE1-directed animal efficacy experiment or human therapeutic study is reported.
- The publisher currently labels the article as an early peer-reviewed accepted version that may undergo further editing before the final Version of Record.

## Q&A

### What is ClockProCRC and how was it constructed?

ClockProCRC is a transcriptomic circadian-misalignment scoring framework. The study began with a 906-gene colon circadian reference and separately used CRC single-cell co-expression analysis to derive nine modules and a 559-gene four-module CTD signature. The ClockProCRC scoring Methods then applied expression-matched random-background correction to circadian rhythm disorder genes using 100 expression bins and 1,000 random samplings. The reviewed manuscript text does not establish that the 559 CTD genes are identical to the final scoring gene set.

Evidence: KF1, KF2

### How large was the ClockProCRC study?

The Methods reports 3,758 samples across 35 public GEO datasets. The primary GSE166555 single-cell cohort comprised 25 tissue samples (13 CRC and 12 normal mucosa) and retained 94,683 cells after quality control. These are heterogeneous sample, tissue and cell counts and should not be interpreted as 3,758 unique human participants.

Evidence: KF1

### Was ClockProCRC associated with colorectal-cancer progression and survival?

Yes, within the analyzed retrospective/public datasets. ClockProCRC increased across progression-related states from normal mucosa through adenoma/dysplasia and CRC to colorectal liver metastasis, and higher scores were associated with worse overall survival in GSE17537 (n=55; log-rank P<0.01). This is association evidence rather than prospective clinical validation.

Evidence: KF3

### What did the single-cell analysis show about circadian misalignment and the CRC tumor microenvironment?

In GSE200997 and GSE196964, ClockProCRC-defined higher-disruption states were associated with enrichment of epithelial and plasma-cell populations, relative depletion of fibroblast/endothelial populations, and altered intercellular communication including stronger pro-inflammatory signaling patterns. These analyses identify state associations and do not by themselves prove causality.

Evidence: KF4

### Why was SYNE1 prioritized in this study?

SYNE1 emerged from the CTD gene-regulatory-network analysis. Across 100 genetic-algorithm iterations, 61 recurrent regulatory axes were identified, and the NFATC2→SYNE1 relationship showed higher modeled regulatory strength in CRC (0.607; 95% CI 0.412-0.802) than in normal mucosa (-0.102; 95% CI -0.058-0.262). SYNE1 also showed circadian perturbation-associated expression/rhythmicity changes in independent datasets.

Evidence: KF5

### What happened when SYNE1 was knocked down in colorectal-cancer cells?

In HCT116 and SW480 cells, siRNA-mediated SYNE1 knockdown reduced Transwell migration/invasion, reduced wound-healing migration and colony formation, increased E-cadherin and ZO-1, and decreased N-cadherin. These findings support an in-vitro functional role but do not establish in-vivo therapeutic efficacy.

Evidence: KF6

### How did CLOCK knockdown, melatonin and the reported SYNE1 AUC relate to the circadian perturbation experiment?

CLOCK knockdown increased ClockProCRC and disrupted SYNE1 rhythmicity; melatonin partially restored the ClockProCRC-defined state and SYNE1 rhythmicity in CRC-cell experiments. The reported SYNE1 AUC of 0.915 refers specifically to discrimination of CLOCK-knockdown experimental conditions, not to CRC diagnosis or patient-level prediction.

Evidence: KF6

### Are ClockProCRC or SYNE1 clinically validated for diagnosis, prognosis or treatment selection?

No. ClockProCRC has multicohort analytical and retrospective association evidence but lacks large prospective multicenter clinical validation and a universal clinical cutoff. SYNE1 has computational and in-vitro functional evidence but no SYNE1-directed animal efficacy or patient-treatment validation.

Evidence: KF2, KF3, KF5, KF6
