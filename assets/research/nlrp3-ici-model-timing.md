## What the experiments contribute

In the single-ICI mouse model, MCC950 mitigated cardiac injury; the separate tumor experiment reported reduced tumor burden with anti-PD-1 plus MCC950 under the tested B16F10 conditions. The dual-ICI experiment reported mitigated cardiac phenotypes and reduced tumor burden. These tumor-bearing settings support research questions that examine both outcomes during coadministration.

The established-injury experiment addresses a different question: MCC950 after anti-PD-1 cardiac injury ameliorated the reported echocardiographic impairment. Because melanoma inoculation was omitted, its cardiac findings provide a precedent for delayed cardiac intervention. Antitumor efficacy was not evaluated in this no-tumor established-injury experiment.

## Match the design to the endpoint

Figure 2 lists three cardiac groups: IgG plus vehicle, anti-PD-1 plus vehicle, and anti-PD-1 plus MCC950. Figure 3's tumor comparison also includes IgG plus MCC950. Keep that additional arm attached to its actual tumor endpoints. Figure 6 lists the control, dual-ICI plus vehicle, and dual-ICI plus MCC950 groups.

Figures 2 and 6 describe tumor inoculation on day 0, indicated treatment every other day on days 7-19, and heart collection on day 20. The tumor captions identify volume at day 19 and weight at day 20. Results s3-1 separately refers to six anti-PD-1 injections; this comparison retains the figure schedules without deriving a total injection count from that unresolved wording.

For established injury, Supplementary Figure 7A shows IgG or anti-PD-1 on days 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9. On days 11, 13, 15, 17 and 19, the arms were IgG plus vehicle, anti-PD-1 plus vehicle, and anti-PD-1 plus MCC950. Anti-PD-1 continued during MCC950 treatment. Hearts were collected on day 20. Supplementary Figure 7B reports six echocardiographic measures for 9, 8 and 7 mice, respectively. Neither Figure S7 nor the cited Results section specifies a per-mouse cardiac-function threshold or a separate echocardiography acquisition date.
