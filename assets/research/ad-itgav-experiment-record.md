# From an AD expression observation to a candidate experiment

An editable research-planning record, with ITGAV as a worked example. Use it to separate the published observation, the question to test and the result that would change your interpretation. ITGAV is an illustration, not a ranking of candidates.

## Start with what was measured

The source study reports higher ITGAV expression in PC12 cells after an Aβ1–42 challenge than in controls. Its reported challenge context was 7 μM for 12 hours. This is an expression comparison; candidate-specific perturbation, functional rescue and an effective intervention were not established by that comparison.

**Original study:** [Integrated multi-omics analysis revealed the molecular networks and potential targets of cellular senescence in Alzheimer’s disease](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddae189). Source locators: publisher PDF p. 9, cell culture and qPCR methods; Figure 4E–G; Supplementary Tables 7–8 and Figures 5–6. The dose and timing describe the reported experiment, rather than a complete or optimised protocol for the proposed work below.

## Define the next question

**Worked question:** In the chosen cell model, does a verified ITGAV perturbation change a prespecified challenge-associated cellular endpoint?

The design below is proposed follow-up work. Expression direction alone does not determine whether increasing or decreasing the candidate will improve a functional outcome.

## Decide what the result supports

| Observed result | Interpretation for this proposed test |
| --- | --- |
| The intended candidate change is not confirmed | The test does not resolve the candidate's functional role; investigate the perturbation and measurement first. |
| Candidate change is confirmed, but the functional estimate is imprecise | The functional question remains unresolved. A nonsignificant result alone is not evidence that the candidate has no role. |
| Candidate change is confirmed and sufficiently precise results exclude the prespecified meaningful functional change | This weighs against that specific effect in the tested model and conditions; it does not exclude every role of the gene. |
| Candidate change and the prespecified functional effect occur together | This supports further model-specific investigation. Matched controls and, where interpretable, an independent perturbation or rescue help assess specificity. |
